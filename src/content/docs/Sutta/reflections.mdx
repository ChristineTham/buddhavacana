<TabItem label="My Translation">
> 751\. “Bhikkhave, this is the direct path
>
> * for the purification of beings,
> * for the overcoming of sorrow and lamentation,
> * for the disappearance of pain and displeasure,
> * for the attainment of the right method,
> * for the realisation of `nibbāna` (extinguishment)
>
> — namely, `cattāro satipaṭṭhānā` (the four reflections). What four? Here, Bhikkhave:
>
> * a bhikkhu `kāye kāyānupassī viharati` (stays aware, observing the body [or, physical objects] in the body), ardent, clearly comprehending, with awareness, having removed covetousness and displeasure in the world;
> * he stays aware, observing `vedanā` (feelings) in feelings, ardent, clearly comprehending, with awareness, having removed covetousness and displeasure in the world;
> * he stays aware, observing `citta` (the mind) in the mind, ardent, clearly comprehending, with awareness, having removed covetousness and displeasure in the world;
> * he stays aware, observing `dhamma` (perceptual objects) in dhamma, ardent, clearly comprehending, with awareness, having removed covetousness and displeasure in the world.
>
> Bhikkhave, this is the direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow and lamentation, for the disappearance of pain and displeasure, for the attainment of the right method, for the realisation of extinguishment — namely, the four reflections.”
</TabItem>

751\. “Ekāyanvāyaṃ, bhikkhave, maggo sattānaṃ visuddhiyā sokaparidevānaṃ samatikkamāya dukkhadomanassānaṃ atthaṅgamāya ñāyassa adhigamāya nibbānassa sacchikiriyāya, yadidaṃ—  cattāro satipaṭṭhānā. Ekāyanvāyaṃ, bhikkhave, maggo sattānaṃ visuddhiyā sokaparidevānaṃ samatikkamāya dukkhadomanassānaṃ atthaṅgamāya ñāyassa adhigamāya nibbānassa sacchikiriyāya, yadidaṃ—  cattāro satipaṭṭhānā”ti.
