This is a proto-Abhidhamma sutta that provides lists of related (and opposing) terms grouped by ascending numerical order (by size of list). It represents a largely Theravādin view of the core elements of the Buddha's teaching. The corresponding version in the Dharmaguptaka Dīrghāgama — DA11 (Discourse Increasing by One) — is much shorter and better structured. The intention of the list is for communal recitation across the fourfold assembly of monastics and lay disciples.

Although some of the lists are probably derived from the Buddha's teachings, it seems some lists have been inserted at a later stage by disciples. Given these lists are presented outside the context of the Buddha's teachings, it is sometimes hard to understand the purpose and meaning for the items.

[8D/10 Saṅgītisutta](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/8D/10.html)

773\. Thus heard by me —

At one time the Bhagavā, while travelling through the Malla country together with a large Saṅgha of bhikkhū, about five hundred bhikkhū, arrived at Pāvā, a town of the Mallas. There, indeed, the Bhagavā was dwelling at Pāvā, in the mango grove of Cunda the smith's son.
</TabItem>

## The new Ubbhataka assembly hall

<Tabs syncKey="paliquote">
<TabItem label="My Translation">
774\. Now at that time, a new assembly hall called ‘Ubbhataka’ had been recently built by the Mallas of Pāvā, and it had not been occupied by any wanderer, brahmin, or any human being. The Mallas of Pāvā heard, “The Bhagavā, it is said, while travelling through the Malla country together with a large Saṅgha of bhikkhū, about five hundred bhikkhū, has arrived at Pāvā, and is dwelling at Pāvā in the mango grove of Cunda the smith's son.” Then the Mallas of Pāvā approached the Bhagavā; having approached, they bowed to the Bhagavā and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the Mallas of Pāvā said this to the Bhagavā, “Here, Bhante, a new assembly hall called ‘Ubbhataka’ has been recently built by the Mallas of Pāvā, and it has not been occupied by any wanderer, brahmin, or any human being. And may the Bhagavā, Bhante, indeed be the first to use it; after it has been used by the Bhagavā first, the Mallas of Pāvā will use it. That would be for the Mallas of Pāvā’s long-term welfare and happiness.” The Bhagavā consented by silence.

775\. Then the Mallas of Pāvā, knowing the Bhagavā's consent, rose from their seats, bowed to the Bhagavā, circumambulated him keeping their right side towards him, and approached the assembly hall. Having approached, they spread the assembly hall with a complete floor covering, prepared seats for the Bhagavā, set up a water pot, lit an oil lamp, and then approached the Bhagavā. Having approached, they bowed to the Bhagavā and stood to one side. Standing to one side, those Mallas of Pāvā said this to the Bhagavā, “Bhante, the assembly hall is completely spread with floor coverings, seats are prepared for the Bhagavā, a water pot is set up, and an oil lamp is lit. May the Bhagavā, Bhante, do now as he sees fit.”

776\. Then the Bhagavā, having dressed and taking his bowl and robes, approached the assembly hall together with the Saṅgha of bhikkhū. Having approached, he washed his feet, entered the assembly hall, and sat facing east, leaning against the central pillar. The Saṅgha of bhikkhū also washed their feet, entered the assembly hall, and sat facing east, leaning against the western wall, keeping the Bhagavā in front. The Mallas of Pāvā also washed their feet, entered the assembly hall, and sat facing west, leaning against the eastern wall, keeping the Bhagavā in front. Then the Bhagavā, for much of the night, instructed, encouraged, inspired, and gladdened the Mallas of Pāvā with a Dhamma talk, and then dismissed them, saying, “The night is indeed far advanced, Vāseṭṭhas. Do now as you see fit.” “Yes, Bhante,” the Mallas of Pāvā replied to the Bhagavā, and rising from their seats, they bowed to the Bhagavā, circumambulated him keeping their right side towards him, and departed.

777\. Then, soon after the Mallas of Pāvā had departed, the Bhagavā looked over the utterly silent Saṅgha of bhikkhū and addressed āyasmā Sāriputta, “The Saṅgha of bhikkhū, Sāriputta, is indeed free from sloth and torpor. May a Dhamma talk for the bhikkhū occur to you, Sāriputta. My back aches. I shall stretch it.” “Yes, Bhante,” āyasmā Sāriputta replied to the Bhagavā.

778\. Then the Bhagavā, having laid out his four-folded outer robe, lay down on his right side in the lion's posture, placing one foot on the other, aware and clearly comprehending, having fixed his mind on the apperception of rising.

774\. Tena kho pana samayena pāveyyakānaṃ mallānaṃ ubbhatakaṃ nāma navaṃ sandhāgāraṃ acirakāritaṃ hoti anajjhāvuṭṭhaṃ samaṇena vā brāhmaṇena vā kenaci vā manussabhūtena. Assosuṃ kho pāveyyakā mallā—  “bhagavā kira mallesu cārikaṃ caramāno mahatā bhikkhusaṃghena saddhiṃ pañcamattehi bhikkhusatehi pāvaṃ anuppatto pāvāyaṃ viharati cundassa kammāraputtassa ambavane”ti. Atha kho pāveyyakā mallā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinnā kho pāveyyakā mallā bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ—  “idha, bhante, pāveyyakānaṃ mallānaṃ ubbhatakaṃ nāma navaṃ sandhāgāraṃ acirakāritaṃ hoti anajjhāvuṭṭhaṃ samaṇena vā brāhmaṇena vā kenaci vā manussabhūtena. Tañca kho, bhante, bhagavā paṭhamaṃ paribhuñjatu, bhagavatā paṭhamaṃ paribhuttaṃ pacchā pāveyyakā mallā paribhuñjissanti. Tadassa pāveyyakānaṃ mallānaṃ dīgharattaṃ hitāya sukhāyā”ti. Adhivāsesi kho bhagavā tuṇhībhāvena.

775\. Atha kho pāveyyakā mallā bhagavato adhivāsanaṃ viditvā uṭṭhāyāsanā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā yena sandhāgāraṃ tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā sabbasanthariṃ sandhāgāraṃ santharitvā bhagavato āsanāni paññāpetvā udakamaṇikaṃ patiṭṭhapetvā telapadīpaṃ āropetvā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ aṭṭhaṃsu. Ekamantaṃ ṭhitā kho te pāveyyakā mallā bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ—  “sabbasantharisanthataṃ, bhante, sandhāgāraṃ, bhagavato āsanāni paññattāni, udakamaṇiko patiṭṭhāpito, telapadīpo āropito. Yassadāni, bhante, bhagavā kālaṃ maññatī”ti.

776\. Atha kho bhagavā nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya saddhiṃ bhikkhusaṃghena yena sandhāgāraṃ tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā pāde pakkhāletvā sandhāgāraṃ pavisitvā majjhimaṃ thambhaṃ nissāya puratthābhimukho nisīdi. Bhikkhusaṃghopi kho pāde pakkhāletvā sandhāgāraṃ pavisitvā pacchimaṃ bhittiṃ nissāya puratthābhimukho nisīdi bhagavantaṃyeva purakkhatvā. Pāveyyakāpi kho mallā pāde pakkhāletvā sandhāgāraṃ pavisitvā puratthimaṃ bhittiṃ nissāya pacchimābhimukhā nisīdiṃsu bhagavantaṃyeva purakkhatvā. Atha kho bhagavā pāveyyake malle bahudeva rattiṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassetvā samādapetvā samuttejetvā sampahaṃsetvā uyyojesi—  “abhikkantā kho, vāseṭṭhā, ratti. Yassadāni tumhe kālaṃ maññathā”ti. “Evaṃ, bhante”ti kho pāveyyakā mallā bhagavato paṭissutvā uṭṭhāyāsanā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā padakkhiṇaṃ katvā pakkamiṃsu.

777\. Atha kho bhagavā acirapakkantesu pāveyyakesu mallesu tuṇhībhūtaṃ tuṇhībhūtaṃ bhikkhusaṃghaṃ anuviloketvā āyasmantaṃ sāriputtaṃ āmantesi—  “vigatathinamiddho kho, sāriputta, bhikkhusaṃgho. Paṭibhātu taṃ, sāriputta, bhikkhūnaṃ dhammīkathā. Piṭṭhi me āgilāyati. Tamahaṃ āyamissāmī”ti. “Evaṃ, bhante”ti kho āyasmā sāriputto bhagavato paccassosi.

778\. Atha kho bhagavā catugguṇaṃ saṅghāṭiṃ paññāpetvā dakkhiṇena passena sīhaseyyaṃ kappesi pāde pādaṃ accādhāya, sato sampajāno uṭṭhānasaññaṃ manasi karitvā.
</TabItem>

774\. 𑀢𑁂𑀦 𑀔𑁄 𑀧𑀦 𑀲𑀫𑀬𑁂𑀦 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑀸𑀦𑀁 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑀸𑀦𑀁 𑀉𑀩𑁆𑀪𑀢𑀓𑀁 𑀦𑀸𑀫 𑀦𑀯𑀁 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀕𑀸𑀭𑀁 𑀅𑀘𑀺𑀭𑀓𑀸𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀁 𑀳𑁄𑀢𑀺 𑀅𑀦𑀚𑁆𑀛𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀁 𑀲𑀫𑀡𑁂𑀦 𑀯𑀸 𑀩𑁆𑀭𑀸𑀳𑁆𑀫𑀡𑁂𑀦 𑀯𑀸 𑀓𑁂𑀦𑀘𑀺 𑀯𑀸 𑀫𑀦𑀼𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀪𑀽𑀢𑁂𑀦. 𑀅𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄𑀲𑀼𑀁 𑀔𑁄 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑀸 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑀸—  “𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀸 𑀓𑀺𑀭 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑁂𑀲𑀼 𑀘𑀸𑀭𑀺𑀓𑀁 𑀘𑀭𑀫𑀸𑀦𑁄 𑀫𑀳𑀢𑀸 𑀪𑀺𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀼𑀲𑀁𑀖𑁂𑀦 𑀲𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀺𑀁 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀘𑀫𑀢𑁆𑀢𑁂𑀳𑀺 𑀪𑀺𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀼𑀲𑀢𑁂𑀳𑀺 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑀁 𑀅𑀦𑀼𑀧𑁆𑀧𑀢𑁆𑀢𑁄 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑀸𑀬𑀁 𑀯𑀺𑀳𑀭𑀢𑀺 𑀘𑀼𑀦𑁆𑀤𑀲𑁆𑀲 𑀓𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀸𑀭𑀧𑀼𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀲𑁆𑀲 𑀅𑀫𑁆𑀩𑀯𑀦𑁂”𑀢𑀺. 𑀅𑀣 𑀔𑁄 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑀸 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑀸 𑀬𑁂𑀦 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀸 𑀢𑁂𑀦𑀼𑀧𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀫𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀼; 𑀉𑀧𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀫𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀅𑀪𑀺𑀯𑀸𑀤𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀏𑀓𑀫𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀦𑀺𑀲𑀻𑀤𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀼. 𑀏𑀓𑀫𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀦𑀺𑀲𑀺𑀦𑁆𑀦𑀸 𑀔𑁄 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑀸 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑀸 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀏𑀢𑀤𑀯𑁄𑀘𑀼𑀁—  “𑀇𑀥, 𑀪𑀦𑁆𑀢𑁂, 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑀸𑀦𑀁 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑀸𑀦𑀁 𑀉𑀩𑁆𑀪𑀢𑀓𑀁 𑀦𑀸𑀫 𑀦𑀯𑀁 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀕𑀸𑀭𑀁 𑀅𑀘𑀺𑀭𑀓𑀸𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀁 𑀳𑁄𑀢𑀺 𑀅𑀦𑀚𑁆𑀛𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀁 𑀲𑀫𑀡𑁂𑀦 𑀯𑀸 𑀩𑁆𑀭𑀸𑀳𑁆𑀫𑀡𑁂𑀦 𑀯𑀸 𑀓𑁂𑀦𑀘𑀺 𑀯𑀸 𑀫𑀦𑀼𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀪𑀽𑀢𑁂𑀦. 𑀢𑀜𑁆𑀘 𑀔𑁄, 𑀪𑀦𑁆𑀢𑁂, 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀸 𑀧𑀞𑀫𑀁 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀪𑀼𑀜𑁆𑀚𑀢𑀼, 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀢𑀸 𑀧𑀞𑀫𑀁 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀪𑀼𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀧𑀘𑁆𑀙𑀸 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑀸 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑀸 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀪𑀼𑀜𑁆𑀚𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀺. 𑀢𑀤𑀲𑁆𑀲 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑀸𑀦𑀁 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑀸𑀦𑀁 𑀤𑀻𑀖𑀭𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀳𑀺𑀢𑀸𑀬 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀸𑀬𑀸”𑀢𑀺. 𑀅𑀥𑀺𑀯𑀸𑀲𑁂𑀲𑀺 𑀔𑁄 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀸 𑀢𑀼𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀻𑀪𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀦.

775\. 𑀅𑀣 𑀔𑁄 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑀸 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑀸 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀢𑁄 𑀅𑀥𑀺𑀯𑀸𑀲𑀦𑀁 𑀯𑀺𑀤𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀉𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀸𑀬𑀸𑀲𑀦𑀸 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀅𑀪𑀺𑀯𑀸𑀤𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀧𑀤𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀺𑀡𑀁 𑀓𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀬𑁂𑀦 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀕𑀸𑀭𑀁 𑀢𑁂𑀦𑀼𑀧𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀫𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀼; 𑀉𑀧𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀫𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀲𑀩𑁆𑀩𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀣𑀭𑀺𑀁 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀕𑀸𑀭𑀁 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀣𑀭𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀢𑁄 𑀆𑀲𑀦𑀸𑀦𑀺 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸𑀧𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀉𑀤𑀓𑀫𑀡𑀺𑀓𑀁 𑀧𑀢𑀺𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀧𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀢𑁂𑀮𑀧𑀤𑀻𑀧𑀁 𑀆𑀭𑁄𑀧𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀬𑁂𑀦 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀸 𑀢𑁂𑀦𑀼𑀧𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀫𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀼; 𑀉𑀧𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀫𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀅𑀪𑀺𑀯𑀸𑀤𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀏𑀓𑀫𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀅𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀁𑀲𑀼. 𑀏𑀓𑀫𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀞𑀺𑀢𑀸 𑀔𑁄 𑀢𑁂 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑀸 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑀸 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀏𑀢𑀤𑀯𑁄𑀘𑀼𑀁—  “𑀲𑀩𑁆𑀩𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀣𑀭𑀺𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀣𑀢𑀁, 𑀪𑀦𑁆𑀢𑁂, 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀕𑀸𑀭𑀁, 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀢𑁄 𑀆𑀲𑀦𑀸𑀦𑀺 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀸𑀦𑀺, 𑀉𑀤𑀓𑀫𑀡𑀺𑀓𑁄 𑀧𑀢𑀺𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀸𑀧𑀺𑀢𑁄, 𑀢𑁂𑀮𑀧𑀤𑀻𑀧𑁄 𑀆𑀭𑁄𑀧𑀺𑀢𑁄. 𑀬𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀤𑀸𑀦𑀺, 𑀪𑀦𑁆𑀢𑁂, 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀸 𑀓𑀸𑀮𑀁 𑀫𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀢𑀻”𑀢𑀺.

776\. 𑀅𑀣 𑀔𑁄 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀸 𑀦𑀺𑀯𑀸𑀲𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀧𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀘𑀻𑀯𑀭𑀫𑀸𑀤𑀸𑀬 𑀲𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀺𑀁 𑀪𑀺𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀼𑀲𑀁𑀖𑁂𑀦 𑀬𑁂𑀦 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀕𑀸𑀭𑀁 𑀢𑁂𑀦𑀼𑀧𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀫𑀺; 𑀉𑀧𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀫𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀧𑀸𑀤𑁂 𑀧𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀸𑀮𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀕𑀸𑀭𑀁 𑀧𑀯𑀺𑀲𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀫𑀚𑁆𑀛𑀺𑀫𑀁 𑀣𑀫𑁆𑀪𑀁 𑀦𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀸𑀬 𑀧𑀼𑀭𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀸𑀪𑀺𑀫𑀼𑀔𑁄 𑀦𑀺𑀲𑀻𑀤𑀺. 𑀪𑀺𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀼𑀲𑀁𑀖𑁄𑀧𑀺 𑀔𑁄 𑀧𑀸𑀤𑁂 𑀧𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀸𑀮𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀕𑀸𑀭𑀁 𑀧𑀯𑀺𑀲𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀧𑀘𑁆𑀙𑀺𑀫𑀁 𑀪𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺𑀁 𑀦𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀸𑀬 𑀧𑀼𑀭𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀸𑀪𑀺𑀫𑀼𑀔𑁄 𑀦𑀺𑀲𑀻𑀤𑀺 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁𑀬𑁂𑀯 𑀧𑀼𑀭𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸. 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀔𑁄 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑀸 𑀧𑀸𑀤𑁂 𑀧𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀸𑀮𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀕𑀸𑀭𑀁 𑀧𑀯𑀺𑀲𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀧𑀼𑀭𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀺𑀫𑀁 𑀪𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺𑀁 𑀦𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀸𑀬 𑀧𑀘𑁆𑀙𑀺𑀫𑀸𑀪𑀺𑀫𑀼𑀔𑀸 𑀦𑀺𑀲𑀻𑀤𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀼 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁𑀬𑁂𑀯 𑀧𑀼𑀭𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸. 𑀅𑀣 𑀔𑁄 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀸 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑁂 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑁂 𑀩𑀳𑀼𑀤𑁂𑀯 𑀭𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺𑀁 𑀥𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀺𑀬𑀸 𑀓𑀣𑀸𑀬 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀤𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀲𑀫𑀸𑀤𑀧𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀲𑀫𑀼𑀢𑁆𑀢𑁂𑀚𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀲𑀫𑁆𑀧𑀳𑀁𑀲𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀉𑀬𑁆𑀬𑁄𑀚𑁂𑀲𑀺—  “𑀅𑀪𑀺𑀓𑁆𑀓𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀸 𑀔𑁄, 𑀯𑀸𑀲𑁂𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀸, 𑀭𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺. 𑀬𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀤𑀸𑀦𑀺 𑀢𑀼𑀫𑁆𑀳𑁂 𑀓𑀸𑀮𑀁 𑀫𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀣𑀸”𑀢𑀺. “𑀏𑀯𑀁, 𑀪𑀦𑁆𑀢𑁂”𑀢𑀺 𑀔𑁄 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑀸 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑀸 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀢𑁄 𑀧𑀝𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀼𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀉𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀸𑀬𑀸𑀲𑀦𑀸 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀅𑀪𑀺𑀯𑀸𑀤𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀧𑀤𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀺𑀡𑀁 𑀓𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀧𑀓𑁆𑀓𑀫𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀼.

777\. 𑀅𑀣 𑀔𑁄 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀸 𑀅𑀘𑀺𑀭𑀧𑀓𑁆𑀓𑀦𑁆𑀢𑁂𑀲𑀼 𑀧𑀸𑀯𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀓𑁂𑀲𑀼 𑀫𑀮𑁆𑀮𑁂𑀲𑀼 𑀢𑀼𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀻𑀪𑀽𑀢𑀁 𑀢𑀼𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀻𑀪𑀽𑀢𑀁 𑀪𑀺𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀼𑀲𑀁𑀖𑀁 𑀅𑀦𑀼𑀯𑀺𑀮𑁄𑀓𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀆𑀬𑀲𑁆𑀫𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀲𑀸𑀭𑀺𑀧𑀼𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀁 𑀆𑀫𑀦𑁆𑀢𑁂𑀲𑀺—  “𑀯𑀺𑀕𑀢𑀣𑀺𑀦𑀫𑀺𑀤𑁆𑀥𑁄 𑀔𑁄, 𑀲𑀸𑀭𑀺𑀧𑀼𑀢𑁆𑀢, 𑀪𑀺𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀼𑀲𑀁𑀖𑁄. 𑀧𑀝𑀺𑀪𑀸𑀢𑀼 𑀢𑀁, 𑀲𑀸𑀭𑀺𑀧𑀼𑀢𑁆𑀢, 𑀪𑀺𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀽𑀦𑀁 𑀥𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀻𑀓𑀣𑀸. 𑀧𑀺𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀺 𑀫𑁂 𑀆𑀕𑀺𑀮𑀸𑀬𑀢𑀺. 𑀢𑀫𑀳𑀁 𑀆𑀬𑀫𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀸𑀫𑀻”𑀢𑀺. “𑀏𑀯𑀁, 𑀪𑀦𑁆𑀢𑁂”𑀢𑀺 𑀔𑁄 𑀆𑀬𑀲𑁆𑀫𑀸 𑀲𑀸𑀭𑀺𑀧𑀼𑀢𑁆𑀢𑁄 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀢𑁄 𑀧𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄𑀲𑀺.

779\. Now at that time, Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta had recently passed away in Pāvā. Because of his death, the Nigaṇṭhas were divided, split into two factions, had become quarrelsome, engaged in disputes, fallen into arguments, stabbing one another with sharp words, saying: “You don’t understand this Dhamma and Discipline; I understand this Dhamma and Discipline. ## Threes

<Tabs syncKey="paliquote">
<TabItem label="My Translation">
818\. Āvuso, there are three Dhammas well proclaimed by the Bhagavā, the liberated one, the perfectly awakened one, who knows and sees. Therein all should recite in harmony, not quarrel, so that this pure life may endure long and last for a long time, which would be for the welfare and happiness of many people, out of compassion for the world, for the good, welfare, and happiness of gods and humans. What three?

819\. Three unwholesome roots: greed, hatred, and delusion. (1)

820\. Three wholesome roots: non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion. (2)

821\. Three bad conducts: bodily, verbal, and mental. (3)

822\. Three good conducts: bodily, verbal, and mental. (4)

823\. Three unwholesome thoughts: the thought of sensual desire, the thought of ill will, and the thought of harming. (5)

824\. Three wholesome thoughts: the thought of renunciation, the thought of good, and the thought of not harming. (6)

825\. Three unwholesome intentions: the intention of sensual desire, the intention of ill will, and the intention of harming. (7)

826\. Three wholesome intentions: the intention of renunciation, the intention of good, and the intention of not harming. (8)

827\. Three unwholesome `saññā` (apperceptions): the apperception of sensual desire, the apperception of ill will, and the apperception of harming. (9)

828\. Three wholesome apperceptions: the apperception of renunciation, the apperception of good, and the apperception of not harming. (10)

829\. Three unwholesome elements: the element of sensual desire, the element of ill will, and the element of harming. (11)

830\. Three wholesome elements: the element of renunciation, the element of good, and the element of not harming. (12)

831\. Another three elements: the element of sensual desire, the element of form, and the element of the formless. (13)

832\. Another three elements: the element of form, the element of the formless, and the element of cessation. (14)

833\. Another three elements: the inferior, the middling, and the superior. (15)

834\. Three `taṇhā` (cravings): `kāmataṇhā` (craving for sensual pleasures), craving for existence, and `vibhavataṇhā` (craving for non-existence). (16)

835\. Another three cravings: craving for sensual pleasures, craving for form, and craving for the formless. (17)

836\. Another three cravings: craving for form, craving for the formless, and craving for cessation (of existence). (18)

837\. Three fetters: identity view, doubt, and adherence to rites and rituals. (19)

838\. Three `āsavā` (defilements): of sensual pleasure, of existence, and of ignorance. (20)

839\. Three `bhavā` (existences): `kāmabhava` (existence in the realm of sensual desire), existence in the realm of form, and existence in the formless realm. (21)

840\. Three quests: for sensual pleasures, for existence, and for the pure life. (22)

841\. Three kinds of conceit: ‘I am better’, ‘I am equal’, and ‘I am worse’. (23)

842\. Three times: past, future, and present. (24)

843\. Three ends: identity, the origin of identity, and the cessation of identity. (25)

844\. Three `vedanā` (feelings): pleasant, painful, and neutral. (26)

845\. Three kinds of `dukkhatā` (suffering): suffering as pain, suffering due to mental constructions, and suffering due to change. (27)

846\. Three categories: fixed in wrongness, fixed in rightness, and unfixed. (28)

847\. Three kinds of darkness: one doubts, is perplexed, is not fully convinced, and is not confident concerning the past; concerning the future; and concerning the present now. (29)

848\. Three things of the Tathāgata that need not be guarded: The Tathāgata, Āvuso, has perfectly pure bodily conduct; there is no bodily bad conduct of the Tathāgata that the Tathāgata would need to guard, thinking, ‘May others not know this about me.’ The Tathāgata, Āvuso, has perfectly pure verbal conduct; there is no verbal bad conduct of the Tathāgata that the Tathāgata would need to guard, thinking, ‘May others not know this about me.’ The Tathāgata, Āvuso, has perfectly pure mental conduct; there is no mental bad conduct of the Tathāgata that the Tathāgata would need to guard, thinking, ‘May others not know this about me.’ (30)

849\. Three impediments: passion, hatred, and delusion. (31)

850\. Three fires: the fire of passion, the fire of hatred, and the fire of delusion. (32)

851\. Another three fires: the fire of those worthy of offerings, the householder's fire, and the fire of those worthy of gifts. (33)

852\. Three ways of classifying form: visible and impinging, invisible and impinging, and invisible and non-impinging. (34)

853\. Three mental constructions: meritorious, demeritorious, and imperturbable. (35)

854\. Three kinds of persons: the learner, the one beyond training, and the one who is neither a learner nor beyond training. (36)

855\. Three kinds of elders: by birth, by Dhamma, and by convention. (37)

856\. Three bases for making merit: by giving, by virtue, and by development (meditation). (38)

857\. Three bases for admonishment: by what is seen, by what is heard, and by what is suspected. (39)

858\. Three kinds of reappearance in the realm of sensual desire: There are, Āvuso, beings who desire what is present; they wield power over the sensual pleasures that are present, such as humans, some gods, and some beings in the lower realms. This is the first kind of reappearance in the realm of sensual desire. There are, Āvuso, beings who desire what is created; having created and created, they wield power over sensual pleasures, such as the Nimmānaratī gods. This is the second kind of reappearance in the realm of sensual desire. There are, Āvuso, beings who desire what is created by others; they wield power over sensual pleasures created by others, such as the Paranimmitavasavattī gods. This is the third kind of reappearance in the realm of sensual desire. (40)

859\. Three kinds of reappearance in happiness: There are, Āvuso, beings who generate happiness again and again and dwell in happiness, such as the Brahmakāyika gods. This is the first kind of reappearance in happiness. There are, Āvuso, beings who are permeated, pervaded, filled, and suffused with happiness. They utter an inspired utterance now and then: ‘Oh, what happiness! Oh, what happiness!’, such as the Ābhassara gods. This is the second kind of reappearance in happiness. There are, Āvuso, beings who are permeated, pervaded, filled, and suffused with happiness. Contented, they experience only a peaceful happiness, such as the Subhakiṇhā gods. This is the third kind of reappearance in happiness. (41)

860\. Three kinds of `paññā` (wisdom): the learner's, that of one beyond training, and that of one who is neither a learner nor beyond training. (42)

861\. Another three kinds of wisdom: based on thinking, based on learning, and based on development (meditation). (43)

862\. Three weapons: learning, seclusion, and wisdom. (44)

863\. Three faculties: the faculty of thinking ‘I shall know the unknown’, the faculty of final knowledge, and the faculty of one who has final knowledge. (45)

864\. Three eyes: the physical eye, the divine eye, and the eye of wisdom. (46)

865\. Three trainings: in higher virtue, in higher mind, and in higher wisdom. (47)

866\. Three developments: of the body, of the mind, and of wisdom. (48)

867\. Three supreme things: supreme vision, supreme path, and supreme liberation. (49)

868\. Three kinds of focus: focus with reflection and consideration, focus without reflection but with consideration only, and focus without reflection or consideration. (50)

869\. Another three kinds of focus: empty, signless, and undirected. (51)

870\. Three purities: bodily, verbal, and mental. (52)

871\. Three sagacities: bodily, verbal, and mental. (53)

872\. Three skills: in gain, in loss, and in means. (54)

873\. Three forms of intoxication: with health, with youth, and with life. (55)

874\. Three authorities: oneself as authority, the world as authority, and the Dhamma as authority. (56)

875\. Three topics of conversation: one might speak about the past, saying, ‘Thus it was in the past’; or about the future, saying, ‘Thus it will be in the future’; or about the present now, saying, ‘Thus it is now in the present.’ (57)

876\. Three kinds of knowledge: knowledge of the recollection of past lives, knowledge of the passing away and reappearance of beings, and knowledge of the destruction of the defilements. (58)

877\. Three abodes: divine, Brahmā, and of the liberated. (59)

878\. Three kinds of miracles: of psychic power, of mind-reading, and of instruction. (60)

879\. Āvuso, these are the three Dhammas well proclaimed by the Bhagavā, the liberated one, the perfectly awakened one, who knows and sees. Therein all should recite in harmony, not quarrel, so that this pure life may endure long and last for a long time, which would be for the welfare and happiness of many people, out of compassion for the world, for the good, welfare, and happiness of gods and humans.

818\. 𑀅𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀺 𑀔𑁄, 𑀆𑀯𑀼𑀲𑁄, 𑀢𑁂𑀦 𑀪𑀕𑀯𑀢𑀸 𑀚𑀸𑀦𑀢𑀸 𑀧𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀢𑀸 𑀅𑀭𑀳𑀢𑀸 𑀲𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀸𑀲𑀫𑁆𑀩𑀼𑀤𑁆𑀥𑁂𑀦 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀥𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀸 𑀲𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀤𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀸𑀢𑀸. 𑀢𑀢𑁆𑀣 𑀲𑀩𑁆𑀩𑁂𑀳𑁂𑀯 𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀕𑀸𑀬𑀺𑀢𑀩𑁆𑀩𑀁…𑀧𑁂…  𑀅𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀸𑀬 𑀳𑀺𑀢𑀸𑀬 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀸𑀬 𑀤𑁂𑀯𑀫𑀦𑀼𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀸𑀦𑀁. 𑀓𑀢𑀫𑁂 𑀢𑀬𑁄?

819\. 𑀢𑀻𑀡𑀺 𑀅𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀫𑀽𑀮𑀸𑀦𑀺—  𑀮𑁄𑀪𑁄 𑀅𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀫𑀽𑀮𑀁, 𑀤𑁄𑀲𑁄 𑀅𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀫𑀽𑀮𑀁, 𑀫𑁄𑀳𑁄 𑀅𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀫𑀽𑀮𑀁. (1)

820\. 𑀢𑀻𑀡𑀺 𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀫𑀽𑀮𑀸𑀦𑀺—  𑀅𑀮𑁄𑀪𑁄 𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀫𑀽𑀮𑀁, 𑀅𑀤𑁄𑀲𑁄 𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀫𑀽𑀮𑀁, 𑀅𑀫𑁄𑀳𑁄 𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀫𑀽𑀮𑀁. (2)

821\. 𑀢𑀻𑀡𑀺 𑀤𑀼𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀸𑀦𑀺—  𑀓𑀸𑀬𑀤𑀼𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀁, 𑀯𑀘𑀻𑀤𑀼𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀁, 𑀫𑀦𑁄𑀤𑀼𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀁. (3)

822\. 𑀢𑀻𑀡𑀺 𑀲𑀼𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀸𑀦𑀺—  𑀓𑀸𑀬𑀲𑀼𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀁, 𑀯𑀘𑀻𑀲𑀼𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀁, 𑀫𑀦𑁄𑀲𑀼𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀁. (4)

823\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀅𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀯𑀺𑀢𑀓𑁆𑀓𑀸—  𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀯𑀺𑀢𑀓𑁆𑀓𑁄, 𑀩𑁆𑀬𑀸𑀧𑀸𑀤𑀯𑀺𑀢𑀓𑁆𑀓𑁄, 𑀯𑀺𑀳𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀸𑀯𑀺𑀢𑀓𑁆𑀓𑁄. (5)

824\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀯𑀺𑀢𑀓𑁆𑀓𑀸—  𑀦𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀯𑀺𑀢𑀓𑁆𑀓𑁄, 𑀅𑀩𑁆𑀬𑀸𑀧𑀸𑀤𑀯𑀺𑀢𑀓𑁆𑀓𑁄, 𑀅𑀯𑀺𑀳𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀸𑀯𑀺𑀢𑀓𑁆𑀓𑁄. (6)

825\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀅𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀧𑁆𑀧𑀸—  𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀧𑁆𑀧𑁄, 𑀩𑁆𑀬𑀸𑀧𑀸𑀤𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀧𑁆𑀧𑁄, 𑀯𑀺𑀳𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀸𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀧𑁆𑀧𑁄. (7)

826\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀧𑁆𑀧𑀸—  𑀦𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀧𑁆𑀧𑁄, 𑀅𑀩𑁆𑀬𑀸𑀧𑀸𑀤𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀧𑁆𑀧𑁄, 𑀅𑀯𑀺𑀳𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀸𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀧𑁆𑀧𑁄. (8)

827\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀅𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀲𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸—  𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀲𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸, 𑀩𑁆𑀬𑀸𑀧𑀸𑀤𑀲𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸, 𑀯𑀺𑀳𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀸𑀲𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸. (9)

828\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀲𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸—  𑀦𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀲𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸, 𑀅𑀩𑁆𑀬𑀸𑀧𑀸𑀤𑀲𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸, 𑀅𑀯𑀺𑀳𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀸𑀲𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸. (10)

829\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀅𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼𑀬𑁄—  𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼, 𑀩𑁆𑀬𑀸𑀧𑀸𑀤𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼, 𑀯𑀺𑀳𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀸𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼. (11)

830\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀓𑀼𑀲𑀮𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼𑀬𑁄—  𑀦𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼, 𑀅𑀩𑁆𑀬𑀸𑀧𑀸𑀤𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼, 𑀅𑀯𑀺𑀳𑀺𑀁𑀲𑀸𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼. (12)

831\. 𑀅𑀧𑀭𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼𑀬𑁄—  𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼, 𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼, 𑀅𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼. (13)

832\. 𑀅𑀧𑀭𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼𑀬𑁄—  𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼, 𑀅𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼, 𑀦𑀺𑀭𑁄𑀥𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼. (14)

833\. 𑀅𑀧𑀭𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼𑀬𑁄—  𑀳𑀻𑀦𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼, 𑀫𑀚𑁆𑀛𑀺𑀫𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼, 𑀧𑀡𑀻𑀢𑀥𑀸𑀢𑀼. (15)

834\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸—  𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸, 𑀪𑀯𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸, 𑀯𑀺𑀪𑀯𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸. (16)

835\. 𑀅𑀧𑀭𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸—  𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸, 𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸, 𑀅𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸. (17)

836\. 𑀅𑀧𑀭𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸—  𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸, 𑀅𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸, 𑀦𑀺𑀭𑁄𑀥𑀢𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸. (18)

837\. 𑀢𑀻𑀡𑀺 𑀲𑀁𑀬𑁄𑀚𑀦𑀸𑀦𑀺—  𑀲𑀓𑁆𑀓𑀸𑀬𑀤𑀺𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀺, 𑀯𑀺𑀘𑀺𑀓𑀺𑀘𑁆𑀙𑀸, 𑀲𑀻𑀮𑀩𑁆𑀩𑀢𑀧𑀭𑀸𑀫𑀸𑀲𑁄. (19)

838\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀆𑀲𑀯𑀸—  𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀸𑀲𑀯𑁄, 𑀪𑀯𑀸𑀲𑀯𑁄, 𑀅𑀯𑀺𑀚𑁆𑀚𑀸𑀲𑀯𑁄. (20)

839\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀪𑀯𑀸—  𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀪𑀯𑁄, 𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀪𑀯𑁄, 𑀅𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀪𑀯𑁄. (21)

840\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀏𑀲𑀦𑀸—  𑀓𑀸𑀫𑁂𑀲𑀦𑀸, 𑀪𑀯𑁂𑀲𑀦𑀸, 𑀩𑁆𑀭𑀳𑁆𑀫𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀬𑁂𑀲𑀦𑀸. (22)

841\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀯𑀺𑀥𑀸—  𑀲𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑁄𑀳𑀫𑀲𑁆𑀫𑀻𑀢𑀺 𑀯𑀺𑀥𑀸, 𑀲𑀤𑀺𑀲𑁄𑀳𑀫𑀲𑁆𑀫𑀻𑀢𑀺 𑀯𑀺𑀥𑀸, 𑀳𑀻𑀦𑁄𑀳𑀫𑀲𑁆𑀫𑀻𑀢𑀺 𑀯𑀺𑀥𑀸. (23)

842\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀅𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀸—  𑀅𑀢𑀻𑀢𑁄 𑀅𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀸, 𑀅𑀦𑀸𑀕𑀢𑁄 𑀅𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀸, 𑀧𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀼𑀧𑁆𑀧𑀦𑁆𑀦𑁄 𑀅𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀸. (24)

843\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀅𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀸—  𑀲𑀓𑁆𑀓𑀸𑀬𑁄 𑀅𑀦𑁆𑀢𑁄, 𑀲𑀓𑁆𑀓𑀸𑀬𑀲𑀫𑀼𑀤𑀬𑁄 𑀅𑀦𑁆𑀢𑁄, 𑀲𑀓𑁆𑀓𑀸𑀬𑀦𑀺𑀭𑁄𑀥𑁄 𑀅𑀦𑁆𑀢𑁄. (25)

844\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀯𑁂𑀤𑀦𑀸—  𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀸 𑀯𑁂𑀤𑀦𑀸, 𑀤𑀼𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀸 𑀯𑁂𑀤𑀦𑀸, 𑀅𑀤𑀼𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀫𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀸 𑀯𑁂𑀤𑀦𑀸. (26)

845\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀤𑀼𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀢𑀸—  𑀤𑀼𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀤𑀼𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀢𑀸, 𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀔𑀸𑀭𑀤𑀼𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀢𑀸, 𑀯𑀺𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀡𑀸𑀫𑀤𑀼𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀢𑀸. (27)

846\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀭𑀸𑀲𑀻—  𑀫𑀺𑀘𑁆𑀙𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀦𑀺𑀬𑀢𑁄 𑀭𑀸𑀲𑀺, 𑀲𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀦𑀺𑀬𑀢𑁄 𑀭𑀸𑀲𑀺, 𑀅𑀦𑀺𑀬𑀢𑁄 𑀭𑀸𑀲𑀺. (28)

847\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀢𑀫𑀸—  𑀅𑀢𑀻𑀢𑀁 𑀯𑀸 𑀅𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀦𑀁 𑀆𑀭𑀩𑁆𑀪 𑀓𑀗𑁆𑀔𑀢𑀺 𑀯𑀺𑀘𑀺𑀓𑀺𑀘𑁆𑀙𑀢𑀺 𑀦𑀸𑀥𑀺𑀫𑀼𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀢𑀺 𑀦 𑀲𑀫𑁆𑀧𑀲𑀻𑀤𑀢𑀺, 𑀅𑀦𑀸𑀕𑀢𑀁 𑀯𑀸 𑀅𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀦𑀁 𑀆𑀭𑀩𑁆𑀪 𑀓𑀗𑁆𑀔𑀢𑀺 𑀯𑀺𑀘𑀺𑀓𑀺𑀘𑁆𑀙𑀢𑀺 𑀦𑀸𑀥𑀺𑀫𑀼𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀢𑀺 𑀦 𑀲𑀫𑁆𑀧𑀲𑀻𑀤𑀢𑀺, 𑀏𑀢𑀭𑀳𑀺 𑀯𑀸 𑀧𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀼𑀧𑁆𑀧𑀦𑁆𑀦𑀁 𑀅𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀸𑀦𑀁 𑀆𑀭𑀩𑁆𑀪 𑀓𑀗𑁆𑀔𑀢𑀺 𑀯𑀺𑀘𑀺𑀓𑀺𑀘𑁆𑀙𑀢𑀺 𑀦𑀸𑀥𑀺𑀫𑀼𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀢𑀺 𑀦 𑀲𑀫𑁆𑀧𑀲𑀻𑀤𑀢𑀺. (29)

848\. 𑀢𑀻𑀡𑀺 𑀢𑀣𑀸𑀕𑀢𑀲𑁆𑀲 𑀅𑀭𑀓𑁆𑀔𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀸𑀦𑀺—  𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀲𑀼𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀓𑀸𑀬𑀲𑀫𑀸𑀘𑀸𑀭𑁄 𑀆𑀯𑀼𑀲𑁄 𑀢𑀣𑀸𑀕𑀢𑁄, 𑀦𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀺 𑀢𑀣𑀸𑀕𑀢𑀲𑁆𑀲 𑀓𑀸𑀬𑀤𑀼𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀁, 𑀬𑀁 𑀢𑀣𑀸𑀕𑀢𑁄 𑀭𑀓𑁆𑀔𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬—  ‘𑀫𑀸 𑀫𑁂 𑀇𑀤𑀁 𑀧𑀭𑁄 𑀅𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸𑀲𑀻’𑀢𑀺. 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀲𑀼𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀯𑀘𑀻𑀲𑀫𑀸𑀘𑀸𑀭𑁄 𑀆𑀯𑀼𑀲𑁄, 𑀢𑀣𑀸𑀕𑀢𑁄, 𑀦𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀺 𑀢𑀣𑀸𑀕𑀢𑀲𑁆𑀲 𑀯𑀘𑀻𑀤𑀼𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀁, 𑀬𑀁 𑀢𑀣𑀸𑀕𑀢𑁄 𑀭𑀓𑁆𑀔𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬—  ‘𑀫𑀸 𑀫𑁂 𑀇𑀤𑀁 𑀧𑀭𑁄 𑀅𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸𑀲𑀻’𑀢𑀺. 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀲𑀼𑀤𑁆𑀥𑀫𑀦𑁄𑀲𑀫𑀸𑀘𑀸𑀭𑁄, 𑀆𑀯𑀼𑀲𑁄, 𑀢𑀣𑀸𑀕𑀢𑁄, 𑀦𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀺 𑀢𑀣𑀸𑀕𑀢𑀲𑁆𑀲 𑀫𑀦𑁄𑀤𑀼𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀭𑀺𑀢𑀁 𑀬𑀁 𑀢𑀣𑀸𑀕𑀢𑁄 𑀭𑀓𑁆𑀔𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬—  ‘𑀫𑀸 𑀫𑁂 𑀇𑀤𑀁 𑀧𑀭𑁄 𑀅𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸𑀲𑀻’𑀢𑀺. (30)

849\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀓𑀺𑀜𑁆𑀘𑀦𑀸—  𑀭𑀸𑀕𑁄 𑀓𑀺𑀜𑁆𑀘𑀦𑀁, 𑀤𑁄𑀲𑁄 𑀓𑀺𑀜𑁆𑀘𑀦𑀁, 𑀫𑁄𑀳𑁄 𑀓𑀺𑀜𑁆𑀘𑀦𑀁. (31)

850\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀅𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀻—  𑀭𑀸𑀕𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀺, 𑀤𑁄𑀲𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀺, 𑀫𑁄𑀳𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀺. (32)

851\. 𑀅𑀧𑀭𑁂𑀧𑀺 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀅𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀻—  𑀆𑀳𑀼𑀦𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀺, 𑀕𑀳𑀧𑀢𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀺, 𑀤𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀺𑀡𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀺. (33)

852\. 𑀢𑀺𑀯𑀺𑀥𑁂𑀦 𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀕𑀳𑁄—  𑀲𑀦𑀺𑀤𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀦𑀲𑀧𑁆𑀧𑀝𑀺𑀖𑀁 𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀁, 𑀅𑀦𑀺𑀤𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀦𑀲𑀧𑁆𑀧𑀝𑀺𑀖𑀁 𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀁, 𑀅𑀦𑀺𑀤𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀦𑀅𑀧𑁆𑀧𑀝𑀺𑀖𑀁 𑀭𑀽𑀧𑀁. (34)

853\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀔𑀸𑀭𑀸—  𑀧𑀼𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸𑀪𑀺𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀔𑀸𑀭𑁄, 𑀅𑀧𑀼𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸𑀪𑀺𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀔𑀸𑀭𑁄, 𑀆𑀦𑁂𑀜𑁆𑀚𑀸𑀪𑀺𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀔𑀸𑀭𑁄. (35)

854\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀧𑀼𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀮𑀸—  𑀲𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑁄 𑀧𑀼𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀮𑁄, 𑀅𑀲𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑁄 𑀧𑀼𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀮𑁄, 𑀦𑁂𑀯𑀲𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑁄 𑀦𑀸𑀲𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑁄 𑀧𑀼𑀕𑁆𑀕𑀮𑁄. (36)

855\. 𑀢𑀬𑁄 𑀣𑁂𑀭𑀸—  𑀚𑀸𑀢𑀺𑀣𑁂𑀭𑁄, 𑀥𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀣𑁂𑀭𑁄, 𑀲𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀼𑀢𑀺𑀣𑁂𑀭𑁄. (37)

856\. 𑀢𑀻𑀡𑀺 𑀧𑀼𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀓𑀺𑀭𑀺𑀬𑀯𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀽𑀦𑀺—  𑀤𑀸𑀦𑀫𑀬𑀁 𑀧𑀼𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀓𑀺𑀭𑀺𑀬𑀯𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀼, 𑀲𑀻𑀮𑀫𑀬𑀁 𑀧𑀼𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀓𑀺𑀭𑀺𑀬𑀯𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀼, 𑀪𑀸𑀯𑀦𑀸𑀫𑀬𑀁 𑀧𑀼𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀓𑀺𑀭𑀺𑀬𑀯𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀼. (38)

857\. 𑀢𑀻𑀡𑀺 𑀘𑁄𑀤𑀦𑀸𑀯𑀢𑁆𑀣𑀽𑀦𑀺—  𑀤𑀺𑀝𑁆𑀞𑁂𑀦, 𑀲𑀼𑀢𑁂𑀦, 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀲𑀗𑁆𑀓𑀸𑀬. (39)

858\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀽𑀧𑀧𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺𑀬𑁄—  𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀲𑁄 𑀲𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀸 𑀧𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀼𑀧𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀺𑀢𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀸, 𑀢𑁂 𑀧𑀘𑁆𑀘𑀼𑀧𑀝𑁆𑀞𑀺𑀢𑁂𑀲𑀼 𑀓𑀸𑀫𑁂𑀲𑀼 𑀯𑀲𑀁 𑀯𑀢𑁆𑀢𑁂𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀺, 𑀲𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀣𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀫𑀦𑀼𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀸 𑀏𑀓𑀘𑁆𑀘𑁂 𑀘 𑀤𑁂𑀯𑀸 𑀏𑀓𑀘𑁆𑀘𑁂 𑀘 𑀯𑀺𑀦𑀺𑀧𑀸𑀢𑀺𑀓𑀸. 𑀅𑀬𑀁 𑀧𑀞𑀫𑀸 𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀽𑀧𑀧𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺. 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀲𑁄, 𑀲𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀸 𑀦𑀺𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀺𑀢𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀸, 𑀢𑁂 𑀦𑀺𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀺𑀦𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀦𑀺𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀺𑀦𑀺𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀓𑀸𑀫𑁂𑀲𑀼 𑀯𑀲𑀁 𑀯𑀢𑁆𑀢𑁂𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀺, 𑀲𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀣𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀤𑁂𑀯𑀸 𑀦𑀺𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀸𑀦𑀭𑀢𑀻. 𑀅𑀬𑀁 𑀤𑀼𑀢𑀺𑀬𑀸 𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀽𑀧𑀧𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺. 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀲𑁄 𑀲𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀸 𑀧𑀭𑀦𑀺𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀺𑀢𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀸, 𑀢𑁂 𑀧𑀭𑀦𑀺𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀺𑀢𑁂𑀲𑀼 𑀓𑀸𑀫𑁂𑀲𑀼 𑀯𑀲𑀁 𑀯𑀢𑁆𑀢𑁂𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀺, 𑀲𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀣𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀤𑁂𑀯𑀸 𑀧𑀭𑀦𑀺𑀫𑁆𑀫𑀺𑀢𑀯𑀲𑀯𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀻. 𑀅𑀬𑀁 𑀢𑀢𑀺𑀬𑀸 𑀓𑀸𑀫𑀽𑀧𑀧𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺. (40)

859\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀽𑀧𑀧𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺𑀬𑁄—  𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀲𑁄 𑀲𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀸 𑀉𑀧𑁆𑀧𑀸𑀤𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀉𑀧𑁆𑀧𑀸𑀤𑁂𑀢𑁆𑀯𑀸 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀁 𑀯𑀺𑀳𑀭𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀺, 𑀲𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀣𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀤𑁂𑀯𑀸 𑀩𑁆𑀭𑀳𑁆𑀫𑀓𑀸𑀬𑀺𑀓𑀸. 𑀅𑀬𑀁 𑀧𑀞𑀫𑀸 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀽𑀧𑀧𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺. 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀲𑁄, 𑀲𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀸 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑁂𑀦 𑀅𑀪𑀺𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀦𑀸 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀦𑀸 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀧𑀽𑀭𑀸 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀧𑁆𑀨𑀼𑀝𑀸. 𑀢𑁂 𑀓𑀤𑀸𑀘𑀺 𑀓𑀭𑀳𑀘𑀺 𑀉𑀤𑀸𑀦𑀁 𑀉𑀤𑀸𑀦𑁂𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀺—  ‘𑀅𑀳𑁄 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀁, 𑀅𑀳𑁄 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀦𑁆’𑀢𑀺, 𑀲𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀣𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀤𑁂𑀯𑀸 𑀆𑀪𑀲𑁆𑀲𑀭𑀸. 𑀅𑀬𑀁 𑀤𑀼𑀢𑀺𑀬𑀸 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀽𑀧𑀧𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺. 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀲𑁄, 𑀲𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀸 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑁂𑀦 𑀅𑀪𑀺𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀦𑀸 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀦𑀸 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀧𑀽𑀭𑀸 𑀧𑀭𑀺𑀧𑁆𑀨𑀼𑀝𑀸. 𑀢𑁂 𑀲𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀁𑀬𑁂𑀯 𑀢𑀼𑀲𑀺𑀢𑀸 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀁 𑀧𑀝𑀺𑀲𑀁𑀯𑁂𑀤𑁂𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀺, 𑀲𑁂𑀬𑁆𑀬𑀣𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀤𑁂𑀯𑀸 𑀲𑀼𑀪𑀓𑀺𑀡𑁆𑀳𑀸. 𑀅𑀬𑀁 𑀢𑀢𑀺𑀬𑀸 𑀲𑀼𑀔𑀽𑀧𑀧𑀢𑁆𑀢𑀺. (41)

860\. 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸—  𑀲𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀸 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸, 𑀅𑀲𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀸 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸, 𑀦𑁂𑀯𑀲𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀸𑀦𑀸𑀲𑁂𑀓𑁆𑀔𑀸 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸. (42)

861\. 𑀅𑀧𑀭𑀸𑀧𑀺 𑀢𑀺𑀲𑁆𑀲𑁄 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸—  𑀘𑀺𑀦𑁆𑀢𑀸𑀫𑀬𑀸 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸, 𑀲𑀼𑀢𑀫𑀬𑀸 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸, 𑀪𑀸𑀯𑀦𑀸𑀫𑀬𑀸 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸. (43)

862\. 𑀢𑀻𑀡𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀥𑀸𑀦𑀺—  𑀲𑀼𑀢𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀥𑀁, 𑀧𑀯𑀺𑀯𑁂𑀓𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀥𑀁, 𑀧𑀜𑁆𑀜𑀸𑀯𑀼𑀥𑀁. (44)
