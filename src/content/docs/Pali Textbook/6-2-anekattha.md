The usage examples provided here are not exhaustive, other usages are possible.

A `taddhita` ("secondary derivative") is a noun made from another noun by
adding an affix (`paccaya`) in the sense a rule names, the `vibhatti` ending
of the base being elided {317} (section [6.1](../6-1-apacca/)). This section
covers the four rules whose affixes carry many senses, the group the Basics
chapter calls `aneʼkattha` ("various meanings"): `ṇika` in the senses "mixed
with", "crosses by", "goes by" and "carries by" {350}; `ṇika` in the senses
"studies that", "made by that" and the rest {351}; `ṇa` after a word for a
dye in the sense "dyed with that", and in the sense "this is his" and other
senses {352}; and `ima` and `iya` in the sense "born" and the like {353}. In
each rule the vutti takes its senses one by one, with a base phrase for each:
the phrase shows the `vibhatti` ending the base takes (③ "by which", ② "that",
⑦ "in that", ⑥ "his"), and the derived word stands for the whole phrase. The
`ṇ` written in `ṇika` and `ṇa` is not part of the affix: it marks the affixes
after which the first vowel of the base is strengthened, `a` to `ā`, `i` to
`e`, `u` to `o` (`vuddhi`) {400}, {405}, unless a conjunct consonant follows
that vowel, and the `ṇ` itself is elided {396}. Appendix H tables every
`taddhita` rule with its examples.

### 6.2.1 `ṇika` for "mixed with", "crosses by", "goes by", "carries by" {350}

* `ṇika` is optionally added to a noun in the ③ ending ("by which") in four
  senses: "mixed with that" (`saṁsaṭṭha`), "crosses by that" (`tarati`), "goes
  by that" (`carati`) and "carries by that" (`vahati`). Food mixed with sesame
  (`tilena saṁsaṭṭhaṁ`) is `telikaṁ`, and so `goḷikaṁ` ("with molasses"),
  `ghātikaṁ` ("with ghee"); one who crosses by boat (`nāvāya tarati`) is
  `nāviko`, and so `oḷumpiko` ("by raft"); one who goes by cart (`sakaṭena
  carati`) is `sākaṭiko`, and so `pattiko`, `pādiko` ("on foot"), `daṇḍiko`
  ("with a staff"), `dhammiko` ("by the Dhamma"); one who carries by the head
  (`sīsena vahati`) is `sīsiko`, and so `aṁsiko` ("by the shoulder"),
  `khandhiko` ("by the back"), `aṅguliko` ("with the fingers").
* By the word `vā` ("or"), which the vutti takes in the sense of an option,
  `ṇika` is used in other senses as well: one who dwells in Rājagaha (`rājagahe
  vasati`), or was born there (`rājagahe jāto`), is `rājagahiko`, and so
  `māgadhiko` ("of Magadha"), `sāvatthiko` ("of Sāvatthī"), `kāpilavatthiko`
  ("of Kapilavatthu"), `pāṭaliputtiko` ("of Pāṭaliputta"), `vesāliko` ("of
  Vesālī").

#### Example

> tilena saṁsaṭṭhaṁ bhojanaṁ → **telikaṁ**

🚻⨀③(tilena) 🚻⨀①🔽(ta)(saṁsaṭṭhaṁ) 🚻⨀①(bhojanaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🔽(ṇika)(telikaṁ)  
with sesame / mixed / food → food mixed with sesame  
*`tilena saṁsaṭṭhaṁ bhojanaṁ` ("food mixed with sesame") becomes `telikaṁ` with the affix `ṇika` {350}: the `ṇ` is elided {396}, the first vowel of `tila` takes `vuddhi`, `i` to `e` {400}, {405}, its final `a` is elided before the affix {83}, and the neuter `si` becomes `aṁ` {219}.*

1. > nāvāya tarati → **nāviko**

🚺⨀③(nāvāya) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(tarati) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(nāviko)

*`nāvāya tarati` ("he crosses by boat") becomes `nāviko`, a sailor, with `ṇika` {350} in the sense "crosses by that": the `ṇ` is elided {396} and the final `ā` of `nāvā` is elided before the affix {83}; the first vowel is already long and takes no `vuddhi`.*

2. > sakaṭena carati → **sākaṭiko**

🚻⨀③(sakaṭena) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(carati) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(sākaṭiko)  
by cart / goes → one who goes by cart, a carter  
*`sakaṭena carati` ("he goes by cart") becomes `sākaṭiko`, a carter, with `ṇika` {350} in the sense "goes by that": the `ṇ` is elided {396}, the first `a` of `sakaṭa` becomes `ā` {400}, {405}, and its final `a` is elided before the affix {83}.*

3. > sīsena vahati → **sīsiko**

🚻⨀③(sīsena) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(vahati) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(sīsiko)  
by the head / carries → one who carries on the head  
*`sīsena vahati` ("he carries by the head") becomes `sīsiko` with `ṇika` {350} in the sense "carries by that", as `aṁsena vahati` ("he carries by the shoulder") becomes `aṁsiko`: the `ṇ` is elided {396} and the final `a` of `sīsa` is elided before the affix {83}; the first vowel is already long and takes no `vuddhi`.*

4. > rājagahe vasati → **rājagahiko**

🚻⨀⑦(rājagahe) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(vasati) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(rājagahiko)  
in Rājagaha / dwells → one who dwells in Rājagaha  
*`rājagahe vasati` ("he dwells in Rājagaha") becomes `rājagahiko` with `ṇika` in a sense the rule's `vā` admits {350}, the vutti giving the same word for `rājagahe jāto` ("born in Rājagaha"): the `ṇ` is elided {396} and the final `a` of `rājagaha` is elided before the affix {83}; the first vowel is already long and takes no `vuddhi`.*

### 6.2.2 `ṇika` for "studies that", "made by that", and the other senses {351}

* `ṇika` is optionally added in seven more senses, the base taking the
  `vibhatti` ending its phrase needs: "he studies that" (`tamadhīte`, ②), "made
  by that" and the like (`tenakatādi`, ③), "located in that" (`tamhi
  sannidhānā`, ⑦), "appointed to that" (`tattha niyutto`, ⑦), "that is his
  craft" (`tamassa sippaṁ`), "that is his merchandise" (`tamassa bhaṇḍaṁ`) and
  "that is his livelihood" (`tamassa jīvikaṁ`).
* By the mention of `ādi` ("and the like") in the rule, `ṇika` is to be
  applied in other senses as well; the vutti lists eleven more: killed with,
  bound with, whose weapon, whose ailment, devoted to, the property of, bought
  with, measuring, a heap of, worth, and plays with.

#### 6.2.2.1 `tamadhīte` ("he studies that")

* The thing studied stands in the ② ending: one who studies the Vinaya
  (`vinayaṁ adhīte`) is `venayiko`, and so `suttantiko` ("the Suttas"),
  `ābhidhammiko` ("the Abhidhamma"), `veyyākaraṇiko` ("grammar").

> vinayaṁ adhīte → **venayiko**

🚹⨀②(vinayaṁ) 🔵⨀🤟▶️(adhīte) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(venayiko)  
the Vinaya / studies → one who studies the Vinaya  
*`vinayaṁ adhīte` ("he studies the Vinaya") becomes `venayiko` with `ṇika` {351}: the `ṇ` is elided {396}, the first vowel of `vinaya` takes `vuddhi`, `i` to `e` {400}, {405}, and its final `a` is elided before the affix {83}.*

#### 6.2.2.2 `tenakatādi` ("made by that" and the like)

* The maker stands in the ③ ending: an act done by the body (`kāyena kataṁ
  kammaṁ`) is `kāyikaṁ`, and so `vācasikaṁ` ("by speech", with an `s` inserted
  {184}), `mānasikaṁ` ("by the mind").

> kāyena kataṁ kammaṁ → **kāyikaṁ**

🚹⨀③(kāyena) 🚻⨀①🔽(ta)(kataṁ) 🚻⨀①(kammaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🔽(ṇika)(kāyikaṁ)  
by the body / done / act → a bodily act  
*`kāyena kataṁ kammaṁ` ("an act done by the body") becomes `kāyikaṁ` with `ṇika` {351}: the `ṇ` is elided {396}, the final `a` of `kāya` is elided before the affix {83}, the first vowel being already long, and the neuter `si` becomes `aṁ` {219}.*

#### 6.2.2.3 `tamhi sannidhānā` ("located in that")

* The place stands in the ⑦ ending: a feeling located in the body (`sarīre
  sannidhānā vedanā`) is `sārīrikā`, and so `mānasikā` ("in the mind").

> sarīre sannidhānā vedanā → **sārīrikā**

🚻⨀⑦(sarīre) 🚺⨀①(sannidhānā) 🚺⨀①(vedanā) → 🚺⨀①🔽(ṇika)(sārīrikā)  
in the body / located / feeling → a bodily feeling  
*`sarīre sannidhānā vedanā` ("a feeling located in the body") becomes `sārīrikā` with `ṇika` {351}: the `ṇ` is elided {396}, the first `a` of `sarīra` becomes `ā` {400}, {405}, its final `a` is elided before the affix {83}, and the feminine takes the affix `ā` {237}.*

#### 6.2.2.4 `tattha niyutto` ("appointed to that")

* The post stands in the ⑦ ending: one appointed to the door (`dvāre niyutto`)
  is `dovāriko`, a doorkeeper, and so `bhaṇḍāgāriko` ("to the storehouse"),
  `nāgariko` ("to the city"), `nāvakammiko` ("to building work").

> dvāre niyutto → **dovāriko**

🚻⨀⑦(dvāre) 🚹⨀①🔽(ta)(niyutto) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(dovāriko)  
at the door / appointed → one appointed to the door, a doorkeeper  
*`dvāre niyutto` ("appointed to the door") becomes `dovāriko`, a doorkeeper, with `ṇika` {351}: the `ṇ` is elided {396}, an `o` is inserted after the `d` in place of `vuddhi` {401}, and the final `a` of `dvāra` is elided before the affix {83}.*

#### 6.2.2.5 `tamassa sippaṁ` ("that is his craft")

* The craft is named with `assa` ("his"): one whose craft is the lute (`vīṇā
  assa sippaṁ`) is `veṇiko`, a lute-player, and so `pāṇaviko` ("the small
  drum"), `modiṅgiko` ("the `mudiṅga` drum"), `vaṁsiko` ("the bamboo flute").

> vīṇā assa sippaṁ → **veṇiko**

🚺⨀①(vīṇā) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(assa) 🚻⨀①(sippaṁ) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(veṇiko)  
the lute / his / craft → one whose craft is the lute, a lute-player  
*`vīṇā assa sippaṁ` ("the lute is his craft") becomes `veṇiko`, a lute-player, with `ṇika` {351}: the `ṇ` is elided {396}, the first vowel of `vīṇā` takes `vuddhi`, `ī` to `e` {400}, {405}, and its final `ā` is elided before the affix {83}.*

#### 6.2.2.6 `tamassa bhaṇḍaṁ` ("that is his merchandise")

* One whose merchandise is perfume (`gandho assa bhaṇḍaṁ`) is `gandhiko`, a
  perfumer, and so `teliko` ("oil"), `goḷiko` ("molasses").

> gandho assa bhaṇḍaṁ → **gandhiko**

🚹⨀①(gandho) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(assa) 🚻⨀①(bhaṇḍaṁ) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(gandhiko)  
perfume / his / merchandise → one whose merchandise is perfume, a perfumer  
*`gandho assa bhaṇḍaṁ` ("perfume is his merchandise") becomes `gandhiko`, a perfumer, with `ṇika` {351}: the `ṇ` is elided {396} and the final `a` of `gandha` is elided before the affix {83}; the first vowel stands before a conjunct and takes no `vuddhi` {400}.*

#### 6.2.2.7 `tamassa jīvikaṁ` ("that is his livelihood")

* One who lives by killing sheep (`urabbhaṁ hantvā jīvati`) is `orabbhiko`, a
  sheep-butcher; one who lives by killing deer (`magaṁ hantvā jīvati`) is
  `māgaviko`, a hunter, and so `sokariko` ("pigs"), `sākuṇiko` ("birds").

> urabbhaṁ hantvā jīvati → **orabbhiko**

🚹⨀②(urabbhaṁ) 🔽(tvā)(hantvā) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(jīvati) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(orabbhiko)  
a ram / having killed / lives → one who lives by killing sheep, a sheep-butcher  
*`urabbhaṁ hantvā jīvati` ("he lives having killed a ram") becomes `orabbhiko`, a sheep-butcher, with `ṇika` {351}: the `ṇ` is elided {396}, the first vowel of `urabbha` takes `vuddhi`, `u` to `o` {400}, {405}, and its final `a` is elided before the affix {83}.*

#### 6.2.2.8 `hata` ("killed with")

* One killed with a net (`jālena hato`) is `jāliko`, the first of the senses
  the mention of `ādi` admits.

> jālena hato → **jāliko**

🚻⨀③(jālena) 🚹⨀①🔽(ta)(hato) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(jāliko)  
with a net / killed → killed with a net; a fisherman  
*`jālena hato` ("killed with a net") becomes `jāliko`, which the dictionary gives as a fisherman who uses a net, with `ṇika` {351} by the mention of `ādi`: the `ṇ` is elided {396} and the final `a` of `jāla` is elided before the affix {83}; the first vowel is already long and takes no `vuddhi`.*

### 6.2.3 `ṇa` after a dye for "dyed with that", and the other senses the rule lists {352}

* `ṇa` is optionally added after a word for a dye (`rāga`) in the sense "dyed
  with that" (`tena rattaṁ`, ③), and in the sense "this is his" (`tassedaṁ`,
  ⑥) and in other senses: cloth dyed with brown dye (`kasāvena rattaṁ
  vatthaṁ`) is `kāsāvaṁ`, the pig's meat (`sūkarassa idaṁ maṁsaṁ`) is
  `sokaraṁ`.
* The vutti's other senses, each with its own group below, are: near to, born
  in, come from, the month joined with its constellation, a collection, one
  whose deity that is, one who studies that, the region that is their domain,
  where a thing exists, produced by, whose dwelling, and whose lord; and such
  others are to be applied.

#### 6.2.3.1 `tena rattaṁ` ("dyed with that")

  vatthaṁ`) is `kāsāvaṁ`, the ochre robe, and so `kosumbhaṁ` ("with
  safflower"), `hāliddaṁ` ("with turmeric"), `pattaṅgaṁ` ("with red
  dye-wood"), `mañjiṭṭhaṁ` ("with madder"), `kuṅkumaṁ` ("with saffron").

### 6.2.4 `ima`, `iya` in the sense "born" and the like {353}

* `ima` and `iya` are added in the sense of `jāta` ("born") and the like: one
  born after (`pacchā jāto`) is `pacchimo`, last, and so `antimo` ("at the
  end"), `majjhimo` ("in the middle"), `purimo` ("before"), `uparimo`
  ("above"), `heṭṭhimo` ("below"), `gopphimo` ("at the ankle"); one born of
  the Bodhisatta's kind (`bodhisattajātiyā jāto`) is `bodhisattajātiyo`, and
  so `assajātiyo` ("of the horse kind"), `hatthijātiyo` ("of the elephant
  kind"), `manussajātiyo` ("of human kind").
* By the mention of `ādi` ("and the like"), the affixes `ima`, `iya` and `ika`
  are also used in the senses "appointed to that" and "he has that": `anto
  niyutto` ("set at the end") gives `antimo`, `antiyo`, `antiko`; `putto assa
  atthi, tasmiṁ vā vijjati` ("he has a son, or a son is found in him") gives
  `puttimo`, `puttiyo`, `puttiko`, and so `kappimo`, `kappiyo`, `kappiko`
  ("allowable"). By the mention of `ca` ("and"), `kiya` is used in the sense
  "appointed to that": `jātikiyo` (`jātiyaṁ niyutto`), `andhakiyo` (`andhe
  niyutto`), `jaccandhakiyo` (`jaccandhe niyutto`; `jaccandho`, "blind from
  birth", is `jātiyā andho`).

#### Example

> pacchā jāto → **pacchimo**

⏏️(pacchā) 🚹⨀①🔽(ta)(jāto) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ima)(pacchimo)  
after / born → born after, last  
*`pacchā jāto` ("born after") becomes `pacchimo`, last, with the affix `ima` {353}, which has no `ṇ` and brings no `vuddhi`: the final `ā` of `pacchā` is elided before the affix {83} and `si` becomes `o` {104}.*

4. > jātiyaṁ niyutto → **jātikiyo**

🚺⨀⑦(jātiyaṁ) 🚹⨀①🔽(ta)(niyutto) → 🚹⨀①🔽(kiya)(jātikiyo)  
in a caste / assigned → one assigned to a caste  
*`jātiyaṁ niyutto` ("assigned to a caste") becomes `jātikiyo` with `kiya` {353} by the mention of `ca`, in the sense "appointed to that", as `andhe niyutto` ("assigned to a blind man") becomes `andhakiyo` and `jaccandhe niyutto` becomes `jaccandhakiyo`: the affix begins with a consonant, so the final `i` of `jāti` stays, and `si` becomes `o` {104}.*
