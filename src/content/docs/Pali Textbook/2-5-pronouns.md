Every sentence of chapter 5 has one of these words: `idaṁ kho pana, bhikkhave,
dukkhaṁ ariyasaccaṁ`, `yo cāyaṁ`, `me`. Kaccāyana calls them `sabbanāma`
(pronouns, literally "all-names"): a word that names nothing of its own but
stands for any noun, and takes that noun's gender, number and ending. There
are twenty-seven ([Appendix L](../l-sabbanama/)); the ones on every page are `ta` (that; he,
she, it), `eta` (this), `ima` (this), `ya` (which, whoever: the relative),
`kiṁ` (who?, what?), `sabba` (all), `añña` (other), `katama` (which?), `eka`
(one), `ubha` (both), and the two without gender, `amha` (I) and `tumha` (you).

Ask of a pronoun what you ask of a noun, "what gender, number and ending?",
and one thing more: "which noun does it stand for?" Beside a noun it carries
the noun's ending, `sā majjhimā paṭipadā`, `ete ubho ante`; alone, its ending
gives its job (chapter 4) and its noun is the one named before. The endings
are those of `purisa`, `citta` and `kaññā` ([2.2](../2-2-nouns/)) with a few
of their own {161}–{177}; the tables give each form's rule.

### The forms of `ta` (that; he, she, it)

| ending | 🚹⨀ | 🚻⨀ | 🚺⨀ | 🚹⨂ | 🚻⨂ | 🚺⨂ |
| :-: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ① | `so` {174} | `taṁ` {219} | `sā` {174} | `te` {164} | `tāni` {218} | `tā` {118} |
| ② | `taṁ` {55} | `taṁ` {219} | `taṁ` {55} | `te` {107} | `tāni` {218} | `tā` {118} |
| ③ | `tena` {103} | `tena` {103} | `tāya` {111} | `tehi` {101} | `tehi` {101} | `tāhi` {55} |
| ④ ⑥ | `tassa` {61} | `tassa` {61} | `tassā` {179} | `tesaṁ` {168} | `tesaṁ` {168} | `tāsaṁ` {168} |
| ⑤ | `tasmā` {110}, `tamhā` {99} | `tasmā` {110} | `tāya` {111} | `tehi` {101} | `tehi` {101} | `tāhi` {55} |
| ⑦ | `tasmiṁ` {110}, `tamhi` {99} | `tasmiṁ` {110} | `tassaṁ` {179}, `tāyaṁ` {216} | `tesu` {101} | `tesu` {101} | `tāsu` {55} |

The ⑤ and ⑦ keep `-smā` and `-smiṁ`, never the `-ā` and `-e` of `purisā`,
`purise` {110}. An `n` may stand for the `t` anywhere {175}: `naṁ`, `nena`,
`ne`; before `-ssa`, `-smā`, `-smiṁ` the whole `ta` may shrink to `a` {176}:
`assa`, `asmā`, `asmiṁ`, which `ima` shares {177}. At the end of a compound a
pronoun goes like a noun {165}–{167}; `amu` (that yonder: `asu`, `amuṁ`) {173}
is rare in the texts.

### The other pronouns: where they differ from `ta`

| lemma | meaning | ① ⨀ 🚹, 🚻, 🚺 | shapes of its own | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| `eta` | this (just spoken of) | `eso`, `etaṁ`, `esā` | the rest as `ta` with `et-`: `etena`, `etassa`, `ete`; 🚺 `etissā` | {174}, {219}, {63} |
| `ima` | this (at hand) | `ayaṁ`, `idaṁ`, `ayaṁ` | `imaṁ`; `iminā`, `anena`; `imassa`, `assa`; `imasmiṁ`, `asmiṁ`; `ime`; `imesaṁ`, `esaṁ`; `imesu`, `esu`; 🚺 `imissā` | {172}, {129}, {171}, {177}, {170}, {63} |
| `ya` | which, who; whoever | `yo`, `yaṁ`, `yā` | as `ta` with `y-`, and no `s`-forms: `yena`, `yassa`, `ye`, `yāni`, `yesaṁ` | {104}, {219}, {220} |
| `kiṁ` | who?, what? | `ko`, `kiṁ`, `kā` | `ka-` before the endings: `kena`, `kassa`, `ke`, `kesaṁ`; with `ci` added, "some, any": `koci`, `kāci`, `kiñci`, `kenaci` | {229}, {220}, {31} |
| `sabba`, `añña`, `katama`, `eka` | all; other; which?; one | `sabbo`, `sabbaṁ`, `sabbā` | as `ya`: `sabbe`, `sabbesaṁ`, `sabbesu`; `aññe`, `katame`, `eko` | {104}, {220}, {164}, {168} |
| `ubha` | both (plural only) | `ubho`, `ubhe` | `ubhinnaṁ` ④ ⑥ | {205}, {164}, {86} |

### `amha` (I, we) and `tumha` (you)

| ending | `amha` ⨀ | `amha` ⨂ | `tumha` ⨀ | `tumha` ⨂ |
| :-: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ① | `ahaṁ` {140} | `mayaṁ` {121}, `amhe` {164} | `tvaṁ` {140} | `tumhe` {164} |
| ② | `maṁ` {143} | `amhe` {107}, `no` {147} | `taṁ` {143} | `tumhe` {107}, `vo` {147} |
| ③ ⑤ | `mayā` {145}, {270}; `me` {150} | `amhehi` {101}, `no` {151} | `tayā` {145}, {270}; `te` {150} | `tumhehi` {101}, `vo` {151} |
| ④ ⑥ | `mama` {141}, `me` {148} | `amhākaṁ` {161}, `no` {147} | `tava` {141}, `te` {148} | `tumhākaṁ` {161}, `vo` {147} |
| ⑦ | `mayi` {139} | `amhesu` {101} | `tayi` {139} | `tumhesu` {101} |

They have no gender, so no noun agrees with them: ⚧ in the symbol line. The
short `me`, `te`, `no`, `vo` never open a sentence but lean on the word before
them, `kho me`, `…saccan’ti me` {147}–{151}. Rarer: `mayhaṁ`, `tuyhaṁ` ④ ⑥
{142}, `tumhākaṁ` ② {162}, `tumhaṁ` ④ ⑥ {163}.

:::note[`te` and `taṁ`: two words each]
`te` is "they, those" (`ta` ①⨂ 🚹: `te dhammā`) and also "you, your" (`tumha`
⨀, leaning on the word before it: `kho te, sāriputta, indriyāni`); `taṁ` is
"that" (`ta`, ① 🚻 or ② of any gender) and "you" (`tumha` ②⨀). A plural noun in
① beside it, or a verb in the second person, decides.
:::

### `yo … so`: the pair that makes a clause

Pāḷi has no one word for "the one who" or "that which": a `ya` word opens the
clause and a `ta` word of the same gender, number and ending picks it up, `yo …
so`, `yaṁ … taṁ`, `yā … sā`, `ye … te`, and among the adverbs `yathā … tathā`
(as … so), `yadā … tadā` (when … then), `yato … tato`, `yattha … tattha`.
Find the `ya` word, then its `ta` partner; what lies between is the "which"
clause: `yaṁ kiñci samudayadhammaṁ, sabbaṁ taṁ nirodhadhammaṁ`. `ima` or
`eta` may stand where `ta` would: `yo cāyaṁ`, "and that which is this …".

### The adverbs made from them

A pronoun with an affix: `ta` answers, `ya` relates, `kiṁ` asks, `ima` points
to here, `sabba` says every (`sabbattha` everywhere {249}, `sabbadhi` {250},
`sabbato` from all sides {248}, `sabbadā`, `sadā` always {257}, {260}), and
the affix says where, whence, when or how. Kaccāyana names `to` and the rest
inflections {247}; the word made never changes.

| affix | `ta` | `ya` | `kiṁ` | `ima` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| where: `-ttha`, `-tra`, `-hiṁ`, `-dha` | `tattha`, `tatra`, `tahiṁ` there {249}, {253} | `yattha`, `yatra`, `yahiṁ` where {249}, {255} | `kuhiṁ`, `kva` where? {252}, {251} | `idha`, `iha` here {254} |
| from where: `-to` | `tato` from that; then {248} | `yato` from which; since {248} | `kuto` from where? {248} | `ito` from here {248} |
| when: `-dā` {256} | `tadā` then {258} | `yadā` when {257} | `kadā` when? {257} | `idāni`, `etarahi` now {259} |
| how: `-thā`, `-thaṁ` | `tathā` so, thus {398} | `yathā` as, like {398} | `kathaṁ` how? {399} | `itthaṁ` in this way {399} |

`evaṁ` (thus) and the pair `yāva … tāva` (as long as … so long: `tāvāhaṁ` in
chapter 5) are particles, not made by these rules; [2.8](../2-8-nipata/) lists
them with the rule that drops their endings {221}.

### Examples

> **Dveme**, bhikkhave, antā

⚧⨂①(dvi) *️⃣🤟🚹⨂①(ima) 🚹⨂⓪(bhikkhu) 🚹⨂①(anta)  
two / these / bhikkhave / extremes  
*"These two extremes": `dveme` is `dve` + `ime` ([2.1](../2-1-sandhi/)); `ime`, `ima` ①⨂ 🚹 {164}, agrees with `antā`.*

> yo **tassāyeva** taṇhāya asesavirāganirodho

*️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(ya) *️⃣🤟🚺⨀⑥(ta) ⏏️(eva) 🚺⨀⑥(taṇhā) 🚹⨀①🆎(asesa+virāga+nirodha)  
which / of that / very / craving / remainderless fading away and cessation  
*"Which is the … cessation of that very craving": `tassāyeva` is `tassā` + `eva`; `tassā`, `ta` ⑥⨀ 🚺 {179}, goes with `taṇhāya`, and `yo` {104} with `nirodho`.*

> ‘**Taṁ** kho **panidaṁ** dukkhaṁ ariyasaccaṁ pariññeyyan’ti **me**

*️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(ta) ⏏️(kho) ⏏️(pana) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(ima) 🚻⨀①(dukkha) 🚻⨀①🆎(ariya+sacca) 🚻⨀①🔽(ṇya)(pariññeyya) ⏏️(iti) *️⃣👆⚧⨀④(ahaṁ)  
that / indeed / now / this / suffering / realisation / to be fully understood / thus / for me  
*"This realisation of suffering should be fully understood: thus for me …": `taṁ` {219} and `idaṁ` {129}, the neuter ① of `ta` and `ima`, like `ariyasaccaṁ`; `me`, `amha` ④⨀ {148}, leans on `ti`.*

> **yaṁ kiñci** samudayadhammaṁ **sabbaṁ taṁ** nirodhadhamman”ti

*️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(ya) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(kaci) 🚻⨀①🆎(samudaya+dhamma) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(sabba) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(ta) 🚻⨀①🆎(nirodha+dhamma) ⏏️(iti)  
whatever / anything / subject to origination / all / that / subject to cessation / (end quote)  
*"Whatever is subject to origination, all that is subject to cessation": the pair `yaṁ … taṁ`, neuter ① {219}; `kiñci` is `kiṁ` + `ci`, "any" {31}; `sabbaṁ` {219} agrees with `taṁ`.*

### Exercises

Each is from the texts of chapters 3, 5 and 11. For each pronoun give its lemma,
gender, number and ending, and the noun it agrees with or stands for; for an
adverb, the pronoun and the affix.

:::tip[Hint]
You may find the [Digital Pali Dictionary](https://digitalpalidictionary.github.io/)
useful for looking up words, as well as doing the sandhi and compound word
deconstruction.
:::

1. Tatra kho bhagavā pañcavaggiye bhikkhū āmantesi.
2. Ete kho, bhikkhave, ubho ante anupagamma.
3. Katamā ca sā, bhikkhave, majjhimā paṭipadā?
4. Yo cāyaṁ kāmesu kāmasukhallikānuyogo.
5. imesu catūsu ariyasaccesu
6. neva tāvāhaṁ, bhikkhave
7. Yato ca kho me, bhikkhave
8. Tassa te saṅkhārā ca vihārā ca.

### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > Tatra kho bhagavā pañcavaggiye bhikkhū āmantesi.

⏏️(tatra) ⏏️(kho) 🚹⨀①(bhagavant) 🚹⨂②🆎(pañca+vaggiya) 🚹⨂②(bhikkhu) 🟢⨀🤟⏮(āmanteti)  
there / indeed / the Bhagavā / of the group of five / bhikkhū / addressed  
*There the Bhagavā addressed the group of five bhikkhū.*  
`tatra`: `ta` + `tra`, "there", a place in the sense of ⑦ {249}; it never changes.

2. > Ete kho, bhikkhave, ubho ante anupagamma.

*️⃣🤟🚹⨂②(eta) ⏏️(kho) 🚹⨂⓪(bhikkhu) *️⃣🤟🚹⨂②(ubha) 🚹⨂②(anta) 🔽(ya)(anupagacchati)  
these / indeed / bhikkhave / both / extremes / not approaching  
*Without veering towards either of these two extremes, bhikkhave, …*  
`ete`, `eta` ②⨂ 🚹 {107}, and `ubho`, `ubha` ②⨂ {205}, both agree with `ante`.

3. > Katamā ca sā, bhikkhave, majjhimā paṭipadā?

*️⃣🤟🚺⨀①(katama) ⏏️(ca) *️⃣🤟🚺⨀①(ta) 🚹⨂⓪(bhikkhu) 🚺⨀①(majjhima) 🚺⨀①(paṭipadā)  
which / and / that / bhikkhave / middle / way  
*And what, bhikkhave, is that middle way?*  
`katamā` and `sā` {174}: 🚺⨀① like `paṭipadā`; the question word is a pronoun and agrees like one.

4. > Yo cāyaṁ kāmesu kāmasukhallikānuyogo.

*️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(ya) ⏏️(ca) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(ima) 🚹⨂⑦(kāma) 🚹⨀①🆎(kāmasukhallikā+anuyoga)  
which / and / this / in sensual pleasures / pursuit of sensual happiness  
*That which is this pursuit of sensual happiness in sensual pleasures …*  
`cāyaṁ` is `ca` + `ayaṁ` ([2.1](../2-1-sandhi/)): `yo` {104} and `ayaṁ` {172}, both 🚹⨀① with `kāmasukhallikānuyogo`; `yo` opens the clause answering `katame dve`.

5. > imesu catūsu ariyasaccesu

*️⃣🤟🚻⨂⑦(ima) 🚻⨂⑦(catu) 🚻⨂⑦🆎(ariya+sacca)  
in these / four / realisations  
*in these four realisations*  
`imesu`, `ima` ⑦⨂ 🚻 {170}, agreeing with `ariyasaccesu`; `esu` would be the same word {170}.

6. > neva tāvāhaṁ, bhikkhave

⏏️(na) ⏏️(eva) ⏏️(tāva) *️⃣👆⚧⨀①(ahaṁ) 🚹⨂⓪(bhikkhu)  
not / indeed / so long / I / bhikkhave  
*so long, bhikkhave, I did not …*  
`tāvāhaṁ` is `tāva` + `ahaṁ` ([2.1](../2-1-sandhi/)): `ahaṁ`, `amha` ①⨀ {140}, the subject of `paccaññāsiṁ`; `tāva` answers the `yāva` before it.

7. > Yato ca kho me, bhikkhave

⏏️(yato) ⏏️(ca) ⏏️(kho) *️⃣👆⚧⨀⑥(ahaṁ) 🚹⨂⓪(bhikkhu)  
but when / and / indeed / my / bhikkhave  
*But when, bhikkhave, my …*  
`yato`: `ya` + `to`, "from which", here "when", in the sense of ⑤ {248}; `me`, `amha` ⑥⨀ {148}, "my", leans on `kho`.

8. > Tassa te saṅkhārā ca vihārā ca.

🚹⑥⨀(ta) 🚹①⨂(ta) 🚹①⨂(saṅkhāra) 🔼(ca) 🚹①⨂(vihāra) 🔼(ca)  
of that / those / mental constructions / and / state of mind / and  
*Regarding that, those mental constructions and state of mind …*  
`tassa`, `ta` ⑥⨀ 🚹 {61}, stands for the bhikkhu of the sentence before; `te`, `ta` ①⨂ 🚹 {164}, agrees with `saṅkhārā` and `vihārā`.
