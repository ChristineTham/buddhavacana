The usage examples provided here are not exhaustive, other usages are possible.

A `samāsa` ("compound") is two or more nouns joined into one word {316}. This
section covers the one rule that names the `bahubbīhi` ("attributive
compound"), {328}: nouns compounded in the sense of another word, a word that
stands outside the compound and is qualified by it. `chinnahattho` ("whose hand
is cut off") names neither the cutting nor the hand but the man, `puriso`, who
is outside the compound.

### 4.6.1 Nouns compounded in the sense of another word {328}

* When two or more nouns are compounded in the sense of another word
  (`aññapadattha`), the compound is named `bahubbīhi` ("attributive compound").
* The compound counts as a noun {601} and takes the gender, number and
  `vibhatti` ending of the word it qualifies: `chinnahattho` is 🚹⨀① because
  `puriso` is, with `si` → `o` {104}.

The vutti sets out each example as an analysis phrase: the members as separate
inflected words, the other word beside them (`imaṁ saṅghārāmaṁ`, `yassa rañño`
…) and `so` ("that one"), which the compound then names — the vutti's `soyaṁ`
is `so ayaṁ`, "that one is this" — and after the comma the noun the compound
qualifies (`saṅghārāmo`, `rājā`). The members' endings are elided {317} and the
compound takes an ending of its own, as the index page of this chapter sets
out. The vutti shows the other word in each ending from ② to ⑦ in turn, then
compounds with a standard of comparison (`iva`, `viya`, "like") first, and then
long compounds that it analyses member by member, naming the kinds of compound
nested (`gabbha`) inside each and classing each as a `bahubbīhi` of members
with the same referent (`tulyādhikaraṇa`) or with different referents
(`bhinnādhikaraṇa`); the groups below follow that order. The vutti ends by
asking what the term is for: it is used in {167}, where a `bahubbīhi` that ends
in a pronoun (`piyapubbā`, "whose former [spouse] was dear") does not take the
pronominal endings.

#### 4.6.1.1 The other word in the ② ending

* The thing named is the object of the action in the analysis phrase:
  `āgatā samaṇā imaṁ saṅghārāmaṁ`, "ascetics have come to this monastery",
  compounded in the sense of `imaṁ saṅghārāmaṁ` ②, names the monastery.

> āgatā samaṇā imaṁ saṅghārāmaṁ so → **āgatasamaṇo** saṅghārāmo

🚹⨂①(🔽(ta)(āgatā) samaṇā) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀②(imaṁ) 🚹⨀②🆎(saṅgha+ārāma) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(āgata+samaṇa) 🚹⨀①🆎(saṅgha+ārāma)  
come / ascetics / to this / monastery / that [one] → to which ascetics have come / monastery  
*`āgatā samaṇā imaṁ saṅghārāmaṁ` ("ascetics have come to this monastery") becomes `āgatasamaṇo`, a `bahubbīhi` qualifying `saṅghārāmo` ("monastery"), the other word in the ② ending.*

#### 4.6.1.2 The other word in the ③ ending

* The thing named is the agent of the action: `jitāni indriyāni anena
  samaṇena`, "the senses have been conquered by this ascetic", compounded in
  the sense of `anena samaṇena` ③, names the ascetic.

> jitāni indriyāni anena samaṇena so → **jitindriyo** samaṇo

🚻⨂①(🔽(ta)(jitāni) indriyāni) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀③(anena) 🚹⨀③(samaṇena) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(jita+indriya) 🚹⨀①(samaṇo)  
conquered / senses / by this / by the ascetic / that [one] → by whom the senses have been conquered / ascetic  
*`jitāni indriyāni anena samaṇena` ("the senses have been conquered by this ascetic") becomes `jitindriyo`, a `bahubbīhi` qualifying `samaṇo` ("ascetic"), the other word in the ③ ending; `jita` + `indriya` joins with the elision of `a` before `i` {12}.*

#### 4.6.1.3 The other word in the ④ ending

* The thing named is the recipient: `dinno suṅko yassa rañño`, "tax is paid to
  which king", compounded in the sense of `yassa rañño` ④, names the king.

> dinno suṅko yassa rañño so → **dinnasuṅko** rājā

🚹⨀①(🔽(ta)(dinno) suṅko) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀④(yassa) 🚹⨀④(rañño) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(dinna+suṅka) 🚹⨀①(rājā)  
paid / tax / to whom / to the king / that [one] → to whom tax is paid / king  
*`dinno suṅko yassa rañño` ("tax is paid to which king") becomes `dinnasuṅko`, a `bahubbīhi` qualifying `rājā` ("king"), the other word in the ④ ending.*

#### 4.6.1.4 The other word in the ⑤ ending

* The thing named is the point of departure: `niggatā janā asmā gāmā`, "the
  people have gone out from this village", compounded in the sense of `asmā
  gāmā` ⑤, names the village.

> niggatā janā asmā gāmā so → **niggatajano** gāmo

🚹⨂①(🔽(ta)(niggatā) janā) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑤(asmā) 🚹⨀⑤(gāmā) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(niggata+jana) 🚹⨀①(gāmo)  
gone out / people / from this / from the village / that [one] → from which the people have gone out / village  
*`niggatā janā asmā gāmā` ("the people have gone out from this village") becomes `niggatajano`, a `bahubbīhi` qualifying `gāmo` ("village"), the other word in the ⑤ ending.*

#### 4.6.1.5 The other word in the ⑥ ending

* The thing named is the possessor: `chinno hattho yassa purisassa`, "the hand
  of which man is cut off", compounded in the sense of `yassa purisassa` ⑥,
  names the man. Most of the vutti's later examples are of this kind (`yassa`,
  `yassā`, `yesaṁ`).

> chinno hattho yassa purisassa so → **chinnahattho** puriso

🚹⨀①(🔽(ta)(chinno) hattho) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) 🚹⨀⑥(purisassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(chinna+hattha) 🚹⨀①(puriso)  
cut off / hand / of whom / of the man / that [one] → whose hand is cut off / man  
*`chinno hattho yassa purisassa` ("the hand of which man is cut off") becomes `chinnahattho`, a `bahubbīhi` qualifying `puriso` ("man"), the other word in the ⑥ ending.*

#### 4.6.1.6 The other word in the ⑦ ending

* The thing named is the location: `sampannāni sassāni yasmiṁ janapade`, "the
  crops have ripened in which country", compounded in the sense of `yasmiṁ
  janapade` ⑦, names the country.

> sampannāni sassāni yasmiṁ janapade so → **sampannasasso** janapado

🚻⨂①(🔽(ta)(sampannāni) sassāni) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑦(yasmiṁ) 🚹⨀⑦🆎(jana+pada) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(sampanna+sassa) 🚹⨀①🆎(jana+pada)  
ripened / crops / in which / in the country / that [one] → in which the crops have ripened / country  
*`sampannāni sassāni yasmiṁ janapade` ("the crops have ripened in which country") becomes `sampannasasso`, a `bahubbīhi` qualifying `janapado` ("country"), the other word in the ⑦ ending.*

#### 4.6.1.7 A standard of comparison first (`iva`, `viya`)

* The first member may be a standard of comparison (`upamāna`), read with `iva`
  or `viya` ("like"). The vutti first makes `nigrodhassa parimaṇḍalo` ("the
  round shape of a banyan") into the `tappurisa` `nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo` {327},
  and then compounds `nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo iva parimaṇḍalo` ("rounded like the
  round shape of a banyan") in the sense of the other word.
* The other word may stand in the ① ending, `yo rājakumāro` ("the prince who
  is rounded like …"), or in the ⑥, `yassa rājakumārassa` ("the prince whose
  rounded [form] is like …"); the vutti gives both readings (`atha vā`, "or
  else").

> nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo iva parimaṇḍalo yo rājakumāro so → **nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo** rājakumāro

🚹⨀①🆎(nigrodha+parimaṇḍala) ⏏️(iva) 🚹⨀①(parimaṇḍalo) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(yo) 🚹⨀①🆎(rāja+kumāra) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(nigrodha+parimaṇḍala) 🚹⨀①🆎(rāja+kumāra)  
the round shape of a banyan / like / rounded / who / prince / that [one] → rounded like a banyan / prince  
*`nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo iva parimaṇḍalo yo rājakumāro` ("the prince who is rounded like the round shape of a banyan") becomes `nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo`, a `bahubbīhi` qualifying `rājakumāro` ("prince"), with a standard of comparison first and the other word in the ① ending.*

> nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo iva parimaṇḍalo yassa rājakumārassa so → **nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo** rājakumāro

🚹⨀①🆎(nigrodha+parimaṇḍala) ⏏️(iva) 🚹⨀①(parimaṇḍalo) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) 🚹⨀⑥🆎(rāja+kumāra) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(nigrodha+parimaṇḍala) 🚹⨀①🆎(rāja+kumāra)  
the round shape of a banyan / like / rounded / of whom / of the prince / that [one] → rounded like a banyan / prince  
*Or `nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo iva parimaṇḍalo yassa rājakumārassa` ("the prince whose rounded [form] is like the round shape of a banyan"): the same `bahubbīhi`, read with the other word in the ⑥ ending.*

The compound is one of the thirty-two marks of a great man ([7D 1.3,
Dvattiṁsamahāpurisalakkhaṇā](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/7D/1/1.3#70)):

> ayañhi, deva, kumāro **nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo**

*️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(ayaṁ) ⏏️(hi) 🚹⨀⓪(deva) 🚹⨀①(kumāro) 🚹⨀①🆎(nigrodha+parimaṇḍala)  
this / indeed / sire / prince / rounded like a banyan  
*This prince, sire, is rounded like a banyan.*

#### 4.6.1.8 Two members: a numeral or an adjective first

* The epithets of the Buddha that the vutti gives next are `bahubbīhi` of two
  members with the other word in the ⑥ ending, `yassa`: a numeral with its
  noun, `catasso disā yassa` ("whose are the four directions"), `pañca cakkhūni
  yassa` ("whose are the five eyes"), `dasa balāni yassa` ("whose are the ten
  powers"), or an adjective with its noun, `anantaṁ ñāṇaṁ yassa` ("whose
  knowledge is without end").
* In the phrase the numeral takes the gender and number of its noun (`catasso
  disā` 🚺⨂①, `dasa balāni` 🚻⨂①); the compound takes those of the word it
  qualifies, `bhagavā` or `tathāgato` 🚹⨀①.

> dasa balāni yassa so → **dasabalo** bhagavā

🚻⨂①(dasa balāni) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(dasa+bala) 🚹⨀①(bhagavā)  
ten / powers / of whom / that [one] → who has the ten powers / the Blessed One  
*`dasa balāni yassa` ("whose are the ten powers") becomes `dasabalo`, a `bahubbīhi` of two members qualifying `bhagavā` ("the Blessed One"), the other word in the ⑥ ending.*

The epithet stands in a verse of the Vinaya ([3V 1.13,
Bimbisārasamāgamakathā](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/3V/1/1.13#215)):

> dasavāso **dasabalo**, dasadhammavidū dasabhi cupeto

🚹⨀①🆎(dasa+vāsa) 🚹⨀①🆎(dasa+bala) 🚹⨀①🆎(dasa+dhamma+vidū) ⚧⨂③(dasabhi) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(🔽(ta)(upeto))  
following the ten ways of living / who has the ten powers / who knows the ten truths / with ten / and / endowed  
*Following the ten ways of living, having the ten powers, knowing the ten truths, and endowed with ten.*

#### 4.6.1.9 Three members and more: compounds nested inside

* A `bahubbīhi` may have three members or more. The vutti then builds it up
  step by step, naming the kinds of compound nested (`gabbha`) inside it, a
  `dvanda`, a `kammadhāraya` or a `tappurisa`, before the whole is compounded
  in the sense of the other word: `na mitaṁ amitaṁ` ("not measured:
  measureless"), `ghanaṁ eva sarīraṁ ghanasarīraṁ` ("a body that is solid", a
  `kammadhāraya`), `amitaṁ ghanasarīraṁ yassa tathāgatassa so` ("the Tathāgata
  whose solid body is measureless").

> amitaṁ ghanasarīraṁ yassa tathāgatassa so → **amitaghanasarīro** tathāgato

🚻⨀①(amitaṁ) 🚻⨀①🆎(ghana+sarīra) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) 🚹⨀⑥(tathāgatassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(amita+ghana+sarīra) 🚹⨀①(tathāgato)  
measureless / solid body / of whom / of the Tathāgata / that [one] → whose solid body is measureless / the Tathāgata  
*`amitaṁ ghanasarīraṁ yassa tathāgatassa` ("the Tathāgata whose solid body is measureless") becomes `amitaghanasarīro`, a `bahubbīhi` of three members qualifying `tathāgato`, with the `kammadhāraya` `ghanasarīraṁ` nested inside it and the other word in the ⑥ ending.*

> pīṇā urakkhaṁsabāhavo yassa bhagavato so → **pīṇorakkhaṁsabāhu** bhagavā

🚹⨂①(pīṇā) 🚹⨂①🆎(ura+akkha+aṁsa+bāhu) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) 🚹⨀⑥(bhagavato) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(pīṇa+ura+akkha+aṁsa+bāhu) 🚹⨀①(bhagavā)  
full / chest, collar-bones, shoulders and arms / of whom / of the Blessed One / that [one] → whose chest, collar-bones, shoulders and arms are full / the Blessed One  
*`pīṇā urakkhaṁsabāhavo yassa bhagavato` ("the Blessed One whose chest, collar-bones, shoulders and arms are full") becomes `pīṇorakkhaṁsabāhu`, a `bahubbīhi` of five members qualifying `bhagavā`, with the `dvanda` `urakkhaṁsabāhavo` (`uro ca akkhaṁ ca aṁso ca bāhu ca`) nested inside it; `pīṇa` + `ura` joins as `pīṇora` by sandhi, and the compound ends in `u` like `bāhu`.*

#### 4.6.1.10 The same referent and different referents (`tulyādhikaraṇa`, `bhinnādhikaraṇa`)

* The vutti classes each of its long compounds as a `tulyādhikaraṇa` ("with
  the same referent") `bahubbīhi`, whose members describe the one thing and so
  stand in the same ending, or a `bhinnādhikaraṇa` ("with different
  referents") one, whose members describe different things and stand in
  different endings.
* Several compounds it reads both ways (`atha vā`, "or else"). In the first
  reading the last member is compounded last, `nānādumapatitapupphavāsitā
  sānū` ("plateaus scented with flowers fallen from trees of many kinds"), so
  that the members agree. In the second a compound is formed first, `vāsitā
  sānū vāsitasānu` ("scented plateaus"), which is a compound even though it
  depends on another word, because the sense is understood (`sāpekkhatte
  satipi gamakattā samāso`), and `nānādumapatitapupphehi` ③ then stands against
  it.

> nānādumapatitapupphavāsitā sānū yassa pabbatarājassa so → **nānādumapatitapupphavāsitasānu** pabbatarājā

🚺⨂①🆎(nānā+duma+patita+puppha+vāsita) 🚺⨂①(sānū) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) 🚹⨀⑥🆎(pabbata+rāja) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(nānā+duma+patita+puppha+vāsita+sānu) 🚹⨀①🆎(pabbata+rāja)  
scented with flowers fallen from trees of many kinds / plateaus / of whom / of the king of mountains / that [one] → whose plateaus are scented with flowers fallen from trees of many kinds / king of mountains  
*`nānādumapatitapupphavāsitā sānū yassa pabbatarājassa` ("the king of mountains whose plateaus are scented with flowers fallen from trees of many kinds") becomes `nānādumapatitapupphavāsitasānu`, a `tulyādhikaraṇa` `bahubbīhi` qualifying `pabbatarājā`, `vāsitā` and `sānū` agreeing; nested inside it are the `kammadhāraya` `nānādumā` ("trees of many kinds") and `nānādumapatitapupphāni` ("flowers fallen from trees of many kinds"), and the `tappurisa` `nānādumapatitāni` ("fallen from trees of many kinds") and `nānādumapatitapupphavāsitā` ("scented with flowers fallen from trees of many kinds").*

> nānādumapatitapupphehi vāsitasānū yassa pabbatarājassa so → **nānādumapatitapupphavāsitasānu** pabbatarājā

🚻⨂③🆎(nānā+duma+patita+puppha) 🚺⨂①🆎(vāsita+sānu) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) 🚹⨀⑥🆎(pabbata+rāja) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(nānā+duma+patita+puppha+vāsita+sānu) 🚹⨀①🆎(pabbata+rāja)  
with flowers fallen from trees of many kinds / scented plateaus / of whom / of the king of mountains / that [one] → whose plateaus are scented with flowers fallen from trees of many kinds / king of mountains  
*Or `nānādumapatitapupphehi vāsitasānū yassa pabbatarājassa` ("the king of mountains whose plateaus are scented, with flowers fallen from trees of many kinds"): the same compound read as a `bhinnādhikaraṇa` `bahubbīhi`, `pupphehi` in the ③ ending and `vāsitasānū` in the ① describing different things, with the `kammadhāraya` `vāsitasānū` formed first.*

#### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > cakkhubhūto iva bhūto yo bhagavā so → **cakkhubhūto** bhagavā

🚹⨀①🆎(cakkhu+bhūta) ⏏️(iva) 🚹⨀①(🔽(ta)(bhūto)) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(yo) 🚹⨀①(bhagavā) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(cakkhu+bhūta) 🚹⨀①(bhagavā)  
become an eye / like / become / who / the Blessed One / that [one] → who is as an eye / the Blessed One  
*`cakkhubhūto iva bhūto yo bhagavā` ("the Blessed One who is as one become an eye") becomes `cakkhubhūto`, a `bahubbīhi` with a standard of comparison first, qualifying `bhagavā`, the other word in the ① ending; `cakkhubhūto` in the phrase is the `tappurisa` `cakkhuno bhūto` ("become an eye").*

2. > suvaṇṇavaṇṇo viya vaṇṇo yassa bhagavato so → **suvaṇṇavaṇṇo** bhagavā

🚹⨀①🆎(suvaṇṇa+vaṇṇa) ⏏️(viya) 🚹⨀①(vaṇṇo) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) 🚹⨀⑥(bhagavato) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(suvaṇṇa+vaṇṇa) 🚹⨀①(bhagavā)  
the colour of gold / like / colour / of whom / of the Blessed One / that [one] → golden-coloured / the Blessed One  
*`suvaṇṇavaṇṇo viya vaṇṇo yassa bhagavato` ("the Blessed One whose colour is like the colour of gold") becomes `suvaṇṇavaṇṇo`, a `bahubbīhi` with a standard of comparison first, qualifying `bhagavā`, the other word in the ⑥ ending; `suvaṇṇavaṇṇo` in the phrase is the `tappurisa` `suvaṇṇassa vaṇṇo` ("the colour of gold").*

3. > brahmassaro viya saro yassa bhagavato so → **brahmassaro** bhagavā

🚹⨀①🆎(brahma+sara) ⏏️(viya) 🚹⨀①(saro) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) 🚹⨀⑥(bhagavato) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(brahma+sara) 🚹⨀①(bhagavā)  
the voice of Brahmā / like / voice / of whom / of the Blessed One / that [one] → with the voice of Brahmā / the Blessed One  
*`brahmassaro viya saro yassa bhagavato` ("the Blessed One whose voice is like the voice of Brahmā") becomes `brahmassaro`, a `bahubbīhi` with a standard of comparison first, qualifying `bhagavā`, the other word in the ⑥ ending; `brahmassaro` in the phrase is the `tappurisa` `brahmuno saro` ("the voice of Brahmā").*

4. > catasso disā yassa so → **catuddiso** bhagavā

🚺⨂①(catasso disā) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(catu+disā) 🚹⨀①(bhagavā)  
four / directions / of whom / that [one] → to whom the four directions [are open] / the Blessed One  
*`catasso disā yassa` ("whose are the four directions") becomes `catuddiso`, a `bahubbīhi` of two members with a numeral first, qualifying `bhagavā`, the other word in the ⑥ ending.*

5. > pañca cakkhūni yassa tathāgatassa so → **pañcacakkhu** tathāgato

🚻⨂①(pañca cakkhūni) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) 🚹⨀⑥(tathāgatassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(pañca+cakkhu) 🚹⨀①(tathāgato)  
five / eyes / of whom / of the Tathāgata / that [one] → who has the five eyes / the Tathāgata  
*`pañca cakkhūni yassa tathāgatassa` ("the Tathāgata whose are the five eyes") becomes `pañcacakkhu`, a `bahubbīhi` of two members with a numeral first, qualifying `tathāgato`, the other word in the ⑥ ending; the compound ends in `u` like `cakkhu` and has the masculine ① form of a lemma in `u`.*

6. > anantaṁ ñāṇaṁ yassa tathāgatassa so → **anantañāṇo** tathāgato

🚻⨀①🆎(na+anta) 🚻⨀①(ñāṇaṁ) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) 🚹⨀⑥(tathāgatassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(ananta+ñāṇa) 🚹⨀①(tathāgato)  
without end / knowledge / of whom / of the Tathāgata / that [one] → whose knowledge is without end / the Tathāgata  
*`anantaṁ ñāṇaṁ yassa tathāgatassa` ("the Tathāgata whose knowledge is without end") becomes `anantañāṇo`, a `bahubbīhi` of two members with an adjective first, qualifying `tathāgato`, the other word in the ⑥ ending; `ananta` is itself `nassa anto` ("its end is not"), with `a` for `na` {333}.*

7. > amitabalaparakkamā pattā yena so → **amitabalaparakkamapatto** bhagavā

🚹⨂①🆎(amita+bala+parakkama) 🚹⨂①(🔽(ta)(pattā)) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀③(yena) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(amita+bala+parakkama+patta) 🚹⨀①(bhagavā)  
measureless strength and striving / attained / by whom / that [one] → by whom measureless strength and striving have been attained / the Blessed One  
*`amitabalaparakkamā pattā yena` ("by whom measureless strength and striving have been attained") becomes `amitabalaparakkamapatto`, a `tulyādhikaraṇa` `bahubbīhi` qualifying `bhagavā`, the other word in the ③ ending; nested inside it are the `dvanda` `balaparakkamā` (`balañca parakkamo ca`) and the `kammadhāraya` `amitabalaparakkamā` (`amitā eva balaparakkamā`), `amitā` itself being `na mitā`.*

8. > pīṇā gaṇḍavadanathanūrujaghanā yassā sā → **pīṇagaṇḍavadanathanūrujaghanā**

🚻⨂①(pīṇā) 🚻⨂①🆎(gaṇḍa+vadana+thana+ūru+jaghana) *️⃣🤟🚺⨀⑥(yassā) *️⃣🤟🚺⨀①(sā) → 🚺⨀①🆎(pīṇa+gaṇḍa+vadana+thana+ūru+jaghana)  
full / cheeks, face, breasts, thighs and hips / of whom / that [one] → whose cheeks, face, breasts, thighs and hips are full  
*`pīṇā gaṇḍavadanathanūrujaghanā yassā sā` ("she whose cheeks, face, breasts, thighs and hips are full") becomes `pīṇagaṇḍavadanathanūrujaghanā`, a `tulyādhikaraṇa` `bahubbīhi` with the `dvanda` `gaṇḍavadanathanūrujaghanā` (`gaṇḍo ca vadanañca thano ca ūru ca jaghanañca`) nested inside it; the vutti names no noun, but `yassā sā` shows that the one qualified is feminine, and the compound takes the feminine ① ending `ā`.*

9. > amitā balaparakkamajutiyo yassa so → **amitabalaparakkamajuti**

🚺⨂①(amitā) 🚺⨂①🆎(bala+parakkama+juti) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(amita+bala+parakkama+juti)  
measureless / strength, striving and splendour / of whom / that [one] → whose strength, striving and splendour are measureless  
*`amitā balaparakkamajutiyo yassa so` ("he whose strength, striving and splendour are measureless") becomes `amitabalaparakkamajuti`, a `tulyādhikaraṇa` `bahubbīhi` with the `kammadhāraya` `amitā` (`na mitā`) and the `dvanda` `balaparakkamajutiyo` (`balañca parakkamo ca juti ca`) nested inside it; the vutti names no noun, and `yassa so` shows that the one qualified is masculine, so the compound ends in `i` like `juti` with the masculine ① form of a lemma in `i`.*
