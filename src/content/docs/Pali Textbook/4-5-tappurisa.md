The usage examples provided here are not exhaustive, other usages are possible.

A `samāsa` ("compound") is two or more nouns whose meanings are connected made
into one word {316}. This section covers the `tappurisa` ("determinative
compound"), in which a word in the ② ending or a later one is compounded with
the word that follows it {327}, and the rule that counts the `kammadhāraya`
("appositional compound") and the `digu` ("numeral compound") as `tappurisa`
as well {326}.

### 4.5.1 A word in the ② ending or a later one with the word that follows {327}

* When a word in the ② ending, or in any later ending (③, ④, ⑤, ⑥ or ⑦), is
  compounded with the word that follows it, the compound is named `tappurisa`
  ("determinative compound").
* The first member keeps the sense of its ending, and the `tappurisa` is named
  after it: `rājaputto` ("the king's son"), from `rañño putto`, is a
  `tappurisa` of the ⑥ ending.

The vutti gives, for each ending from ② to ⑦, the analysis phrase and then the
compound. The ending of the first member is the one that the Kārakakappa
(chapter [2](../2-0-karaka/)) assigns to it; when the two words are joined it
is elided {317}, and the compound takes a `vibhatti` ending of its own, in
these examples the ① ending.

#### 4.5.1.1 The ② ending

* The first member is in the ② ending, the object {297}, section
  [2.5](../2-5-accusative/), or a time through which something goes on {298}.

> bhūmiṁ gato → **bhūmigato**

🚺⨀②(bhūmiṁ) 🚹⨀①(🔽(ta)(gato)) → 🚹⨀①🆎(bhūmi+gata)  
to the ground / gone → gone to the ground  
*`bhūmiṁ gato` ("gone to the ground") becomes `bhūmigato`, a `tappurisa` of the ② ending.*

The compound in the Tipiṭaka (Vinaya Piṭaka:
[5V 7.2, Dukavāra](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/5V/7/7.2#1446)):

> atthāpatti **bhūmigato** āpajjati, no vehāsagato

🟢⨀🤟▶️(atthi) 🚺⨀①(āpatti) 🚹⨀①🆎(bhūmi+gata) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(āpajjati) ⏏️(no) 🚹⨀①🆎(vehāsa+gata)  
there is / an offence / one on the ground / commits / not / one in the air  
*There is an offence [that] one on the ground commits, not one in the air.*

#### 4.5.1.2 The ③ ending

* The first member is in the ③ ending, the instrument {286} or the agent of a
  passive participle {288}, section [2.4](../2-4-instrumental/).

> sallena viddho → **sallaviddho**

🚹⨀③(sallena) 🚹⨀①(🔽(ta)(viddho)) → 🚹⨀①🆎(salla+viddha)  
by an arrow / pierced → pierced by an arrow  
*`sallena viddho` ("pierced by an arrow") becomes `sallaviddho`, a `tappurisa` of the ③ ending, the ③ of the instrument {286}.*

The compound in the Tipiṭaka (Vessantarajātaka:
[23J 22.1.10.9, Maddīpabba](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/23J/22/22.1/22.1.10/22.1.10.9#3576)):

> idaṁ tato dukkhataraṁ, **sallaviddho** yathā vaṇo

*️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(idaṁ) ⏏️(tato) 🚻⨀①(dukkhataraṁ) 🚹⨀①🆎(salla+viddha) ⏏️(yathā) 🚹⨀①(vaṇo)  
this / than that / more painful / pierced by an arrow / like / a wound  
*This is more painful than that, like a wound pierced by an arrow.*

#### 4.5.1.3 The ④ ending

* The first member is in the ④ ending, "for" {293}, section
  [2.2](../2-2-dative/); the `sa` ending of `kathinassa` is read as ④.

> kathinassa dussaṁ → **kathinadussaṁ**

🚻⨀④(kathinassa) 🚻⨀①(dussaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(kathina+dussa)  
for the kathina (robe ceremony) / cloth → cloth for the kathina  
*`kathinassa dussaṁ` ("cloth for the `kathina`") becomes `kathinadussaṁ`, a `tappurisa` of the ④ ending.*

#### 4.5.1.4 The ⑤ ending

* The first member is in the ⑤ ending, "from" {295}, section
  [2.1](../2-1-ablative/): the source from which fear arises {271}, or the
  thing from which one has gone away.

> corā bhayaṁ → **corabhayaṁ**

🚹⨀⑤(corā) 🚻⨀①(bhayaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(cora+bhaya)  
from a thief / fear → fear of thieves  
*`corā bhayaṁ` ("fear from a thief") becomes `corabhayaṁ`, a `tappurisa` of the ⑤ ending; the thief is the source from which the fear arises {271}.*

The compound in the Tipiṭaka (Aṅguttara Nikāya:
[15A4 3.2.10, Dutiyabhayasutta](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/15A4/3/3.2/3.2.10#778)):

> aggibhayaṁ, udakabhayaṁ, rājabhayaṁ, **corabhayaṁ**

🚻⨀①🆎(aggi+bhaya) 🚻⨀①🆎(udaka+bhaya) 🚻⨀①🆎(rāja+bhaya) 🚻⨀①🆎(cora+bhaya)  
fear of fire / fear of water / fear of kings / fear of thieves  
*Fear of fire, fear of water, fear of kings, fear of thieves.*

#### 4.5.1.5 The ⑥ ending

* The first member is in the ⑥ ending, the owner {301}, section
  [2.7](../2-7-genitive/).

> rañño putto → **rājaputto**

🚹⨀⑥(rañño) 🚹⨀①(putto) → 🚹⨀①🆎(rāja+putta)  
of the king / son → the king's son  
*`rañño putto` ("the son of the king") becomes `rājaputto`, a `tappurisa` of the ⑥ ending.*

#### 4.5.1.6 The ⑦ ending

* The first member is in the ⑦ ending, the location {302}, section
  [2.3](../2-3-locative/).

> rūpe saññā → **rūpasaññā**

🚻⨀⑦(rūpe) 🚺⨀①(saññā) → 🚺⨀①🆎(rūpa+saññā)  
in form / perception → perception of form  
*`rūpe saññā` ("perception in [regard to] form") becomes `rūpasaññā`, a `tappurisa` of the ⑦ ending.*

The compound in the Tipiṭaka (Dīgha Nikāya 9, Poṭṭhapādasutta:
[6D 9.1.2, Sahetukasaññuppādanirodhakathā](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/6D/9/9.1/9.1.2#701)):

> tassa yā purimā **rūpasaññā**, sā nirujjhati

*️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(tassa) *️⃣🤟🚺⨀①(yā) 🚺⨀①(purimā) 🚺⨀①🆎(rūpa+saññā) *️⃣🤟🚺⨀①(sā) 🔵⨀🤟▶️(nirujjhati)  
his / which / former / perception of form / that / ceases  
*Whatever former perception of form he had, that ceases.*

#### Exercises

Resolve the following compounds into their members, name the type of compound,
and give the meaning. Look up the words in a dictionary and take note of the
`vibhatti` endings of the members.

You may find the [Digital Pali Dictionary](https://digitalpalidictionary.github.io/)

:::

1. sabbarattisobhaṇo
2. apāyagato
3. issarakataṁ
4. āgantukabhattaṁ
5. methunāpeto
6. dhaññarāsi
7. saṁsāradukkhaṁ

#### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > sabbarattiṁ sobhaṇo → **sabbarattisobhaṇo**

🚺⨀②🆎(sabba+ratti) 🚹⨀①(sobhaṇo) → 🚹⨀①🆎(sabbaratti+sobhaṇa)  
the whole night / beautiful → beautiful the whole night  
*`sabbarattiṁ sobhaṇo` ("beautiful the whole night") becomes `sabbarattisobhaṇo`, a `tappurisa` of the ② ending, the ② of a time through which something goes on {298}.*

2. > apāyaṁ gato → **apāyagato**

🚹⨀②(apāyaṁ) 🚹⨀①(🔽(ta)(gato)) → 🚹⨀①🆎(apāya+gata)  
to a state of loss / gone → gone to a state of loss  
*`apāyaṁ gato` ("gone to a state of loss") becomes `apāyagato`, a `tappurisa` of the ② ending.*

3. > issarena kataṁ → **issarakataṁ**

🚹⨀③(issarena) 🚻⨀①(🔽(ta)(kataṁ)) → 🚻⨀①🆎(issara+kata)  
by a lord / made → made by a lord  
*`issarena kataṁ` ("made by a lord") becomes `issarakataṁ`, a `tappurisa` of the ③ ending, the ③ of the agent {288}.*

4. > āgantukassa bhattaṁ → **āgantukabhattaṁ**

🚹⨀④(āgantukassa) 🚻⨀①(bhattaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(āgantuka+bhatta)  
for a guest / food → food for guests  
*`āgantukassa bhattaṁ` ("food for a guest") becomes `āgantukabhattaṁ`, a `tappurisa` of the ④ ending, the `sa` ending read as ④.*

5. > methunā apeto → **methunāpeto**

🚻⨀⑤(methunā) 🚹⨀①(🔽(ta)(apeto)) → 🚹⨀①🆎(methuna+apeta)  
from sexual intercourse / abstaining → abstaining from sexual intercourse  
*`methunā apeto` ("abstaining from sexual intercourse") becomes `methunāpeto`, a `tappurisa` of the ⑤ ending; the final `a` of `methuna` and the initial `a` of `apeta` join as `ā` by sandhi {12}, {15}.*

6. > dhaññānaṁ rāsi → **dhaññarāsi**

🚻⨂⑥(dhaññānaṁ) 🚹⨀①(rāsi) → 🚹⨀①🆎(dhañña+rāsi)  
of grains / a heap → a heap of grain  
*`dhaññānaṁ rāsi` ("a heap of grains") becomes `dhaññarāsi`, a `tappurisa` of the ⑥ ending; the first member is plural.*

7. > saṁsāre dukkhaṁ → **saṁsāradukkhaṁ**

🚹⨀⑦(saṁsāre) 🚻⨀①(dukkhaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(saṁsāra+dukkha)  
in the round of rebirth / suffering → the suffering of the round of rebirth  
*`saṁsāre dukkhaṁ` ("suffering in the round of rebirth") becomes `saṁsāradukkhaṁ`, a `tappurisa` of the ⑦ ending.*

### 4.5.2 Both the `kammadhāraya` and the `digu` are `tappurisa` as well {326}

* Both the `kammadhāraya` ("appositional compound", section
  [4.3](../4-3-kammadharaya/)) and the `digu` ("numeral compound", section
  [4.4](../4-4-digu/)) are named `tappurisa` as well.
* The name is given so that the rule {333}, which replaces `na` ("not") by `a`
  in a `tappurisa`, applies to them: `na brāhmaṇo` ("not a brahmin") becomes
  `abrāhmaṇo`.

The vutti's examples all begin with `na`: three in which `na` is joined to a
noun with the same referent, a `kammadhāraya` (`na brāhmaṇo`, `na vasalo`,
`na bhikkhu`), and five in which it is joined to a `digu` (`na pañcavassaṁ`
and the rest). Since both kinds count as `tappurisa`, `na` becomes `a` in
each {333}. In the `digu` examples the end of the compound changes as well,
`godāvarī` to `godāvara` and `go` to `gava` {337}, treated in section
[4.8](../4-8-samasanta/).

#### Example

> na brāhmaṇo → **abrāhmaṇo**

⏏️(na) 🚹⨀①(brāhmaṇo) → 🚹⨀①🆎(na+brāhmaṇa)  
not / a brahmin → a non-brahmin  
*`na brāhmaṇo` ("not a brahmin") becomes `abrāhmaṇo`, a `kammadhāraya` counted as a `tappurisa`, so that `na` becomes `a` {333}.*

The phrase and the compound side by side in the Tipiṭaka (Majjhima Nikāya 98:
[10M 5.8, Vāseṭṭhasutta](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/10M/5/5.8#1459)):

> na jaccā brāhmaṇo hoti, na jaccā hoti **abrāhmaṇo**; kammunā brāhmaṇo hoti, kammunā hoti **abrāhmaṇo**

⏏️(na) 🚺⨀③(jaccā) 🚹⨀①(brāhmaṇo) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(hoti) ⏏️(na) 🚺⨀③(jaccā) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(hoti) 🚹⨀①🆎(na+brāhmaṇa) 🚻⨀③(kammunā) 🚹⨀①(brāhmaṇo) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(hoti) 🚻⨀③(kammunā) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(hoti) 🚹⨀①🆎(na+brāhmaṇa)  
not / by birth / a brahmin / one is / not / by birth / one is / a non-brahmin / by action / a brahmin / one is / by action / one is / a non-brahmin  
*Not by birth is one a brahmin, not by birth is one a non-brahmin; by action is one a brahmin, by action is one a non-brahmin.*

#### Exercises

Resolve the following compounds into their members, name the type of compound,
and give the meaning. Look up the words in a dictionary and take note of the
`vibhatti` endings of the members.

1. avasalo
2. abhikkhu
3. apañcavassaṁ
4. apañcapūlī
5. asattagodāvaraṁ
6. adasagavaṁ
7. apañcagavaṁ

#### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > na vasalo → **avasalo**

⏏️(na) 🚹⨀①(vasalo) → 🚹⨀①🆎(na+vasala)  
not / an outcaste → not an outcaste  
*`na vasalo` ("not an outcaste") becomes `avasalo`, a `kammadhāraya` counted as a `tappurisa`, so that `na` becomes `a` {333}.*

2. > na bhikkhu → **abhikkhu**

⏏️(na) 🚹⨀①(bhikkhu) → 🚹⨀①🆎(na+bhikkhu)  
not / a bhikkhu → not a bhikkhu  
*`na bhikkhu` ("not a bhikkhu") becomes `abhikkhu`, a `kammadhāraya` counted as a `tappurisa`, so that `na` becomes `a` {333}.*

3. > na pañcavassaṁ → **apañcavassaṁ**

⏏️(na) 🚻⨀①🆎(pañca+vassa) → 🚻⨀①🆎(na+pañca+vassa)  
not / five years → not five years  
*`na pañcavassaṁ` ("not five years") becomes `apañcavassaṁ`; `pañcavassaṁ` is a `digu`, counted as a `tappurisa`, so that `na` becomes `a` {333}.*

4. > na pañcapūlī → **apañcapūlī**

⏏️(na) 🚺⨀①🆎(pañca+pūlī) → 🚺⨀①🆎(na+pañca+pūlī)  
not / five sheaves → not five sheaves  
*`na pañcapūlī` ("not five sheaves") becomes `apañcapūlī`; `pañcapūlī` is a `digu`, counted as a `tappurisa`, so that `na` becomes `a` {333}.*

5. > na sattagodāvaraṁ → **asattagodāvaraṁ**

⏏️(na) 🚻⨀①🆎(satta+godāvarī) → 🚻⨀①🆎(na+satta+godāvarī)  
not / the seven Godāvarīs → not the seven Godāvarīs  
*`na sattagodāvaraṁ` ("not the seven Godāvarīs") becomes `asattagodāvaraṁ`; `sattagodāvaraṁ` is a `digu`, counted as a `tappurisa`, so that `na` becomes `a` {333}, and the final `ī` of `godāvarī` has become `a` at the end of the compound {337}.*

6. > na dasagavaṁ → **adasagavaṁ**

⏏️(na) 🚻⨀①🆎(dasa+go) → 🚻⨀①🆎(na+dasa+go)  
not / ten cows → not ten cows  
*`na dasagavaṁ` ("not ten cows") becomes `adasagavaṁ`; `dasagavaṁ` is a `digu`, counted as a `tappurisa`, so that `na` becomes `a` {333}, and `go` has taken the form `gava` at the end of the compound {337}.*

7. > na pañcagavaṁ → **apañcagavaṁ**

⏏️(na) 🚻⨀①🆎(pañca+go) → 🚻⨀①🆎(na+pañca+go)  
not / five cows → not five cows  
*`na pañcagavaṁ` ("not five cows") becomes `apañcagavaṁ`; `pañcagavaṁ` is a `digu`, counted as a `tappurisa`, so that `na` becomes `a` {333}, and `go` has taken the form `gava` at the end of the compound {337}.*
