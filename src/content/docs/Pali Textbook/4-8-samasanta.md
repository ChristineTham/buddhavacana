The usage examples provided here are not exhaustive, other usages are possible.

A `samāsa` ("compound") is a joining of nouns whose meanings are connected
{316}: the `vibhatti` endings of the members are elided {317} and the whole
takes a `vibhatti` ending of its own. The earlier sections sorted compounds by
the relation between their members; this section covers the four rules that
change the last member at the `samāsanta` ("end of a compound"), whatever the
kind of compound: the final vowel of the last member sometimes becomes `a`
{337}, the suffix `ka` is added after a feminine `ī` or `ū` {338}, `jāyā`
("wife") is replaced before `pati` ("husband") {339}, and the suffix `ā` is
added after `dhanu` ("bow") {340}. Each change is made after the members'
endings are elided and before the compound takes its own ending, which is then
added to the changed form.

### 4.8.1 Sometimes `a` for the final of a word at the end of a compound {337}

* Sometimes (`kvaci`) the final vowel of a noun standing at the end of a
  compound becomes the letter `a`. So `rāja` ("king") and `sakha` ("friend"),
  which have an inflection of their own (① `rājā`, `sakhā` {189}), become
  plain `a` lemmas at the end of a compound and are inflected like `purisa`:
  `devarājo`, `devasakho`; `akkhi` ("eye") becomes `akkha` (`visālakkho`),
  `upāhanā` ("sandals") becomes `upāhana` (`chattupāhanaṁ`), and `go` ("cow")
  takes `a` with its `o` becoming `av` (`pañcagavaṁ`).
* Because the rule says `kvaci` ("sometimes") the change is optional:
  `devarājā` and `devasakhā`, inflected like `rāja`, stand beside `devarājo`
  and `devasakho`.
* The sutta says `akāra`, "the letter `a`", and the vutti explains that the
  mention of `kāra` lets the final become `ā` or `i` as well: `paccakkhadhammā`
  ("one to whom the teachings are evident"), with final `ā`; `surabhigandhi`
  ("fragrant"), `sugandhi` ("sweet-smelling"), `pūtigandhi`
  ("foul-smelling"), `kugandhi` ("ill-smelling") and `duggandhi`
  ("evil-smelling"), with final `i`.
* The vutti adds that after a compound ending in a `nadī` (a feminine `ī` or
  `ū`) or in `kattu` ("agent") the suffix `ka` is added at the end of the
  compound: `bahunadiko` ("with many rivers"), `bahukattuko` ("with many
  agents"). The `nadī` final is the subject of the next rule, {338}.

The rule applies to a compound of any kind: a `tappurisa` ("determinative
compound", `devarājo`), a `digu` ("numeral compound", `pañcāhaṁ`,
`sattāhaṁ`), a `dvanda` ("copulative compound", `chattupāhanaṁ`), an
`abyayībhāva` ("adverbial compound", `upasaradaṁ`) and a `bahubbīhi`
("attributive compound", `visālakkho`). The ① ending `o` of the masculine
compounds is the rule {104}; a `digu` is singular and neuter {321}, and an
`abyayībhāva` in `a` takes the ending `aṁ` {341}.

#### Example

> devānaṁ rājā → **devarājo**

🚹⨂⑥(devānaṁ) 🚹⨀①(rājā) → 🚹⨀①🆎(deva+rāja)  
of the gods / king → the king of the gods  
*`devānaṁ rājā` ("the king of the gods") becomes `devarājo`, a `tappurisa` ("determinative compound") of the ⑥ ending: the last member `rāja` takes the final `a` of this rule and the ① ending `o` {104}, and without the change the compound is `devarājā`, inflected like `rāja` {189}.*

#### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > devānaṁ sakhā → **devasakho**

🚹⨂⑥(devānaṁ) 🚹⨀①(sakhā) → 🚹⨀①🆎(deva+sakha)  
of the gods / friend → the friend of the gods  
*`devānaṁ sakhā` ("the friend of the gods") becomes `devasakho`, a `tappurisa` of the ⑥ ending, `sakha` taking the final `a` and the ① ending `o` {104}; without the change, `devasakhā`.*

2. > pañca ahāni → **pañcāhaṁ**

🚻⨂①(pañca ahāni) → 🚻⨀①🆎(pañca+aha)  
five / days → five days  
*`pañca ahāni` ("five days") becomes `pañcāhaṁ`, a `digu` ("numeral compound"), singular and neuter {321}; the `a` of `pañca` is elided before the `a` of `aha` {12}, which is lengthened {15}.*

3. > **sattāhaṁ**

🚻⨀①🆎(satta+aha)  
seven days  
*`sattāhaṁ` ("seven days, a week") is a `digu` like `pañcāhaṁ`, with `satta` ("seven") for `pañca` ("five").*

4. > visālāni akkhīni yassa → **visālakkho**

🚻⨂①(visālāni akkhīni) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) → 🚹⨀①🆎(visāla+akkhi)  
large / eyes / whose → large-eyed  
*`visālāni akkhīni yassa` ("whose eyes are large") becomes `visālakkho`, a `bahubbīhi` ("attributive compound"): `akkhi` ("eye") takes the final `a` and becomes `akkha`, and the compound is masculine after the one it describes, with the ① ending `o` {104}.*

5. > **chattupāhanaṁ**

🚻⨀①🆎(chatta+upāhanā)  
sunshade and sandals  
*`chattupāhanaṁ` ("sunshade and sandals") is a `dvanda` ("copulative compound") of `chatta` ("sunshade") and `upāhanā` ("sandals"), in which the final `ā` of `upāhanā` becomes `a` and the whole is neuter and singular.*

6. > saradassa samīpaṁ → **upasaradaṁ**

🚹⨀⑥(saradassa) 🚻⨀①(samīpaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(upa+sarada)  
of autumn / nearness → near autumn  
*`saradassa samīpaṁ` ("the nearness of autumn") becomes `upasaradaṁ`, an `abyayībhāva` ("adverbial compound") with the prefix `upa` ("near") first and `sarada` ("autumn") last, neuter {320} and with the ending `aṁ` {341}.*

7. > paccakkhā dhammā yassa so → **paccakkhadhammā**

🚹⨂①(paccakkhā dhammā) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(paccakkha+dhamma)  
evident / teachings / whose / he → one to whom the teachings are evident  
*`paccakkhā dhammā yassa so` ("he to whom the teachings are evident") becomes `paccakkhadhammā`, a `bahubbīhi` whose final becomes `ā` by the mention of `kāra`, after which the ① ending `si` is elided {220}.*

8. > sundaro gandho → **sugandhi**

🚹⨀①(sundaro) 🚹⨀①(gandho) → 🚹⨀①🆎(su+gandha)  
pleasant / smell → sweet-smelling  
*`sundaro gandho` ("a pleasant smell", of the one who has it) becomes `sugandhi`, a `bahubbīhi`: `sundara` is replaced by `su` ("well"), the final of `gandha` ("smell") becomes `i` by the mention of `kāra`, and the ① ending `si` is elided {220}.*

9. > bahavo kattāro yassa so → **bahukattuko**

🚹⨂①(bahavo kattāro) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(bahu+kattu+ka)  
many / agents / whose / he → one who has many agents  
*`bahavo kattāro yassa so` ("he who has many agents") becomes `bahukattuko`, a `bahubbīhi` said of a man (`puriso`): the suffix `ka` is added after the final `kattu`, and the ① ending `o` follows {104}.*

### 4.8.2 And `ka` after a feminine `ī` or `ū` final {338}

* And after a compound ending in a `nadī` — Kaccāyana's name for a feminine
  final in `ī` or `ū` — the suffix `ka` is added at the end of the compound:
  `bahunadiko` ("with many rivers", from `nadī`), `bahunāriko` ("with many
  women", from `nārī`).
* The vutti's third example, `bahukantiko` ("with many desires"), has `kanti`,
  a feminine lemma in short `i`, for its last member.

The suffix is added after the members' endings are elided {317}, the long `ī`
of the last member is shortened before it {403}, and the compound then takes
its own `vibhatti` ending: `bahu + nadī + ka` → `bahunadika + si` →
`bahunadiko` {104}. All three examples are `bahubbīhi` compounds ("attributive
compounds") beginning with `bahu` ("many").

#### Example

> bahū nadiyo yasmiṁ so → **bahunadiko**

🚺⨂①(bahū nadiyo) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑦(yasmiṁ) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(bahu+nadī+ka)  
many / rivers / in which / that → with many rivers  
*`bahū nadiyo yasmiṁ so` ("that in which there are many rivers") becomes `bahunadiko`, a `bahubbīhi` ("attributive compound") said of a country: `ka` is added after the `nadī` final of `nadī` ("river"), its `ī` shortened {403}, and the ① ending `o` follows {104}.*

### 4.8.3 `tudaṁ` and `jāni` for `jāyā` before `pati` {339}

* `jāyā` ("wife") is replaced by `tudaṁ` and by `jāni` when `pati`
  ("husband") follows: `tudaṁpatī`, `jānipatī` ("wife and husband").

Both words are `dvanda` compounds ("copulative compounds") of `jāyā ca pati
ca`, and both are given in the ① plural: the ending `yo` is elided after the
`i` of `pati` {118} and the `i` is lengthened {88}.

#### Example

> jāyā ca pati ca → **tudaṁpatī**

🚺⨀①(jāyā) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(pati) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨂①🆎(jāyā+pati)  
wife / and / husband / and → wife and husband  
*`jāyā ca pati ca` ("wife and husband") becomes `tudaṁpatī`, a `dvanda` ("copulative compound") in which `jāyā` is replaced by `tudaṁ` before `pati`, standing in the ① plural with `yo` elided {118} and the `i` lengthened {88}.*

### 4.8.4 And `ā` after `dhanu` {340}

* And after `dhanu` ("bow") the suffix `ā` is added at the end of a compound:
  `gāṇḍīvadhanvā` ("he whose bow is Gāṇḍīva").

The `u` of `dhanu` becomes `v` before the suffix {18}, and the ① ending `si`
is elided after the final `ā` {220}.

#### Example

> gāṇḍīvo dhanu yassa so → **gāṇḍīvadhanvā**

🚹⨀①(gāṇḍīvo) 🚻⨀①(dhanu) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(gāṇḍīva+dhanu+ā)  
Gāṇḍīva / bow / whose / he → he whose bow is Gāṇḍīva  
*`gāṇḍīvo dhanu yassa so` ("he whose bow is Gāṇḍīva") becomes `gāṇḍīvadhanvā`, a `bahubbīhi` ("attributive compound") naming Arjuna, the owner of the bow Gāṇḍīva: the suffix `ā` is added after `dhanu`, whose `u` becomes `v` {18}, and the ① ending `si` is elided after the `ā` {220}.*
