Section [8.1](../8-1-reading-a-verb/) read a verb from its ending: the time,
the person and the number. Two pieces that sit between the root and that
ending change who the subject of the sentence is. With `ya` the verb is
passive (`kamma`, 🔴): the subject is the thing acted on, `ayaṁ vuccati`
("this is said"), and the one who acts, if named at all, takes the ③ ending
{288}. With `e`, `aya`, `āpe` or `āpaya` it is causative (`kārita`, 🟠): the
subject makes someone else act, `kāreti` ("makes [him] do"), and the one made
to act takes the ② ending, or ③ {300}.

So once the ending is read, ask what sits just before it. Kaccāyana gives the
passive the `attanopada` endings (🔵, `-te`, `-ante`) {453}, `vuccate`, but the
texts nearly always swap them for the `parassapada` endings of 8.1 {518},
`vuccati`: a passive verb usually ends exactly like an active one, and only
the piece before the ending gives it away; chapter 7's `labbhetha` keeps an
`attanopada` ending ([7: 2.2](../7-anatta/#u2-2)).

### The passive piece `ya` {440}

`ya` goes on every root alike {440}, after a root such as `vaca` has lost its
final `a` {521}, but it rarely stays visible: after a consonant it melts into
that consonant, after a vowel it stays, and an `ī` may be put in before it.

| what you see | example | root | rule |
| --- | --- | :-: | :-: |
| `-cc-`, `-jj-`, `-jjh-`, `-ññ-`, `-yy-`: `ya` and the root's last consonant have become one doubled letter of the `c` group, or `y` | `vuccati` ("is said"), `vimuccati` ("is liberated"), `bhaññamāne` ("being spoken"), `haññati` ("is struck") | `vaca`, `muca`, `bhaṇa`, `hana` | {441} |
| `-bbh-`, `-ss-` and the like: `ya` has taken the shape of the consonant before it | `labbhati` ("is obtained"), `upalabbhati` ("is found"), `dissati` ("is seen") | `labha`, `disa` | {443} |
| `-īya-`, also written `-iyya-`: an `ī` put in before `ya` | `karīyati` ("is done") | `kara` | {442} |
| `-āya-`, `-īya-` after a root that ends in a vowel | `paññāyati` ("is clearly known"), `dīyati` ("is given"), `khīyati` ("is exhausted") | `ñā`, `dā` (its `ā` to `ī`), `khī` | {440}, {502} |

### The causative piece {438}

Kaccāyana writes the four pieces `ṇe`, `ṇaya`, `ṇāpe`, `ṇāpaya` {438}. The `ṇ`
is not a sound of the word: it is dropped {523}, and it signals that the
root's vowel is strengthened {483}, `a` to `ā`, `i` to `e`, `u` to `o`:
`kara` gives `kār-`, and `pabbajati` ("goes forth", the bonus chapter's
`pabbajeyyaṁ`, [Bonus: 26, 12.8](../11-pbt/#s26-u267-8)) gives `pabbājeti` ("makes go forth", ordains). Before a vowel
that `o` or `e` opens out into `āv` or `āy` {515}: `su` ("hear") gives
`sāveti` ("makes hear"). A root ending in two consonants keeps its vowel,
`cinteti` {483}.

| what you see | example | root | rule |
| --- | --- | :-: | :-: |
| `-e-` before the ending | `kāreti` ("has it done"), `deseti` ("teaches", literally "makes point out") | `kara`, `disa` | {438}, {523}, {483} |
| `-aya-` | `kārayati`, `namayanti` ("straighten"), `damayanti` ("tame") | `kara`, `nama`, `dama` | {438}, {523}, {483} |
| `-āpe-` | `kārāpeti`, `paṭivāpeti` ("pulls back") | `kara`, `vā` | {438}, {523}, {483} |
| `-āpaya-` | `kārāpayati` | `kara` | {438}, {523}, {483} |
| the same piece before a past ending, `-esi`, `-esuṁ`, or in a verb-like form, `-etabba`; in `-ita` only the strengthened root shows | `desesi` ("taught"), `saddamanussāvesuṁ` ("proclaimed"), `bhāvetabbaṁ` ("to be developed"), `bhāvitaṁ` ("developed") | `disa`, `su`, `bhū` | {438}, {515} |

:::note[How to tell a causative from a `cura`-class verb]
`e` and `aya` are also the piece of the `cura` class {452}: `cinteti`
("thinks"), `vedeti` ("knows, feels") and `manteti` ("consults") make nobody
do anything. The shape cannot tell them apart; the root can. The Digital
Pāḷi Dictionary gives every verb its root with the class and the class's
sign: `√cint (8, e, aya)` is the `cura` class, the eighth in its count, and
the `e` is the class's own; `√dis (1, a)` is the `bhū` class, whose piece is
`a`, so the `e` of `deseti` is the causative's, as the literal meaning
"causes to point out" says. [Appendix D](../d-roots/) lists the classes.
Likewise `ya` is the piece of the `divu` class {447} and doubles there too
{444}: `bujjhati` ("understands") and `maññati` ("thinks") are active; the
meaning, "is said", "is done", marks a passive.
:::

The three questions, in order:

```mermaid
flowchart TD
  Q["the ending is read (8.1): what sits just before it?"] -->|"-cc-, -jj-, -ññ-, -bbh-, -ss-, -īya-, -āya-"| Y{"does the dictionary gloss it as 'is …'?"}
  Q -->|"-e-, -aya-, -āpe-, -āpaya-"| E{"is the root of the cura class?"}
  Q -->|"only the class's own piece: -a-, -ṇā-, -o- …"| A["an active verb: 8.2"]
  Y -->|"yes"| P["passive: vuccati, karīyati; the doer in ③"]
  Y -->|"no"| D["an active verb of the divu class: bujjhati"]
  E -->|"no"| C["causative: kāreti, deseti; the one made to act in ②"]
  E -->|"yes"| V["a plain verb: cinteti, vedeti"]
```

### Examples

The passive with the ending of the active {518}:

> virāgā **vimuccati**

🚹⨀⑤(virāga) 🔴⨀🤟▶️(vimuccati)  
from detachment / is liberated  
*from detachment he `vimuccati` (becomes liberated)*  
*`vimuccati`, "is liberated", the passive: `muca` + `ya` as `-cc-` {441}, with the active ending {518}; [9: 9.1](../9-fire/#u9-1).*

Kaccāyana's own example of the `ī` {442}, in the bonus chapter:

> **karīyati** hidaṁ buddhānaṁ sāsanan’ti

🔴⨀🤟▶️(karīyati) ⏏️(hi) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(ima) 🚹⨂⑥(buddha) 🚻⨀①(sāsana) ⏏️(iti)  
is practised / surely / in this case / of Buddha / teaching / (end quote)  
*surely in this case the teaching of the Buddha is practised*  
*`karīyati` 🔴: `kara` + `ī` + `ya` {442}; the teaching, `sāsanaṁ`, is the subject in ①; [Bonus: 45, 9.8](../11-pbt/#s45-u1061-8).*

The passive piece inside a participle of [8.8](../8-8-verb-like-forms/), with the ⑦ of "while" ([5: 11.1](../5-dhammacakka/#u11-1); the same words open [7: 12.3](../7-anatta/#u12-3) and [9: 10.3](../9-fire/#u10-3)):

> Imasmiñca pana veyyākaraṇasmiṁ **bhaññamāne**

*️⃣🤟🚻⨀⑦(ima) ⏏️(ca) ⏏️(pana) 🚻⨀⑦(veyyākaraṇa) 🚻⨀⑦🔽(māna)(bhaññamāna)  
in this / and / moreover / detailed exposition / being spoken  
*And while this detailed exposition was being spoken*  
*`bhaññamāne`: `bhaṇa` + `ya`, `ṇ` and `ya` one `ññ` {441}, then `-māna` and the ⑦ ending; the exposition is what is spoken, not the speaker.*

The causative in the past, with its lengthening:

> bhummā devā **saddamanussāvesuṁ**

🚹⨂①(bhumma) 🚹⨂①(deva) 🟠⨂🤟⏮(saddamanussāveti)  
earth-dwelling / devas / raised a cry  
*`bhummā devā` (the earth-dwelling devas) raised a cry*  
*`saddamanussāvesuṁ` 🟠, "made the cry heard": `saddaṁ` + `anussāvesuṁ`, causative `e` {438} on `su` strengthened to `sāv` {483}, {515}, before the past ending `-suṁ`; [5: 12.1](../5-dhammacakka/#u12-1).*

The `-aya-` causative:

> Attānaṁ **damayanti** paṇḍitā.

🚹⨀②(atta) 🟠⨂🤟▶️(damayati) 🚹⨂①(paṇḍita)  
self / tame / the wise  
*The wise tame the self.*  
*`damayanti` 🟠: `dama` ("be tame") + `aya` {438}, "make tame"; the one made tame, `attānaṁ`, is the object in ② {297}; [Bonus: 21, 4](../11-pbt/#s21-u86-4).*

### Exercises

Each of the following is from chapter [5](../5-dhammacakka/),
[7](../7-anatta/) or the [bonus chapter](../11-pbt/). Find the verb or verb-like form and
say which piece stands before its ending, whether it is passive, causative or
neither, and what its root is; a dictionary gives the root's class.

:::tip[Hint]
You may find the [Digital Pali Dictionary](https://digitalpalidictionary.github.io/)
useful for looking up words, as well as doing the sandhi and compound word
deconstruction.
:::

1. Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāvāyāmo.
2. Tassa me tathāgato vā tathāgatasāvako vā dhammaṁ desesi.
3. labbhetha ca rūpe
4. Usukārā namayanti tejanaṁ;
5. dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā ariyasaccaṁ bhāvetabban’ti
6. Taṁ kiṁ maññatha, bhikkhave, rūpaṁ niccaṁ vā aniccaṁ vā”ti?

### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > Ayaṁ **vuccati**, bhikkhave, sammāvāyāmo.

*️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(ima) 🔴⨀🤟▶️(vuccati) 🚹⨂⓪(bhikkhu) 🚹⨀①🆎(sammā+vāyāma)  
this / is said / Bhikkhave / right effort  
*Bhikkhave, this is said to be "sammāvāyāma" (right effort).*  
*Passive 🔴: `vaca` + `ya` as `-cc-` {441}, with the active ending {518}; `sammāvāyāmo`, the thing said, in ①; [Bonus: 35, 21.24](../11-pbt/#s35-u1079-3).*

2. > Tassa me tathāgato vā tathāgatasāvako vā dhammaṁ **desesi**.

*️⃣🤟🚹⨀④(ta) *️⃣👆⚧⨀④(ahaṁ) 🚹⨀①🆎(tathā+gata) ⏏️(vā) 🚹⨀①🆎(tathā+gata+sāvaka) ⏏️(vā) 🚹⨀②(dhamma) 🟠⨀🤟⏮(deseti)  
to that / to me / Tathāgata / or / disciple of the Buddha / or / Dhamma / taught  
*Because of that, the Tathāgata and his disciples taught the Dhamma to me.*  
*Causative 🟠: `disa` ("point out", the `bhū` class) + `e` {438} before the past ending `-si`; what is taught, `dhammaṁ`, in ② {297}; [Bonus: 26, 12.3](../11-pbt/#s26-u267-3).*

3. > **labbhetha** ca rūpe

🔵⨀🤟⏯(labhati) ⏏️(ca) 🚻⨀⑦(rūpa)  
could be obtained / and / in form  
*and `rūpa` could be obtained*  
*Passive 🔴: `labha` + `ya` as `-bbh-` {443}, here with the `attanopada` ending `-etha` {425}, {453}; [7: 2.2](../7-anatta/#u2-2).*

4. > Usukārā **namayanti** tejanaṁ;

🚹⨂①🆎(usu+kāra) 🟠⨂🤟▶️(namayati) 🚻⨀②(tejana)  
arrow-makers / straighten / arrow  
*Arrow makers straighten arrow;*  
*Causative 🟠: `nama` ("bend") + `aya` {438}, "make bend"; the arrow, `tejanaṁ`, in ② {297}; [Bonus: 21, 2](../11-pbt/#s21-u86-2).*

5. > dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā ariyasaccaṁ **bhāvetabban**’ti

🚺⨀①🆎(dukkha+nirodha+gāmī) 🚺⨀①(paṭipadā) 🚻⨀①🆎(ariya+sacca) 🚻⨀①🔽(tabba)(bhāvetabba) ⏏️(iti)  
leading to the cessation of suffering / path / realisation / to be developed / thus  
*‘This `dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā ariyasacca` should be `bhāvetabbaṁ` (developed)’: thus*  
*Causative 🟠 under the `-tabba` of [8.8](../8-8-verb-like-forms/): `bhū` ("be") strengthened to `bhāv` {483}, {515} + `e` {438}, `bhāveti`, "makes be", develops; [5: 8.2](../5-dhammacakka/#u8-2).*

6. > Taṁ kiṁ **maññatha**, bhikkhave, rūpaṁ niccaṁ vā aniccaṁ vā”ti?

*️⃣🤟🚻⨀②(ta) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀②(kiṁ) 🟢⨂🤘▶️(maññati) 🚹⨂⓪(bhikkhu) 🚻⨀①(rūpa) 🚻⨀①(nicca) ⏏️(vā) 🚻⨀①🆎(na+nicca) ⏏️(vā) ⏏️(iti)  
that / what / do you think / Bhikkhave / form / permanent / or / impermanent / or / (end quote)  
*“Bhikkhave, what do you consider: is `rūpa` (form) `nicca` (permanent) or `anicca` (impermanent)?”*  
*Neither: `-ññ-` is the `divu` class's own `ya` {447}, {444}, `√man (3, ya)`, and the dictionary glosses it "think", not "is …": active; [7: 6.1](../7-anatta/#u6-1).*
