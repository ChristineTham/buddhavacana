The usage examples provided here are not exhaustive, other usages are possible.

`liṅgattha` ("the meaning of the lemma", the bare meaning of a word) and
`ālapana` ("calling", the vocative) are not `kāraka`. A `kāraka` names a
relation to an action, and these two name none: a word in the `liṅgattha`
sense simply names its thing, and a word in the `ālapana` sense calls the
person spoken to, before any action is asked of them. They are placed in this
chapter because Kaccāyana states the use of every `vibhatti` ending together in
{284}–{315}, and these two uses of the ① ending open that list.

### 2.8.1 The bare meaning of the lemma {284}

* The ① ending merely expresses the meaning of the lemma (`liṅgattha`): the
  word names its thing, and nothing more is said about it. (The lemma,
  `liṅga`, is the bare word before any ending is added: `purisa`.)

Kaccāyana's examples are a noun in the singular and the plural (`puriso`,
`purisā`), two numerals (`eko`, `dve`) and six particles (`nipāta`: `ca`,
`vā`, `he`, `ahe`, `re`, `are`). The Padarūpasiddhi explains the list: the
bare meaning of a noun carries its gender and number, while a particle's
meaning is free even of those, and it is still a bare meaning, so the particles
stand under the same rule. The doer of an action named with the ① ending is
the agent (`kattā`), which has its own section, [2.6](../2-6-agent/); here
each word stands by itself.

#### Example

> **puriso**

🚹⨀①(puriso)  
a man  
*a man*

The dictionary lists `purisā` as the ① plural, as the ③ and ⑤ singular and
as a vocative form as well; which one is meant, only the sentence tells.
Kaccāyana's example here is the ① plural.

2. > eko

🚹⨀①(eko)  
one  
*one*

3. > dve

⚧⨂①(dve)  
two  
*two*

`dve` is the form of `dvi` before `yo` {132}. The numerals from two upwards  
are plural, and `dvi` has the one set of forms for all three genders (⚧).

4. > ca

⏏️(ca)
and
*and*

5. > vā

⏏️(vā)
or
*or*

6. > he

⏏️(he)
hey!
*hey!*

7. > ahe

⏏️(ahe)
friend! (said to an equal)
*friend!*

8. > re

⏏️(re)
hey, you! (said in contempt)
*hey, you!*

9. > are

⏏️(are)
away with you!
*away with you!*

A particle has no gender, number or ending to work out: the dictionary gives
its meaning directly. `he`, `ahe`, `re` and `are` are the calling particles;
in 2.8.2 `he` stands before a vocative noun.

### 2.8.2 Calling someone {285}

* The ① ending is also used when the sense of calling (`ālapana`, the
  vocative) is added to the bare meaning of the lemma: the word names the
  person spoken to.

Kaccāyana gives the vocative no ending of its own. Its singular ending is the  
① ending `si`, which {57} renames `ga` for the vocative, and so the vocative is  
counted as a use of the ① ending. Its forms often differ from the ① forms,  
`purisa` ("man!") beside `puriso` ("a man"), so this book marks them ⓪ in the  
analysis line, as chapter 1 does. The Padarūpasiddhi explains why calling is  
not a `kāraka`: a word of address turns the hearer towards the speaker, and  
only then is the hearer joined to an action, "come", "go" and the like; at the  
moment of calling there is no action yet.

Kaccāyana's examples set each vocative noun after a word of address: `bho`
("sir") and `bhavanto` ("sirs"), the vocative singular and plural of
`bhavanta`, which take the number of the noun they go with; or the particle
`he` ("hey!") of 2.8.1. `bho purisa`, "sir, man!", is the polite address of
Pāḷi, much like the English "good man".

#### Example

> **bho purisa**

🚹⨀⓪(bho purisa)  
sir / man  
*Sir, man!*

The same words open a command in the Tipiṭaka (Dīgha Nikāya 24, Pāthikasutta:
[8D 1.5, Iddhipāṭihāriyakathā](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/8D/1/1.5#44)):
the man is called first and then told what to do.

> ehi tvaṁ, **bho purisa**

🟢⨀🤘⏹(ehi) *️⃣🤘⚧⨀①(tvaṁ) 🚹⨀⓪(bho purisa)  
come / you / sir / man  
*Come, you, good man!*
