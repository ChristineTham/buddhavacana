Open any page of the Canon and you meet words like `mahāpuriso` ("a great
man"), `kaṇhasappo` ("a black snake") or `abrāhmaṇo` ("a non-brahmin"): two
words that name one and the same thing, made into one. Kaccāyana calls such a
compound a `kammadhāraya` {324}, a compound of two words with the same referent
(`tulyādhikaraṇa`): `kaṇho sappo` is a snake, and it is black. When the two
join, the ending of each member drops {317} and the whole takes one ending,
like any other noun; this book marks the compound 🆎 with its members in their
lemma form, 🚹⨀①🆎(kaṇha+sappa). The question to ask when you meet one is: do
both members name the same thing? If the first describes the second ("black" +
"snake", "great" + "man", "not" + "brahmin"), it is a `kammadhāraya`; if the
compound means "the X of Y" it is a `tappurisa` ([6.5](../6-5-tappurisa/)), and
if it names a third thing it is a `bahubbīhi` ([6.6](../6-6-bahubbihi/)).

Four first members change their shape when they join, and these are the ones
to learn to recognise: `mahanta` ("great") becomes `mahā` {330}, a feminine
word goes back to its masculine shape {331}–{332}, the word `na` ("not")
becomes `a` or `an` {333}–{334}, and `ku` ("bad") becomes `kad` or `kā`
{335}–{336}. Kaccāyana counts every `kammadhāraya` as a `tappurisa` as well
{326}, which is why the rules for `na` speak of a `tappurisa`; and a
`kammadhāraya` whose first member is a numeral has a name of its own, the
`digu` {325} ([6.4](../6-4-digu/)).

### The kinds of `kammadhāraya`

| what you see | what it means | example | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| an adjective, then its noun | the noun as described: a snake that is black | `kaṇhasappo` ("a black snake", `kaṇha` + `sappa`) | {324} |
| a noun, then another noun for the same person or thing | both names at once: a brahmin woman who is a girl | `brāhmaṇadārikā` ("a brahmin girl", `brāhmaṇī` + `dārikā`) | {324} |
| `mahā-`, or `maha-` with the next consonant doubled | "great": `mahanta` in its compound shape | `mahāpuriso` ("a great man"), `mahapphalaṁ` ("of great fruit") | {330} |
| a feminine word in its masculine shape, then a feminine noun | the first word describes the second | `khattiyakaññā` ("a khattiya maiden", `khattiyā` + `kaññā`) | {331}, {332} |
| `a-` before a consonant, `an-` before a vowel | "not": the word `na` | `abrāhmaṇo` ("a non-brahmin"), `anariyo` ("ignoble") | {333}, {334} |
| `kad-` before a vowel, `kā-`, or `ku-` before a consonant | "bad", and `kā-` also "a little": the word `ku` | `kadannaṁ` ("bad food"), `kālavaṇaṁ` ("a little salt"), `kujanā` ("bad people") | {335}, {336} |

The `maha-` shape doubles the consonant after it {29}, so `mahapphalaṁ` and
`mahabbhayaṁ` ("great danger") carry the same `mahanta` as `mahāpuriso`. The
rule for a feminine word is stated first for a `bahubbīhi`, `dīghajaṅgho` ("one
whose shins are long", `dīghā` + `jaṅghā`) {331}, then extended to the
`kammadhāraya` {332}; it applies only to a word that has a masculine shape to
go back to, so `brāhmaṇabandhu` ("a brahmin's kinswoman") keeps its form in
`brāhmaṇabandhudārikā`. `kā-` for `ku` means "a little" and now and then "bad",
as in `kāpurisā` ("wretched men") {336}. Every rule number links to the full
rule in the Kaccāyana translation; the [chapter overview](../6-0-samasa/)
tables every rule of the Samāsakappa.

### Examples

> kaṇho ca so sappo ca → **kaṇhasappo**

🚹⨀①(kaṇho) ⏏️(ca) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) 🚹⨀①(sappo) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨀①🆎(kaṇha+sappa)  
black / and / it / a snake / and → a black snake  
*adjective + noun {324}: `kaṇha` ("black") describes `sappa` ("snake"); both endings drop and the compound takes its own.*

> mahanto ca so puriso ca → **mahāpuriso**

🚹⨀①(mahanto) ⏏️(ca) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) 🚹⨀①(puriso) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨀①🆎(mahanta+purisa)  
great / and / he / a man / and → a great man  
*`mahā` for `mahanta` {330}: "great" + "man"; it is the first part of `mahāpurisavihāro` in chapter 11.*

> na brāhmaṇo → **abrāhmaṇo**

⏏️(na) 🚹⨀①(brāhmaṇo) → 🚹⨀①🆎(na+brāhmaṇa)  
not / a brahmin → a non-brahmin  
*`a` for `na` {333}; before a vowel `na` becomes `an`, `anariyo` ("ignoble") {334}.*

### Exercises

Each of the following compounds you have met in the Bodhikathā (chapter 3) or
in the Pali Buddhist Texts (chapter 11), in the form it has there. Resolve it
into its members, say which kind of `kammadhāraya` it is, and give the meaning.
Look the word up in a dictionary and check its first member against the table
above.

:::tip[Hint]
You may find the [Digital Pali Dictionary](https://digitalpalidictionary.github.io/)
useful for looking up words, as well as for resolving a compound into its
members.
:::

1. avijjāya
2. paṭhamābhisambuddho
3. nīcaseyyaṁ
4. sāyanhasamayaṁ
5. tathāgato
6. sammādiṭṭhi
7. anagāriyaṁ
8. amataṁ

### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > na + vijjā → **avijjāya**

🚺⨀⑥🆎(na+vijjā)  
not + knowledge → of ignorance  
*`a` for `na` {333}: "not-knowledge"; Bodhikathā 4.1, and the first member of `avijjāpaccayā` in 3.1.*

2. > paṭhama + abhisambuddha → **paṭhamābhisambuddho**

🚹⨀①🆎(paṭhama+abhisambuddha)  
first + fully awakened → newly fully awakened  
*`paṭhama` ("first") describes `abhisambuddha` ("fully awakened") {324}; `a` + `a` join as `ā` {12}, {15}; Bodhikathā 2.1.*

3. > nīca + seyyā → **nīcaseyyaṁ**

🚺⨀②🆎(nīca+seyyā)  
low + bed → low bed  
*adjective + noun {324}: `nīca` ("low") describes `seyyā` ("bed"); chapter 11, `nīcaseyyaṁ kappenti`.*

4. > sāyanha + samaya → **sāyanhasamayaṁ**

🚹⨀②🆎(sāyanha+samaya)  
evening + time → in the late afternoon  
*noun + noun {324}: `sāyanha` ("evening") and `samaya` ("time") name one time; chapter 11, `sāyanhasamayaṁ paṭisallānā vuṭṭhito`.*

5. > tathā + gata → **tathāgato**

🚹⨀①🆎(tathā+gata)  
thus + gone → Tathāgata  
*`tathā` ("thus") describes `gata` ("gone", "become") {324}: the Buddha's title; chapter 11, `tathāgato vā tathāgatasāvako vā`.*

6. > sammā + diṭṭhi → **sammādiṭṭhi**

🚺⨀①🆎(sammā+diṭṭhi)  
rightly + view → right view  
*`sammā` ("rightly") describes `diṭṭhi` ("view") {324}: the first factor of the path; chapter 11, `sammādiṭṭhi sammāsaṅkappo`.*

7. > na + agāriya → **anagāriyaṁ**

🚻⨀②🆎(na+agāriya)  
not + belonging to a house → to homeless state  
*`an` for `na` before a vowel {334}: `agāriya` ("belonging to a house"); chapter 11, `agārasmā anagāriyaṁ`.*

8. > na + mata → **amataṁ**

🚻⨀①🆎(na+mata)  
not + dead → free from death  
*`a` for `na` {333}: "not dead", the deathless, a name of nibbāna; chapter 11, `amataṁ ajjāpi ca labhanīyamidaṁ`.*
