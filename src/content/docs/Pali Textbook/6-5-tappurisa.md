`rājaputto` ("the king's son") is the two words `rañño putto` made into one.
The second member, `putto`, names the thing; the first says whose son, and if
the two stood apart it would carry the ⑥ ending, `rañño` ("of the king").
Kaccāyana calls such a compound a `tappurisa` {327}: a word that would carry
the ② ending or any later one, ③ to ⑦, is joined to the word that follows it,
and the compound keeps the sense of the ending that was dropped. The name is
itself one: `tappuriso` is `tassa puriso`, "his man".

With the `kammadhāraya`, these are the commonest compounds in the texts. When
you meet a compound, ask two questions. Is it the kind of thing its last member
names? A `rājaputto` is a `putto`, and the compound takes its gender from that
member; a compound that names something else, `chinnahattho` ("one whose hand
is cut off"), is the `bahubbīhi` of section [6.6](../6-6-bahubbihi/), and one
whose members name the same thing, `mahāpuriso` ("a great man"), the
`kammadhāraya` of [6.3](../6-3-kammadharaya/). And what small English word
links the members, "of", "by", "for", "from", "in"? That word is the ending
the first member has lost, and it names the kind.

### The six kinds, by the ending of the first member

One phrase and its compound for each ending, ② to ⑦; the ending is the one
chapter [4](../4-0-karaka/) gives the first word for its work in the sentence:

| ending | the first member is … | phrase → compound | meaning | rule |
| :-: | --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| ② `dutiyā` | the object, or the place gone to {297} | `bhūmiṁ gato` → `bhūmigato` | gone to the ground | {327} |
| ③ `tatiyā` | the instrument {286}, or the doer {288} | `sallena viddho` → `sallaviddho` | pierced by an arrow | {327} |
| ④ `catutthī` | the one it is for {293} | `kathinassa dussaṁ` → `kathinadussaṁ` | cloth for the `kathina` | {327} |
| ⑤ `pañcamī` | the source, "from" {295}, or the thing feared {271} | `corā bhayaṁ` → `corabhayaṁ` | fear of thieves | {327} |
| ⑥ `chaṭṭhī` | the owner, "of" {301} | `rañño putto` → `rājaputto` | the king's son | {327} |
| ⑦ `sattamī` | the place or time, "in", "at" {302} | `rūpe saññā` → `rūpasaññā`; `saṁsāre dukkhaṁ` → `saṁsāradukkhaṁ` | apperception of form; suffering in `saṁsāra` | {327} |

The rule's other examples, `sabbarattisobhaṇo` ("beautiful the whole night",
② of a stretch of time {298}), `issarakataṁ` ("made by a lord", ③) and
`dhaññarāsi` ("a heap of grain", ⑥, from the plural `dhaññānaṁ`) among them,
run the same way, and so does the Fire Sermon's `rāgagginā` ("with the fire of
passion", ⑥: `rāgassa aggi`, [9: 2.6](../9-fire/#u2-6)). The Digital Pāḷi
Dictionary names the kind by this ending, in the Pāḷi of the first column: a
`chaṭṭhī tappurisa` had the ⑥ ending on its first member, a `dutiyā
tappurisa` the ②.

In each the ending of the first member is dropped {317} and the member
stands in its lemma form, the form a word has before its ending,
`rañño` as `rāja`, `bhūmiṁ` as `bhūmi` {318}; the whole compound then takes an
ending of its own, as `bodhirukkhamūle` ("at the root of the Bodhi tree") in
[3: 2.1](../3-awakening/#u2-1) carries the ⑦.

### The `kammadhāraya` and the `digu` count as `tappurisa` too

The second rule here is a naming rule: the `kammadhāraya` and the `digu` are
called `tappurisa` as well {326}, so that a rule for the `tappurisa` reaches
them: `na` ("not") at the head of a `tappurisa` becomes `a` {333}. Every
example begins with `na`:

| phrase | compound | the compound is | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| `na brāhmaṇo` | `abrāhmaṇo` ("a non-brahmin") | a `kammadhāraya`, `na` with a noun | {326}, {333} |
| `na pañcavassaṁ` | `apañcavassaṁ` ("not five years") | a `digu`, `na` with a numeral compound | {326}, {333} |

The `a-` for `na` is treated in section [6.3](../6-3-kammadharaya/); every rule
number links to the full rule in the Kaccāyana translation, and
[chapter 6](../6-0-samasa/) tables them all.

### Examples

> rañño putto → **rājaputto**

🚹⨀⑥(rañño) 🚹⨀①(putto) → 🚹⨀①🆎(rāja+putta)  
of the king / son → the king's son  
*⑥ {327}: the first member answers "whose?"; `rañño` loses its ending and stands as `rāja`.*

> bhūmiṁ gato → **bhūmigato**

🚺⨀②(bhūmiṁ) 🚹⨀①(🔽(ta)(gato)) → 🚹⨀①🆎(bhūmi+gata)  
to the ground / gone → gone to the ground  
*② {327}: the first member is where one has gone; a word like `gato` ("gone") often ends a ② compound.*

### Exercises

Each of the following is a `tappurisa` from chapters [3](../3-awakening/) and
the [bonus chapter](../11-pbt/), in the form it has there, with a link to its sentence. Split
it into its members, say which ending the first member would carry on its own,
and give the meaning. Look the word up in a dictionary and check the kind it is
given.

### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > bodhirukkhassa mūle → **bodhirukkhamūle**

🚹⨀⑥🆎(bodhi+rukkha) 🚻⨀⑦(mūle) → 🚻⨀⑦🆎(bodhirukkha+mūla)  
of the Bodhi tree / at the root → at the root of the Bodhi tree  
*⑥ {327}: two compounds in one, `bodhirukkha` being itself `bodhiyā rukkho`; the whole takes the ⑦ ending.*

2. > saṅkhārānaṁ nirodho → **saṅkhāranirodho**

🚹⨂⑥(saṅkhārānaṁ) 🚹⨀①(nirodho) → 🚹⨀①🆎(saṅkhāra+nirodha)  
of mental constructions / cessation → the cessation of mental constructions  
*⑥ {327}: a plural first member, as in `dhaññarāsi`; every `-nirodho` of [chapter 3](../3-awakening/) is built so.*

3. > vimuttisukhaṁ paṭisaṁvedī → **vimuttisukhapaṭisaṁvedī**

🚻⨀②🆎(vimutti+sukha) 🚹⨀①(paṭisaṁvedī) → 🚹⨀①🆎(vimuttisukha+paṭisaṁvedī)  
the bliss of liberation / experiencing → experiencing the bliss of liberation  
*② {327}: the object of `paṭisaṁvedī` ("experiencing"); `vimuttisukhaṁ` is itself `vimuttiyā sukhaṁ`, ⑥.*

4. > samādhimhā jaṁ → **samādhijaṁ**

🚹⨀⑤(samādhimhā) 🚻⨀①(jaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(samādhi+ja)  
from focus / born → born from focus  
*⑤ {327}: "born from", the source {295}; `-ja` ("born") closes a ⑤ compound as `-gato` closes a ②.*

5. > tathāgatassa sāvako → **tathāgatasāvako**

🚹⨀⑥(tathāgatassa) 🚹⨀①(sāvako) → 🚹⨀①🆎(tathāgata+sāvaka)  
of the Tathāgata / disciple → disciple of the Buddha  
*⑥ {327}: "the disciple of the Tathāgata", as `rājaputto` is "the son of the king".*

6. > sammāsambuddhena desitaṁ → **sammāsambuddhadesitaṁ**

🚹⨀③🆎(sammā+sambuddha) 🚻⨀①🔽(ta)(desita) → 🚻⨀①🆎(sammāsambuddha+desita)  
by the perfectly awakened one / taught → taught by the perfectly awakened one  
*③ {327}: the doer {288} of `desitaṁ` ("taught"), as in `issarakataṁ` ("made by a lord"); the word describes `nibbānaṁ`.*
