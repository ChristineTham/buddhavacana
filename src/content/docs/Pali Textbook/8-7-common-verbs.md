A handful of verbs do most of the work of the texts: to be, to do, to go, to
give, to know, to say, to see, to hear, to stand and to sit. Their present
forms follow the classes of [8.2](../8-2-present/), but their past forms,
absolutives and participles are mostly not what the endings of
[8.4](../8-4-past/) and [8.8](../8-8-verb-like-forms/) lead you to expect:
`ahosi` ("was") shows nothing of `hoti`, `akāsi` ("did") nothing of `karoti`,
and `diṭṭha` ("seen") and `dinna` ("given") are the participles of `passati`
and `dadāti`. Each shape has its rule in Kaccāyana, linked by its number; the
reader's question is simpler: which verb (`ākhyāta`) is hiding here, and is
this its present, its past, its absolutive or its participle?

The tables answer it for the eleven verbs, each with its root (`dhātu`) as
Kaccāyana names it; [Appendix D](../d-roots/) lists the roots with their
classes. The forms in bold do not show their root plainly and are learnt as
words.

### `atthi` and `hoti`: the two verbs "to be"

Pāḷi has two verbs for "to be", from the roots `asa` and `hū`, with
different jobs. `atthi` says that something exists, "there is": `atthi
nibbuti` ("there is extinguishment"); with `na` before it, `natthi`, "there
is not". What exists takes the ① ending, and the plural is `santi`. `hoti`
joins a subject to what it is or becomes: `samudayo hoti` ("[this] is the
origin"), `nāmaṁ ahosi` ("the name was"), and with a past participle it makes
a tense of its own, `samannāgato hoti` ("he is endowed"), on every page of
[the bonus chapter](../11-pbt/). The two share their other forms: the past of both is `ahosi`, the
future `bhavissati`, the wish `siyā` or `assa`; `bhavati`, the regular verb
of the root `bhū` ("to become") {445}, is the grammar's model, and the texts
prefer `hoti`.

| what you see | it means | form | root | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | :-: | :-: |
| `atthi` | there is; is | 🟢⨀🤟▶️ | `asa` | {494} |
| `natthi` | there is not | 🟢⨀🤟▶️, `na` + `atthi` | `asa` | {494}, {12} |
| `santi` | there are | 🟢⨂🤟▶️ | `asa` | {506} |
| `amhi`, `asmi` | I am | 🟢⨀👆▶️ | `asa` | {492} |
| `siyā`, `assa` | there might be; would be | 🟢⨀🤟⏯ | `asa` | {506}, {517} |
| `bhavissati` | there will be; will be | 🟢⨀🤟⏭ | `asa` → `bhū` | {507} |
| `hoti` | is; becomes | 🟢⨀🤟▶️ | `hū` | {485}, {510} |
| `honti` | are | 🟢⨂🤟▶️ | `hū` | {485} |
| `hotu` | let it be; may it be | 🟢⨀🤟⏹ | `hū` | {415}, {485} |
| `hontu` | let them be ([7: 4.5](../7-anatta/#u4-5)) | 🟢⨂🤟⏹ | `hū` | {415}, {485} |
| **`ahosi`** | was; became | 🟢⨀🤟⏮ | `hū` | {519}, {491}, {517} |
| **`ahesuṁ`** | they were; `mā ahesuṁ`, "let them not be" ([7: 4.5](../7-anatta/#u4-5)) | 🟢⨂🤟⏮ | `hū` | {519}, {491}, {517}, {420} |
| `hutvā` | having been | 🔽(tvā) | `hū` | {564}, {26} |
| `bhūta` | become; been | 🔽(ta) | `bhū` | {555} |

:::note[How to tell ahosi from gacchasi]
`gacchasi` and `karosi` end in `-si` as the second person of the present,
"you go, you do" {423}. `ahosi`, `akāsi`, `aññāsi` and `adāsi` end in `-si`
for another reason, the `s` inserted in the `ajjatanī` {491} and its
third-person ending `-ī` {428}, shortened to `-i` {517}: they are the past,
"he was, did, knew, gave".
The `a` put before the root {519} is the sign: `a-` in front and `-si` at the
end is a past form, not "you do".
:::

### The nine verbs of doing and going, giving and knowing, saying, seeing and hearing, standing and sitting

| verb | root | present | past | absolutive | past participle |
| --- | :-: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| to do, to make | `kara` | `karoti` {451}; causative `kāreti` {438} | **`akāsi`** {519}, {491} | `katvā` {564}, {587} | `kata` {555}, {587} |
| to go | `gamu` | **`gacchati`** {476} | `agamāsi` {519}, {491}; `agacchi` {517} | `gantvā` {564}, {596}; after a prefix `-gamma` {600} | `gata` {555}, {586} |
| to give | `dā` | `dadāti` {458}; `deti` {510} | `adāsi` {519}, {491} | `datvā` {564}, {26} | **`dinna`** {582} |
| to know | `ñā` | **`jānāti`** {449}, {470} | **`aññāsi`** {519}, {509}, {491} | `ñatvā` {564}, {26} | `ñāta` {555} |
| to say | `vada`, `vaca`, `brū` | `vadati` {445} | **`avoca`** {519}, {477}; **`āha`** {475} | `vatvā` {564} | **`vutta`** {579} |
| to see | `disa` | **`passati`** {471} | **`addasā`** {519}, {471} | **`disvā`** {599} | **`diṭṭha`** {572} |
| to hear | `su` | `suṇāti` {448} | **`assosi`** {519}, {491} | `sutvā` {564} | `suta` {555} |
| to stand | `ṭhā` | **`tiṭṭhati`** {468} | `aṭṭhāsi` {519}, {491} | — | `ṭhita` {588} |
| to sit | `ni` + `sada` | `nisīdati` {609} | `nisīdi` {428}, {517}, {609} | `nisīditvā` {564}, {605} | **`nisinna`** {582} |

Three things to notice in the table:

- The past. `akāsi`, `adāsi`, `aññāsi`, `agamāsi`, `assosi` and `aṭṭhāsi` are
  made alike: the `a` put before the root {519}, an `s` inserted before the
  ending {491}, the root's vowel lengthened or its consonant doubled on the
  way {517}. `avoca` turns the root's `a` to `o` {477}; `āha` ("he said",
  plural `āhu`) is the `parokkhā` of `brū` {475}; `nisīdi` is the plain
  ending `-ī` {428}, shortened {517}, without the `a-`, which {519} puts in
  only "sometimes".
- The participles. `kata`, `gata`, `ṭhita`, `suta` and `ñāta` keep the root
  with its final dropped or changed ({587}, {586}, {588}); `dinna` and
  `nisinna` take `-inna` for `-ta` {582}, `vutta` has `u` for `va` and a
  doubled `t` {579}, `diṭṭha` has `riṭṭha` for `ta` {572}. All agree with a
  noun as an adjective does; `tathāgata` ("thus gone") carries `gata`.
- The absolutives. `katvā`, `gantvā` and `sutvā` are the root with `-tvā`
  {564}; a root in `ā` shortens it, `datvā`, `ñatvā` {26}; `disvā` has `svā`
  for `tvā` {599}; after a prefix a root in `m` takes `-ya`, so that
  `anupagamma` ("not approaching") in chapter 5 belongs to `gacchati` {600}.

### Examples

> Evametassa kevalassa dukkhakkhandhassa samudayo **hoti**.

⏏️(evaṁ) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(eta) 🚹⨀⑥(kevala) 🚹⨀⑥🆎(dukkha+khandha) 🚹⨀①(samudaya) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(hoti)  
thus / of this / of the entire / of the mass of suffering / origin / is  
*`hoti`: the present of the root `hū`, `ū` strengthened to `o` {485}; it joins `samudayo`, its ① subject, to what it is; [3: 3.12](../3-awakening/#u3-12).*

> Iti hidaṁ āyasmato koṇḍaññassa “aññāsikoṇḍañño” tveva nāmaṁ **ahosī**ti.

⏏️(iti) ⏏️(hi) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(ima) 🚹⨀⑥(āyasmant) 🚹⨀⑥(koṇḍañña) 🚹⨀①(aññāsikoṇḍañña) ⏏️(tu) ⏏️(eva) 🚻⨀①(nāma) 🟢⨀🤟⏮(hoti) ⏏️(iti)  
thus / indeed / this / of the venerable / Koṇḍañña / Aññāsikoṇḍañña / just / so / the name / was / (end quote)  
*`ahosi`, "was": the past of `hoti`, the `a` before the root {519} and the `s` inserted {491}, its `-i` lengthened where `ti` joins; [5: 14.2](../5-dhammacakka/#u14-2).*

> evaṁ me rūpaṁ **hotu**

⏏️(evaṁ) *️⃣👆⚧⨀⑥(ahaṁ) 🚻⨀①(rūpa) 🟢⨀🤟⏹(hoti)  
thus / my / form / let be  
*`hotu`, "let it be": the command of `hoti`, `-tu` {424}, third person singular; [7: 2.2](../7-anatta/#u2-2).*

> Idam**avoca** bhagavā.

*️⃣🤟🚻⨀②(ima) 🟢⨀🤟⏮(vadati) 🚹⨀①(bhagavant)  
this / said / the Bhagavā  
*`avoca`, "said": the past of `vadati`, the past's `a` {519}, `o` for the root's `a` {477}, joined to `idaṁ`; [5: 10.3](../5-dhammacakka/#u10-3), [7: 12.1](../7-anatta/#u12-1), [9: 10.1](../9-fire/#u10-1).*

> Yato **pajānāti** sahetudhamman”ti.

⏏️(yato) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(pajānāti) 🚹⨀②🆎(sahetu+dhamma) ⏏️(iti)  
since / knows / phenomena with their causes / (end quote)  
*`pajānāti`, "knows": `pa` + `jānāti`, the root `ñā` become `jā` {470} with the class's piece `nā` {449}; strip `pa-` and `-nāti` for the root; [3: 6.4](../3-awakening/#u6-4).*

### Exercises

Each of the following, from the texts, contains a form of one of the eleven
verbs. Name the verb and its root, say whether the form is the present, the
past, the absolutive or the past participle, and for a finite form give the
person and number. Check the shape against the tables above.

:::tip[Hint]
You may find the [Digital Pali Dictionary](https://digitalpalidictionary.github.io/)
useful for looking up words, as well as doing the sandhi and compound word
deconstruction.
:::

1. ekapallaṅkena nisīdi
2. Vidhūpayaṁ tiṭṭhati mārasenaṁ
3. aññāsi vata bho, koṇḍañño
4. Bhummānaṁ devānaṁ saddaṁ sutvā
5. ādittan’ti vadāmi
6. kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ
7. atthi nibbuti, na nibbuto pumā
8. nisinno vā ‘nisinnomhī’ti pajānāti

### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > ekapallaṅkena **nisīdi**

🚹⨀③🆎(eka+pallaṅka) 🟢⨀🤟⏮(nisīdati)  
in one cross-legged posture / sat down  
*`nisīdi`, "sat down": the past of `nisīdati`, the ending `-i` {428}, {517} on the present's shape `nisīda-` {609}, without the `a-`; third person singular; [3: 2.2](../3-awakening/#u2-2).*

2. > Vidhūpayaṁ **tiṭṭhati** mārasenaṁ

🚹⨀①🔽(anta)(vidhūpayanta) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(tiṭṭhati) 🚺⨀②🆎(māra+senā)  
scattering / stands / the army of Māra  
*`tiṭṭhati`: the present of the root `ṭhā`, replaced by `tiṭṭha` {468}; third person singular; [3: 12.3](../3-awakening/#u12-3).*

3. > **aññāsi** vata bho, koṇḍañño

🟢⨀🤟⏮(ājānāti) ⏏️(vata) 🚹⨀⓪(bhavant) 🚹⨀①(koṇḍañña)  
has understood / indeed / Bho / Koṇḍañña  
*`aññāsi`, "he understood": the past of `ājānāti`, `ā` + `jānāti`, made like `akāsi`: `a-` {519}, `-si` {491}, the `nā` gone {509}; [5: 14.1](../5-dhammacakka/#u14-1).*

4. > Bhummānaṁ devānaṁ saddaṁ **sutvā**

🚹⨂⑥(bhumma) 🚹⨂⑥(deva) 🚹⨀②(sadda) 🔽(tvā)(suṇāti)  
of the earth-dwelling / devas / the cry / having heard  
*`sutvā`: the absolutive of `suṇāti`, the root `su` with `-tvā` {564}, "having heard"; not a finite verb, so no person or number; [5: 12.2](../5-dhammacakka/#u12-2).*

5. > ādittan’ti **vadāmi**

🚻⨀①🔽(ta)(āditta) ⏏️(iti) 🟢⨀👆▶️(vadati)  
burning / (end quote) / I say  
*`vadāmi`, "I say": the present of `vadati` {445}, first person singular `-mi` {423}, the `a` before it lengthened {478}; [9: 2.6](../9-fire/#u2-6).*

6. > **kataṁ** karaṇīyaṁ

🚻⨀①🔽(ta)(kata) 🚻⨀①🔽(anīya)(karaṇīya)  
done / what had to be done  
*`kataṁ`: the past participle of `karoti`, the `r` of `kara` dropped before `ta` {587}, agreeing with `karaṇīyaṁ` 🚻⨀①; [9: 9.2](../9-fire/#u9-2), [7: 11.4](../7-anatta/#u11-4).*

7. > **atthi** nibbuti, na nibbuto pumā

🟢⨀🤟▶️(atthi) 🚺⨀①(nibbuti) ⏏️(na) 🚹⨀①🔽(ta)(nibbuta) 🚹⨀①(puma)  
there is / extinguishment / not / extinguished / male  
*`atthi`: the root `asa` with `ti` become `tthi` {494}, "there is"; `nibbuti`, what exists, takes ①; third person singular; [Bonus: 7, 1.3](../11-pbt/#s7-u1-3) (Visuddhimagga XVI).*

8. > **nisinno** vā ‘nisinnomhī’ti pajānāti

🚹⨀①🔽(ta)(nisinna) ⏏️(vā) 🚹⨀①🔽(ta)(nisinna) 🟢⨀👆▶️(atthi) ⏏️(iti) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(pajānāti)  
sitting / or / sitting / I am / (end quote) / knows clearly  
*`nisinno`, "sitting": the past participle of `nisīdati`, `-inna` for `-ta` {582}, agreeing with the bhikkhu 🚹⨀①; `nisinnomhi` is `nisinno` + `amhi`, "I am"; [Bonus: 36, 3.1](../11-pbt/#s36-u953-1).*
