The usage examples provided here are not exhaustive, other usages are possible.

A `samāsa` ("compound") is two or more nouns whose meanings are connected joined
into one word, with the `vibhatti` endings of the members elided {316}–{318}.
This section covers the `dvanda` ("copulative compound"), in which the members
are simply combined, as English strings nouns together with "and": the rule that
names it {329}, the rule that makes a `dvanda` of certain kinds of things
singular and neuter {322}, and the rule that makes this optional for other kinds
{323}.

In the analysis phrase each member stands as a separate inflected word with `ca`
("and") after it: `samaṇo ca brāhmaṇo ca`. The compound counts as a noun {601}
and takes a `vibhatti` ending of its own: when it is plural, the ① plural `yo`,
which becomes `ā` after `a` {107}; when it is singular and neuter, the ① singular
`si`, which becomes `aṁ` {219}.

### 4.7.1 A combination of nouns is a `dvanda` {329}

* A combination (`samuccaya`) of nouns that have the same `vibhatti` ending
  (`ekavibhattika`) is named a `dvanda` ("copulative compound").
* All the examples the vutti gives are plural: the compound names its members
  together.

#### Example

> samaṇo ca brāhmaṇo ca → **samaṇabrāhmaṇā**

🚹⨀①(samaṇo) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(brāhmaṇo) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨂①🆎(samaṇa+brāhmaṇa)  
an ascetic / and / a brahmin / and → ascetics and brahmins  
*`samaṇo ca brāhmaṇo ca` ("an ascetic and a brahmin") becomes `samaṇabrāhmaṇā`, a `dvanda`, plural because it names both.*

### 4.7.2 A `dvanda` of limbs, instruments, parts of a team or an army, small creatures, opposites and things of different kinds is singular and neuter {322}

* Likewise (`tathā`), that is, as a `digu` is by {321}, a `dvanda` of limbs
  (`pāṇi`), musical instruments (`tūriya`), parts of a team (`yogga`), parts of
  an army (`senaṅga`), small creatures (`khuddajantuka`), opposites
  (`vividhaviruddha`), things of different kinds (`visabhāga`) and the like
  takes the singular number (`ekatta`) and the neuter gender
  (`napuṁsakaliṅgatta`).
* The mention of "and the like" (`ādi`) takes in the other pairs the vutti
  lists: female and male slaves, woman and man, bowl and robe, sunshade and
  sandals, threes and fours, basket-weavers and chariot-makers, fowlers and
  deer-hunters, long and medium.

The compound is singular even when its members are plural things, and neuter
whatever the gender of its members: the neuter ① singular `si` becomes `aṁ`
{219}, and a long vowel at the end of the compound is shortened, `nāsikā` →
`-nāsikaṁ` {342}. In the vutti's phrases `sotañca`, `maṁsañca` stand for `sotaṁ
ca`, `maṁsaṁ ca`, the niggahīta becoming `ñ` before `ca` by sandhi {31}.

#### Example

> cakkhu ca sotañca → **cakkhusotaṁ**

🚻⨀①(cakkhu) ⏏️(ca) 🚻⨀①(sotaṁ) ⏏️(ca) → 🚻⨀①🆎(cakkhu+sota)  
the eye / and / the ear / and → eye and ear  
*`cakkhu ca sotañca` ("the eye and the ear") becomes `cakkhusotaṁ`, a `dvanda` of limbs, singular and neuter.*

### 4.7.3 Optionally so for trees, grasses, animals, wealth, grain, countries and the like {323}

* A `dvanda` of trees (`rukkha`), grasses (`tiṇa`), animals (`pasu`), wealth
  (`dhana`), grain (`dhañña`), countries (`janapada`) and the like optionally
  (`vibhāsā`) takes the singular number and the neuter gender.
* When the option is not taken, the compound is plural, as a `dvanda` is by
  {329}: each pair has two forms, `ajeḷakaṁ` and `ajeḷakā`.
* The mention of "and the like" (`ādi`) takes in the pairs of opposites the
  vutti lists: the blameworthy and the blameless, the inferior and the
  superior, the dark and the bright.

The option marker `vibhāsā` makes the neuter singular of {322} an alternative to
the plural of {329}; the vutti gives each pair in both forms, the singular
first.

#### Example

> ajo ca eḷako ca → **ajeḷakaṁ**

🚹⨀①(ajo) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(eḷako) ⏏️(ca) → 🚻⨀①🆎(aja+eḷaka)  
a goat / and / a sheep / and → goats and sheep  
*`ajo ca eḷako ca` ("a goat and a sheep") becomes `ajeḷakaṁ`, a `dvanda` of animals, singular and neuter by this rule, the `a` of `aja` elided before `e` {12}.*

> ajo ca eḷako ca → **ajeḷakā**

🚹⨀①(ajo) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(eḷako) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨂①🆎(aja+eḷaka)  
a goat / and / a sheep / and → goats and sheep  
*When the option is not taken, the same phrase becomes `ajeḷakā`, plural as a `dvanda` is by {329}.*
