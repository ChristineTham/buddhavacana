Many compounds in the texts are simply lists. `samaṇabrāhmaṇā` is "ascetics
and brahmins", `candimasūriyā` "moon and sun", `jarāmaraṇaṁ` "aging and
death". Kaccāyana calls such a compound a `dvanda` ("pair"): a combination of
nouns that would all carry the same ending if they stood apart {329}, so that
`samaṇo ca brāhmaṇo ca` ("an ascetic and a brahmin") and the one word
`samaṇabrāhmaṇā` say the same thing. The test is to put "and" between the
members: if each keeps its own meaning and the compound means all of them
together, it is a `dvanda`. There may be more than two members:
`sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā` ("sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure
and despair") strings five.

The next question is which of its two shapes the compound has. Usually it is
plural, because it names more than one thing: `samaṇabrāhmaṇā` ends as
`samaṇā` or `brāhmaṇā` would. But a pair of limbs, of instruments, of natural
enemies or of unlike things, among other kinds, is treated as a single set,
and the compound is then singular and neuter, ending in `-aṁ` whatever the
gender of its members: `cakkhusotaṁ` ("eye and ear") {322}. For trees,
grasses, animals, wealth, grain and countries the texts use either shape,
`ajeḷakaṁ` or `ajeḷakā` ("goats and sheep") {323}.

### How to recognise a `dvanda`

| what you see | what it means | example | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| nouns run together, "and" between them, a plural ending | each member counted: X and Y | `samaṇabrāhmaṇā` (ascetics and brahmins) | {329} |
| the same, but singular and neuter in `-aṁ`, for the kinds of things in the table below | the members as one set | `cakkhusotaṁ` (eye and ear), `pattacīvaraṁ` (bowl and robe) | {322} |
| the same, in either shape, for trees, animals, countries and the like | counted, or as one set | `ajeḷakaṁ` or `ajeḷakā` (goats and sheep) | {323} |

Two small changes can disguise the members. Where a member ending in `a` meets
a vowel, the `a` is dropped and the join hides the second member: `kāka` and
`ulūka` give `kākolūkaṁ` ("crow and owl"), `aja` and `eḷaka` give `ajeḷakaṁ`
{12}. And in the singular neuter a long vowel is shortened, at the end of the
compound, `nāsikā` in `mukhanāsikaṁ` ("mouth and nose") {342}, and at the
join, `dāsī` in `dāsidāsaṁ` ("female and male slaves"). Every rule number
links to the full rule in the Kaccāyana translation; the
[chapter overview](../4-0-samasa/) tables all the rules on compounds.

### Which shape a `dvanda` takes

Kaccāyana lists the kinds of things, with a pair for each:

| kind of things | shape | examples | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| anything not listed below: people, gods, the heavenly bodies | plural | `candimasūriyā` (moon and sun), `sāriputtamoggallānā` (Sāriputta and Moggallāna), `brāhmaṇagahapatikā` (brahmins and householders) | {329} |
| limbs | singular neuter | `mukhanāsikaṁ` (mouth and nose), `chavimaṁsalohitaṁ` (skin, flesh and blood) | {322} |
| instruments | singular neuter | `saṅkhapaṇavaṁ` (conch and drum), `gītavāditaṁ` (singing and music) | {322} |
| farm gear | singular neuter | `phālapācanaṁ` (ploughshare and goad), `yuganaṅgalaṁ` (yoke and plough) | {322} |
| parts of an army | singular neuter | `asicammaṁ` (sword and shield), `hatthiassaṁ` (elephants and horses) | {322} |
| small creatures | singular neuter | `ḍaṁsamakasaṁ` (gadflies and mosquitoes), `kīṭasarīsapaṁ` (insects and reptiles) | {322} |
| natural enemies | singular neuter | `ahinakulaṁ` (snake and mongoose), `kākolūkaṁ` (crow and owl) | {322} |
| unlike things | singular neuter | `samathavipassanaṁ` (calm and insight), `vijjācaraṇaṁ` (knowledge and conduct) | {322} |
| and the like | singular neuter | `pattacīvaraṁ` (bowl and robe), `dāsidāsaṁ` (female and male slaves) | {322} |
| trees, grasses | either | `assatthakapītanaṁ` or `assatthakapītanā` (bodhi tree and kapītana tree), `usīrabīraṇaṁ` or `usīrabīraṇā` (vetiver root and vetiver grass) | {323} |
| animals, wealth, grain | either | `ajeḷakaṁ` or `ajeḷakā` (goats and sheep), `hiraññasuvaṇṇaṁ` or `hiraññasuvaṇṇā` (unwrought and wrought gold), `sāliyavaṁ` or `sāliyavā` (rice and barley) | {323} |
| countries, and the like | either | `kāsikosalaṁ` or `kāsikosalā` (Kāsi and Kosala), `kaṇhasukkaṁ` or `kaṇhasukkā` (the dark and the bright) | {323} |

### Examples

In the phrases that spell a `dvanda` out, `sotañca` is `sotaṁ ca`: the `ṁ`
becomes `ñ` before `ca` {31}.

> samaṇo ca brāhmaṇo ca → **samaṇabrāhmaṇā**

🚹⨀①(samaṇo) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(brāhmaṇo) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨂①🆎(samaṇa+brāhmaṇa)  
an ascetic / and / a brahmin / and → ascetics and brahmins  
*`dvanda` {329}: nouns joined with "and", plural because each member is counted.*

> cakkhu ca sotañca → **cakkhusotaṁ**

🚻⨀①(cakkhu) ⏏️(ca) 🚻⨀①(sotaṁ) ⏏️(ca) → 🚻⨀①🆎(cakkhu+sota)  
the eye / and / the ear / and → eye and ear  
*`dvanda` of limbs {322}: the pair taken as one set, so singular and neuter in `-aṁ`.*

### Exercises

Resolve the following compounds into their members, say which shape of
`dvanda` each has and why, and give the meaning. Look up the words in a
dictionary and take note of the ending each member would take on its own. All
six come from the texts of chapters 3 and 5.

:::tip[Hint]
You may find the [Digital Pali Dictionary](https://digitalpalidictionary.github.io/)
useful for looking up words, as well as doing the sandhi and compound word
deconstruction.
:::

1. nāmarūpaṁ
2. jarāmaraṇaṁ
3. sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā
4. kesamassuṁ
5. vitakkavicārā
6. assāsapassāsā

### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > nāmañca rūpañca → **nāmarūpaṁ**

🚻⨀①(nāmaṁ) ⏏️(ca) 🚻⨀①(rūpaṁ) ⏏️(ca) → 🚻⨀①🆎(nāma+rūpa)  
name / and / form / and → name and form  
*`dvanda` of unlike things {322}, singular and neuter; chapter 3 renders it "phenomenal objects".*

2. > jarā ca maraṇañca → **jarāmaraṇaṁ**

🚺⨀①(jarā) ⏏️(ca) 🚻⨀①(maraṇaṁ) ⏏️(ca) → 🚻⨀①🆎(jarā+maraṇa)  
old age / and / death / and → aging and death  
*`dvanda` {322}, singular and neuter although `jarā` is feminine: the two taken as one set.*

3. > soko ca paridevo ca dukkhañca domanassañca upāyāso ca → **sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā**

🚹⨀①(soko) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(paridevo) ⏏️(ca) 🚻⨀①(dukkhaṁ) ⏏️(ca) 🚻⨀①(domanassaṁ) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(upāyāso) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨂①🆎(soka+parideva+dukkha+domanassa+upāyāsa)  
sorrow / and / lamentation / and / pain / and / displeasure / and / despair / and → sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure and despair  
*`dvanda` {329} of five members, plural; the `a` of `domanassa` is dropped before `upāyāsa` {12}.*

4. > kesā ca massu ca → **kesamassuṁ**

🚹⨂①(kesā) ⏏️(ca) 🚻⨀①(massu) ⏏️(ca) → 🚻⨀①🆎(kesa+massu)  
hair / and / beard / and → hair and beard  
*`dvanda` of limbs {322}, singular and neuter: `kesamassuṁ ohāretvā`, "having shaved off hair and beard".*

5. > vitakko ca vicāro ca → **vitakkavicārā**

🚹⨀①(vitakko) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(vicāro) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨂①🆎(vitakka+vicāra)  
thought / and / consideration / and → thinking and investigating  
*`dvanda` {329}, plural: two processes counted apart; chapter 5 renders them "reflection and consideration".*

6. > assāso ca passāso ca → **assāsapassāsā**

🚹⨀①(assāso) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(passāso) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨂①🆎(assāsa+passāsa)  
in-breath / and / out-breath / and → breathing in and out  
*`dvanda` {329}, plural, the two breaths counted apart; chapter 5 renders it "breathing in and out".*
