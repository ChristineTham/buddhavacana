The usage examples provided here are not exhaustive, other usages are possible.

A `samāsa` ("compound") is two or more nouns whose meanings are connected made
into one word, the `vibhatti` endings of the members elided {316}–{317}. This
section covers the `kammadhāraya` ("appositional compound"), two words with the
same referent {324}, and the seven rules that change the form of a first member
in such a compound: `mahā` for `mahanta` {330}, the masculine form of a feminine
word {331}–{332}, `a` and `ana` for the negative `na` {333}–{334}, and `kada`
and `kā` for `ku` {335}–{336}. The compound is marked 🆎 with its members in
their lemma form, as the chapter's [introduction](../4-0-samasa/) sets out.

The vutti analyses each compound into a phrase of its members as separate
inflected words, "it is X and it is Y": `kaṇho ca so sappo ca` ("it is black and
it is a snake"), the pronoun `so`, `sā` or `taṁ` taking the gender of the noun.
It joins the words of the phrase by sandhi, `nīlañca` for `nīlaṁ ca` (the
niggahīta becomes `ñ` before `c` {31}); the analysis lines below show the words
apart.

### 4.3.1 Two words with the same referent make a `kammadhāraya` {324}

* When two words with the same referent (`tulyādhikaraṇa`) are compounded, that
  compound is named `kammadhāraya` ("appositional compound").
* The two members name one and the same thing (`adhikaraṇa`, "referent"): an
  adjective and its noun, `kaṇho sappo` ("a black snake"), or two nouns,
  `brāhmaṇī dārikā` ("a brahmin woman who is a girl").

A `kammadhāraya` is also a `tappurisa` {326}. The endings of both members are
elided {317}, and the compound takes its own ending like any other noun {601}:
`kaṇhasappa` + `si` → `kaṇhasappo` {104}. Of the vutti's six examples,
`mahāpuriso` is the Example of 4.3.2 below and `khattiyakaññā` that of 4.3.4,
since the form of their first member comes from those rules.

#### Example

> kaṇho ca so sappo ca → **kaṇhasappo**

🚹⨀①(kaṇho) ⏏️(ca) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) 🚹⨀①(sappo) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨀①🆎(kaṇha+sappa)  
black / and / it / a snake / and → a black snake  
*`kaṇho ca so sappo ca` ("it is black and it is a snake") becomes `kaṇhasappo` ("a black snake"), a `kammadhāraya` of an adjective and its noun.*

### 4.3.2 `mahā` for `mahanta` before a word with the same referent {330}

* The word `mahanta` ("great") is replaced by `mahā` before a word with the same
  referent: `mahanto ca so puriso ca` → `mahāpuriso` ("a great man").
* By the mention of the plural (`mahataṁ`, "of the words `mahanta`"), in some
  places `mahanta` is replaced by `maha` instead, and the first consonant of the
  next member is doubled {29}: `mahapphalaṁ` ("of great fruit"), `mahabbalaṁ`
  ("of great strength"), `mahaddhanaṁ` ("of great wealth"), `mahabbhayaṁ`
  ("great danger").

The replacement is made once the endings of the members are elided {317}, so
the feminine `mahantī` (`mahantī ca sā devī ca`) and the neuter `mahantaṁ`
(`mahantañca taṁ balañca`) become `mahā` just as the masculine `mahanto` does.

#### Example

> mahanto ca so puriso ca → **mahāpuriso**

🚹⨀①(mahanto) ⏏️(ca) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) 🚹⨀①(puriso) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨀①🆎(mahanta+purisa)  
great / and / he / a man / and → a great man  
*`mahanto ca so puriso ca` ("he is great and he is a man") becomes `mahāpuriso` ("a great man"), a `kammadhāraya` with `mahā` for `mahanta`.*

Three such compounds, with `maha` and with `mahā`, in one sentence of the
Tipiṭaka ([14S5 11.5.5, Dutiyamahaddhanasutta](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/14S5/11/11.5/11.5.5#2071)):

> aḍḍho **mahaddhano** **mahābhogo** **mahāyaso**

🚹⨀①(aḍḍho) 🚹⨀①🆎(mahanta+dhana) 🚹⨀①🆎(mahanta+bhoga) 🚹⨀①🆎(mahanta+yasa)  
rich / of great wealth / of great possessions / of great fame  
*[He is] rich, of great wealth, of great possessions, of great fame.*

### 4.3.3 A feminine word also spoken as masculine takes the masculine form before a feminine word with the same referent {331}

* If a feminine word that has also been spoken as masculine (`bhāsitapuma`)
  stands before a feminine word with the same referent, it is to be regarded
  just as masculine: it takes its masculine form in the compound, `dīghā` →
  `dīgha` in `dīghajaṅgho`.
* By saying "spoken as masculine" the rule excludes a feminine word that has
  not been spoken as masculine, which keeps its form: `brāhmaṇabandhu ca sā
  bhariyā ca` → `brāhmaṇabandhubhariyā` ("a wife who is a brahmin's kinswoman").

A word like `dīgha` ("long") is spoken as masculine or as feminine according to
its noun. The vutti's three examples are `bahubbīhi` compounds {328}, which name
a third thing, the man who has the quality: `dīghā jaṅghā yassa so` ("he whose
shins are long"). The feminine `dīghā` goes back to `dīgha`; the compound, now
masculine, shortens the final `ā` of `jaṅghā` {403} and takes the ① ending `o`
{104}: `dīghajaṅgho`.

#### Example

> dīghā jaṅghā yassa so → **dīghajaṅgho**

🚺⨂①(dīghā) 🚺⨂①(jaṅghā) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(dīgha+jaṅghā)  
long / shins / whose / he → one whose shins are long  
*`dīghā jaṅghā yassa so` ("he whose shins are long") becomes `dīghajaṅgho` ("one whose shins are long", "long-shinned"), a `bahubbīhi`; the feminine `dīghā` takes the masculine form `dīgha`.*

### 4.3.4 And in a compound named `kammadhāraya` {332}

* And in a compound named `kammadhāraya`, if a feminine word that has formerly
  been spoken as masculine stands before a feminine word with the same
  referent, it is to be regarded just as masculine: `khattiyā ca sā kaññā ca`
  → `khattiyakaññā` ("a khattiya maiden").
* By saying "spoken as masculine" the rule excludes `khattiyabandhu` and
  `brāhmaṇabandhu`, feminine words that have not been spoken as masculine,
  which keep their form: `khattiyabandhudārikā` ("a girl who is a khattiya's
  kinswoman"), `brāhmaṇabandhudārikā` ("a girl who is a brahmin's kinswoman").

The previous rule's examples were `bahubbīhi` compounds; this rule extends it
to the `kammadhāraya`, where the feminine word is itself the first of the two
words with the same referent. The compound is feminine after its last member
`kaññā`, and its `si` ending is elided {220}.

#### Example

> khattiyā ca sā kaññā ca → **khattiyakaññā**

🚺⨀①(khattiyā) ⏏️(ca) *️⃣🤟🚺⨀①(sā) 🚺⨀①(kaññā) ⏏️(ca) → 🚺⨀①🆎(khattiyā+kaññā)  
a khattiya woman / and / she / a maiden / and → a khattiya maiden  
*`khattiyā ca sā kaññā ca` ("she is a khattiya woman and she is a maiden") becomes `khattiyakaññā` ("a khattiya maiden"), a `kammadhāraya`; the feminine `khattiyā` takes the masculine form `khattiya`.*

The compound, in the plural, in the Tipiṭaka ([22J 16.1.9, Sambulājātaka](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/22J/16/16.1/16.1.9#3589)):

> **khattiyakaññā** paṭilobhayanti naṁ

🚺⨂①🆎(khattiyā+kaññā) 🟠⨂🤟▶️(paṭilobhayanti) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀②(naṁ)  
khattiya maidens / entice / him  
*Khattiya maidens entice him.*

### 4.3.5 `a` for `na` in a `tappurisa` {333}

* In a `tappurisa`, the word `na` ("not") becomes `a` before the last member:
  `na brāhmaṇo` → `abrāhmaṇo` ("a non-brahmin").
* A `kammadhāraya` is a `tappurisa` as well {326}, so the rule covers the
  negated compounds of this section; this book marks them as compounds too,
  as in 🚹⨀①🆎(na+brāhmaṇa) below.

`na` is a particle and has no `vibhatti` ending to lose. In `apañcavassaṁ` and
`apañcagavaṁ` the negated member is itself a compound, `pañcavassa` ("five
years") and `pañcagava` ("five cows").

#### Example

> na brāhmaṇo → **abrāhmaṇo**

⏏️(na) 🚹⨀①(brāhmaṇo) → 🚹⨀①🆎(na+brāhmaṇa)  
not / a brahmin → a non-brahmin  
*`na brāhmaṇo` ("not a brahmin") becomes `abrāhmaṇo` ("a non-brahmin"), a `tappurisa` with `a` for `na`.*

The compound in the Tipiṭaka ([10M 5.8, Vāseṭṭhasutta](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/10M/5/5.8#1459)):

> kammunā brāhmaṇo hoti, kammunā hoti **abrāhmaṇo**

🚻⨀③(kammunā) 🚹⨀①(brāhmaṇo) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(hoti) 🚻⨀③(kammunā) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(hoti) 🚹⨀①🆎(na+brāhmaṇa)  
by action / a brahmin / one is / by action / one is / a non-brahmin  
*By action one is a brahmin, by action one is a non-brahmin.*

### 4.3.6 `ana` before a vowel {334}

* In a `tappurisa`, the word `na` is replaced by `ana` when a vowel follows:
  `na asso` → `anasso` ("not a horse").

The final `a` of `ana` is then elided before the vowel of the next member {12},
`ana` + `asso` → `anasso`, `ana` + `issaro` → `anissaro`, so that the negative
shows as `an` before a vowel and as `a` {333} before a consonant.

#### Example

> na asso → **anasso**

⏏️(na) 🚹⨀①(asso) → 🚹⨀①🆎(na+assa)  
not / a horse → not a horse  
*`na asso` ("not a horse") becomes `anasso` ("not a horse"), a `tappurisa` with `ana` for `na` before the vowel `a` {12}.*

1. anissaro
2. anariyo

#### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > na issaro → **anissaro**

⏏️(na) 🚹⨀①(issaro) → 🚹⨀①🆎(na+issara)  
not / a master → not a master  
*`na issaro` ("not a master") becomes `anissaro` ("not a master", "without authority"), a `tappurisa` with `ana` for `na` before the vowel `i` {12}.*

The compound in the Tipiṭaka ([3V 4.26, Pavāraṇāsaṅgaha](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/3V/4/4.26#1116)):

🚹⨀①🆎(na+issara) ⏏️(kho) *️⃣👆⚧⨀⑥(me) *️⃣🤘⚧⨀①(tvaṁ) ⏏️(āvuso) 🚺⨀⑦(pavāraṇāya)  
without authority / indeed / over me / you / friend / in the invitation  
*You, friend, have no authority over me in the invitation [ceremony].*

2. > na ariyo → **anariyo**

⏏️(na) 🚹⨀①(ariyo) → 🚹⨀①🆎(na+ariya)  
not / noble → ignoble  
*`na ariyo` ("not noble") becomes `anariyo` ("ignoble"), a `tappurisa` with `ana` for `na` before the vowel `a` {12}.*

### 4.3.7 `kada` for `ku` before a vowel {335}

* The prefix `ku` ("bad") becomes `kada` when a vowel follows: `kucchitaṁ
  annaṁ` ("despicable food") → `kadannaṁ` ("bad food").
* By saying "before a vowel" the rule excludes a following consonant, before
  which `ku` keeps its form: `kudārā` ("[those] whose wives are bad",
  `kucchitā dārā yesaṁ te`), `kujanā` ("bad people"), and likewise `kuputtā`,
  `kugehā`, `kuvatthā`, `kudāsā`.

The vutti analyses `ku` with the participle `kucchitaṁ` ("despicable"). The
final `a` of `kada` is elided before the vowel of the next member {12}: `kada` +
`annaṁ` → `kadannaṁ`.

#### Example

> kucchitaṁ annaṁ → **kadannaṁ**

🚻⨀①🔽(ta)(kucchitaṁ) 🚻⨀①(annaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(ku+anna)  
despicable / food → bad food  
*`kucchitaṁ annaṁ` ("despicable food") becomes `kadannaṁ` ("bad food"), a `kammadhāraya` with `kada` for `ku` before the vowel `a` {12}.*

### 4.3.8 And `kā` in the sense of "little" {336}

* And this `ku` becomes `kā` in the senses of "little": `appakaṁ lavaṇaṁ`
  ("a little salt") → `kālavaṇaṁ`.
* The plural `appatthesu` ("in the senses of little") is mentioned so that in
  some places `ku` becomes `kā` in other senses as well: `kāpurisā` ("wretched
  men", `kucchitā purisā`).

#### Example

> appakaṁ lavaṇaṁ → **kālavaṇaṁ**

🚻⨀①(appakaṁ) 🚻⨀①(lavaṇaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(ku+lavaṇa)  
little / salt → a little salt  
*`appakaṁ lavaṇaṁ` ("little salt") becomes `kālavaṇaṁ` ("a little salt"), a `kammadhāraya` with `kā` for `ku` in the sense of "little".*
