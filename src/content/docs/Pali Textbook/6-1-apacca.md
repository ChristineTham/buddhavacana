The usage examples provided here are not exhaustive, other usages are possible.

A derived word (`taddhitanāma`) is a noun made from another noun by adding an
affix (`paccaya`) in a stated sense, the `vibhatti` ending of the base and the
word that carried the sense being elided {317}. This section covers the first
sense Kaccāyana treats, the descendant (`apacca`), with its seven affixes,
`ṇa` {344}, `ṇāyana` and `ṇāna` {345}, `ṇeyya` {346}, `ṇi` {347}, `ṇava`
{348} and `ṇera` {349}; and then the rules, which Kaccāyana states at the end
of the chapter, that give every affixed word its shape: the `ṇ` of the affix
is elided {396}, the first vowel is strengthened (`vuddhi`, "strengthening")
{400}, {401}, {402}, {405}, and vowels are lengthened, shortened or otherwise
changed before the affixes {403}, {404}. The shaping rules add no sense of
their own, so each has a bullet and an example and no exercises.
[Appendix H](../h-taddhita/) tables every `taddhita` rule.

### 6.1.1 `ṇa` in the sense of a descendant {344}

* `ṇa` is added to a noun in the ⑥ ending in the sense of its descendant
  (`apacca`): the one who is the descendant of Vasiṭṭha is `vāsiṭṭho`. The
  `ṇ` of the affix marks it as one that strengthens the first vowel {400} and
  is itself elided {396}.
* The rule is optional (`vā`): the phrase `vasiṭṭhassa apaccaṁ` may stand as
  it is instead of the derived word, and the vutti carries this option through
  every rule of the descendant. Each name is given in the three genders:
  masculine `vāsiṭṭho`, feminine `vāsiṭṭhī` with the feminine affix `ī` {239},
  and neuter `vāsiṭṭhaṁ`, agreeing with `apacca`.

#### Example

> vasiṭṭhassa apaccaṁ → **vāsiṭṭho**

🚹⨀⑥(vasiṭṭhassa) 🚻⨀①(apaccaṁ) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇa)(vāsiṭṭho)  
of Vasiṭṭha / descendant → Vasiṭṭha's descendant  
*`vasiṭṭhassa apaccaṁ` ("the descendant of Vasiṭṭha") becomes `vāsiṭṭho` with the affix `ṇa` {344}: the `ṇ` is elided {396} and the first vowel takes `vuddhi` {400}.*

### 6.1.2 `ṇāyana`, `ṇāna` after `vaccha` and the like {345}

* After the clan names of the `vaccha` group, the affixes `ṇāyana` and `ṇāna`
  are added in the sense of a descendant, each name taking either affix: the
  descendant of Vaccha is `vacchāyano` or `vacchāno`. The rule is optional
  (`vā`), as {344} is.
* The vutti lists seventeen names, among them `moggallāno` and `kaccāyano`,
  the family name borne by the author of this grammar, and gives each in the
  three genders, `vacchāyano`, `vacchāyanī`, `vacchāyanaṁ`.

#### Example

> vacchassa apaccaṁ → **vacchāyano**

🚹⨀⑥(vacchassa) 🚻⨀①(apaccaṁ) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇāyana)(vacchāyano)  
of Vaccha / descendant → Vaccha's descendant  
*`vacchassa apaccaṁ` ("the descendant of Vaccha") becomes `vacchāyano` with the affix `ṇāyana` {345}, or `vacchāno` with `ṇāna`: the `ṇ` is elided {396} and the final `a` of `vaccha` is elided before the `ā` of the affix {83}; the first `a` stands before the conjunct `cch` and takes no `vuddhi` {400}.*

### 6.1.3 `ṇeyya` after `kattikā` and the like {346}

* After the clan names of the `kattikā` group, the affix `ṇeyya` is added in
  the sense of a descendant: the descendant of Kattikā is `kattikeyyo`. The
  rule is optional (`vā`).
* The bases are mostly feminine names, in the ⑥ ending `kattikāya`,
  `suciyā`: the final vowel of the base is elided before the `e` of the affix
  {83}, and the first vowel takes `vuddhi` where it can, `vinatā` giving
  `venateyyo` and `suci` giving `soceyyo` {400}.

#### Example

> kattikāya apaccaṁ → **kattikeyyo**

🚺⨀⑥(kattikāya) 🚻⨀①(apaccaṁ) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇeyya)(kattikeyyo)  
of Kattikā / descendant → Kattikā's descendant  
*`kattikāya apaccaṁ` ("the descendant of Kattikā") becomes `kattikeyyo` with the affix `ṇeyya` {346}: the `ṇ` is elided {396} and the final `ā` of `kattikā` is elided before the `e` of the affix {83}; the first `a` stands before the conjunct `tt` and takes no `vuddhi` {400}.*

### 6.1.4 `ṇi` after a lemma in `a` {347}

* After a lemma ending in `a`, the affix `ṇi` is added in the sense of a
  descendant: the descendant of Dakkha is `dakkhi`. The rule is optional
  (`vā`): `dakkhassa apaccaṁ` may stand as it is.
* The word `vā` also admits the affix `ṇika` in the same sense: the descendant
  of the Sakyan's son is `sakyaputti` with `ṇi` or `sakyaputtiko` with `ṇika`.

#### Example

> dakkhassa apaccaṁ → **dakkhi**

🚹⨀⑥(dakkhassa) 🚻⨀①(apaccaṁ) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇi)(dakkhi)  
of Dakkha / descendant → Dakkha's descendant  
*`dakkhassa apaccaṁ` ("the descendant of Dakkha") becomes `dakkhi` with the affix `ṇi` {347}: the `ṇ` is elided {396} and the final `a` of `dakkha` is elided before the `i` of the affix {83}; the first `a` stands before the conjunct `kkh` and takes no `vuddhi` {400}.*

### 6.1.5 `ṇava` after `upaku` and the like {348}

* After `upaku` and the like (`manu`, `bhaggu`, `paṇḍu`, `bahu`), the affix
  `ṇava` is added in the sense of a descendant: the descendant of Upaku is
  `opakavo`, of Manu `mānavo`. The rule is optional (`vā`).
* The final `u` of the base is elided before the `a` of the affix {83}, and
  the first vowel takes `vuddhi` where no conjunct follows it {400}: `upaku`
  gives `opakavo` and `manu` `mānavo`, but `bhaggu` keeps its `a` in
  `bhaggavo`.

#### Example

> manuno apaccaṁ → **mānavo**

🚹⨀⑥(manuno) 🚻⨀①(apaccaṁ) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇava)(mānavo)  
of Manu / descendant → Manu's descendant, a young man  
*`manuno apaccaṁ` ("the descendant of Manu") becomes `mānavo` with the affix `ṇava` {348}, a young man, a brahmin youth: the `ṇ` is elided {396}, the first `a` takes `vuddhi` to `ā` {400} and the final `u` of `manu` is elided before the affix {83}.*

### 6.1.6 `ṇera` after `vidhavā` and the like {349}

* After `vidhavā` and the like, the affix `ṇera` is added in the sense of a
  descendant: the son of a widow (`vidhavā`) is `vedhavero`, the offspring of
  an ascetic (`samaṇa`) is `sāmaṇero`, a novice. The rule is optional (`vā`).
* The vutti gives `sāmaṇero` in the three genders, `sāmaṇero`, `sāmaṇerī` (a
  female novice), `sāmaṇeraṁ`, and likewise `nāḷikero`, `nāḷikerī`,
  `nāḷikeraṁ`.

#### Example

> vidhavāya apaccaṁ → **vedhavero**

🚺⨀⑥(vidhavāya) 🚻⨀①(apaccaṁ) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇera)(vedhavero)  
of a widow / descendant → a widow's son  
*`vidhavāya apaccaṁ` ("the descendant of a widow") becomes `vedhavero` with the affix `ṇera` {349}: the `ṇ` is elided {396}, the first `i` takes `vuddhi` to `e` {400}, {405} and the final `ā` of `vidhavā` is elided before the affix {83}.*

### 6.1.7 The `ṇ` of these affixes is elided {396}

* The `ṇ` written in the affixes `ṇa`, `ṇāyana`, `ṇāna`, `ṇeyya`, `ṇi`, `ṇava`
  and `ṇera`, and in `ṇika`, `ṇya` and `kaṇ` of the later sections, is not
  part of the affix: it is elided, and marks only that the first vowel of the
  base takes `vuddhi` {400}. `ṇa` therefore adds a bare `a`, so that the
  descendant of Gotama is `gotamo`, and `ṇeyya` adds `eyya`, `venateyyo`.

#### Example

> gotamassa apaccaṁ → **gotamo**

🚹⨀⑥(gotamassa) 🚻⨀①(apaccaṁ) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇa)(gotamo)  
of Gotama / descendant → Gotama's descendant  
*`gotamassa apaccaṁ` ("the descendant of Gotama") becomes `gotamo` with the affix `ṇa` {344}, the Buddha's clan name: the `ṇ` is elided {396}, the final `a` of `gotama` is elided before the `a` of the affix {83}, and the first vowel, `o`, has no `vuddhi` to take {405}, so that the derived word has the same form as its base.*

### 6.1.8 `vuddhi` of the first vowel {400}

* Before an affix marked with `ṇ`, the first vowel of the base, or the vowel
  of its first consonant, is optionally strengthened (`vuddhi`): `a` to `ā` in
  `vāsiṭṭho` and `ābhidhammiko`, `i` to `e` in `venateyyo` {405}. The vowel is
  not strengthened when a conjunct consonant follows it: the first `a` of
  `bhaggu`, `manta` and `kuntī` stands before `gg`, `nt`, so `bhaggavo`,
  `manteyyo` and `kunteyyo` keep it.

#### Example

> **ābhidhammiko**

🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(ābhidhammiko)  
one versed in the Abhidhamma  
*`ābhidhammiko` ("one versed in the Abhidhamma") is `abhidhamma` with the affix `ṇika` {351}: the `ṇ` is elided {396} and the initial `a` takes `vuddhi` to `ā` {400}, {405}.*

### 6.1.9 No `vuddhi` of initial `i`, `u`; `e`, `o` inserted instead {401}

* An initial `i` or `u` that stands as the semivowel `y` or `v` before another
  vowel (`byākaraṇa`, `dvāra`) is not strengthened; instead the strengthened
  vowel, `e` or `o`, is inserted in its place, before the semivowel: one who
  studies grammar (`byākaraṇa`) is `veyyākaraṇiko`, one posted at the door
  (`dvāra`) is `dovāriko`.

#### Example

> dvāre niyutto → **dovāriko**

🚻⨀⑦(dvāre) 🚹⨀①🔽(ta)(niyutto) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(dovāriko)  
at the door / appointed → a gatekeeper  
*`dvāre niyutto` ("appointed at the door") becomes `dovāriko` with the affix `ṇika` {351}, a gatekeeper: the `ṇ` is elided {396}, and `o` is inserted before the `v` of `dvāra`, in place of the `vuddhi` of its `u` {401}.*

### 6.1.10 `ā` for initial `i`, `u`, with `ri` inserted {402}

* An initial `i` or `u` also becomes `ā`, and the letter `ri` is inserted
  where it is in place (`ṭhāne`): the state of a sage (`isi`) is `ārisyaṁ`,
  the state of a bull (`usabha`), pre-eminence, is `āsabhaṁ`, straightness
  (`uju`), uprightness, is `ajjavaṁ`. An initial `a` is not affected
  (`āpāyiko`, "bound for a state of loss", has ordinary `vuddhi`), nor are
  the words where usage keeps `e` or `o` (`vematiko`, "in doubt",
  `opanayiko`, "leading onward").

#### Example

> isissa bhāvo → **ārisyaṁ**

🚹⨀⑥(isissa) 🚹⨀①(bhāvo) → 🚻⨀①🔽(ṇa)(ārisyaṁ)  
of a sage / state → the state of a sage  
*`isissa bhāvo` ("the state of a sage") becomes `ārisyaṁ` with the affix `ṇa` in the sense of an abstract state (`bhāva`) {361}: the initial `i` becomes `ā` and `ri` is inserted in its place {402}, and the final `i` of `isi` becomes `y` before the `a` of the affix {21}.*

### 6.1.11 Vowels lengthened and shortened before the affixes {403}

* Sometimes (`kvaci`) the first, middle or last vowel of a word is lengthened
  or shortened, both before an affix and where there is none: the first `a`
  of `magadha` is long in `aṅgamāgadhiko` ("of Aṅga and Magadha"), the first
  vowel of `pākāro` ("a wall") and the last of `khantī` ("patience") are
  lengthened without an affix, and `pageva` ("all the more") has its first
  vowel shortened.

#### Example

> **aṅgamāgadhiko**

🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(aṅgamāgadhiko)  
of Aṅga and Magadha  
*`aṅgamāgadhiko` ("of Aṅga and Magadha") is the compound `aṅga + magadha` with the affix `ṇika`: the middle vowel, the first `a` of `magadha`, is lengthened before the affix {403}.*

### 6.1.12 `vuddhi`, elision, insertion, modification, metathesis and replacement in the affixes {404}

* In the first, middle and last positions of a word, so far as this does not
  conflict with the Conqueror's word (`jinavacana`), there is sometimes
  strengthening (`vuddhi`: `ābhidhammiko`), elision (`lopa`: `tālīsaṁ`,
  "forty", for `cattālīsaṁ`), insertion (`āgama`: the `v` of `vutto`,
  "said"), modification (`vikāra`: the `ā` of `ārisyaṁ`), metathesis
  (`viparīta`: `digu`, with `u` for the `o` of `go`) or replacement (`ādesa`:
  `sabbaseṭṭho`, "best of all", with `sa` for `pasattha`).

#### Example

> **vedallaṁ**

🚻⨀①🔽(la)(vedallaṁ)  
a catechism  
*`vedallaṁ` ("resting on knowledge": a `vedalla`, a discourse of questions and answers) is `vedaṁ` with the affix `la` {358}: the `ṁ` in the middle of the word is elided and a second `l` is inserted at its end {404}.*

### 6.1.13 The `vuddhi` of `a`, `i`, `u` is `ā`, `e`, `o` {405}

* `vuddhi` ("strengthening") names the change of `a` to `ā`, of the `i`-sound
  (`i`, `ī`) to `e` and of the `u`-sound (`u`, `ū`) to `o`: `ābhidhammiko`,
  `venateyyo`, `oḷumpiko` ("one who crosses by raft"); `ā`, `ī` and `ū` count
  as `vuddhi` as well. The term is used by {400}, and the vutti states it here
  again so that the last member of a compound is strengthened too:
  `aṅgamāgadhikā` ("come from Aṅga and Magadha") has `ā` in its second member.

#### Example

> **oḷumpiko**

🚹⨀①🔽(ṇika)(oḷumpiko)  
one who crosses by raft  
*`oḷumpiko` ("one who crosses by raft") is `uḷumpa` ("a raft") with the affix `ṇika` {350}: the `ṇ` is elided {396} and the initial `u` takes `vuddhi` to `o` {400}, {405}.*
