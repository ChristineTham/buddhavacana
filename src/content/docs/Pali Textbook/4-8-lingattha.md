Not every word with the ① ending is the doer of an action. A `kāraka` names
what a noun is doing in relation to an action; the two uses in this section
name none. In the `liṅgattha` ("the meaning of the lemma") use the word
simply names its thing, `puriso` ("a man"); in the `ālapana` ("addressing")
use it calls the person spoken to, `bho purisa` ("sir, man!"), before
anything is asked of them. They open Kaccāyana's list of what each ending
does {284}–{285}, and they close this chapter.

The question to ask is whether the word is aimed at the listener. `rūpaṁ`
("form") and `saṅkhārā` ("mental constructions") just stand there, naming;
`bhikkhave` ("monks!") and `bhante` ("venerable sir") call whoever is listening. This
book marks the second kind ⓪, as chapter 1 does, since its singular usually
differs from the ① form: `purisa` ("man!") beside `puriso` ("a man").

### The two uses of the ① ending

| what the word is doing | what you see | example | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| it simply names its thing, and nothing more is said of it (`liṅgattha`) | the ① ending on a noun or a numeral; or a particle (`nipāta`), which has no gender, number or ending at all and stands under the same rule | `puriso` ("a man"), `purisā` ("men"), `eko` ("one"), `dve` ("two"), `ca` ("and"), `vā` ("or") | {284} |
| it calls the person spoken to (`ālapana`); this book marks it ⓪ | the ① ending with the sense of calling added, usually after a word of address; the singular usually differs from the ① form, the plural is mostly the same | `bho purisa` ("sir, man!"), `bhavanto purisā` ("sirs, men!"), `he sakhe` ("hey, friend!"), `bhikkhave` ("monks!") | {285} |

The ⓪ has no ending of its own: its singular ending is the ① ending `si`,
renamed `ga` for it {57}, which is why it counts as a use of the ① ending
and why its singular looks different, `purisa`, `rāja`, `sakhe`. In the
plural the two are usually one form, `purisā`, `rājāno`, `sakhino`, that
only the sentence tells apart, though `bhikkhu` has `bhikkhave` {116}. The
surest sign of a ⓪ form is the word of address before it, `bho` and `he` in
Kaccāyana, `bhante` in the texts of chapters [7](../7-anatta/) and
the [bonus chapter](../11-pbt/), `āvuso` in the bonus chapter:

| word of address | meaning | where you meet it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bho` | sir | `bho purisa`, `bho rāja`, with a singular {285} |
| `bhavanto` | sirs | `bhavanto purisā`, `bhavanto rājāno`, with a plural {285} |
| `bhante` | venerable sir, another ⓪ form of `bhavanta` | the monks answering the Buddha: `evaṁ, bhante` ("yes, Bhante", [Bonus: 1, 24.15](../11-pbt/#s1-u24-15)), `aniccaṁ, bhante` ([7: 6.2](../7-anatta/#u6-2)) |
| `āvuso` | friend, a particle | one monk to another: `āvuso visākha` ("friend Visākha", [Bonus: 10, 22.2](../11-pbt/#s10-u22-2)) |
| `he`, `ahe`, `re`, `are` | hey!; friend! (to an equal); hey, you! (in contempt); away with you! | `he sakhe` ("hey, friend!") {285}; alone they are particles under {284} |

Every rule number links to the full rule in the Kaccāyana translation;
[Appendix G](../g-vibhatti/) tables the endings themselves, `bhikkhave`
among them {116}.

### Examples

> **puriso**

🚹⨀①(purisa)  
a man  
*"A man" {284}: the ① ending names him, and nothing is said of what he does.*

> **bho purisa**

🚹⨀⓪(bhavant) 🚹⨀⓪(purisa)  
sir / man  
*"Sir, man!" {285}: `purisa`, not `puriso`, after the word of address `bho`; the man is called, not described.*

> **bhavanto purisā**

🚹⨂⓪(bhavant) 🚹⨂⓪(purisa)  
sirs / men  
*"Sirs, men!" {285}: in the plural the ⓪ form is the ① form, `purisā`; `bhavanto` shows the calling.*

### Exercises

listener (⓪), then translate; look the words up in a dictionary and note their
endings.

### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > Evaṁ, **bhante**.

⏏️(evaṁ) 🚹⨀⓪(bhavant)  
yes / Bhante  
*Yes, Bhante.*

`bhante`: the ⓪ form of `bhavanta` {285}, calling the one spoken to; `evaṁ`, a
particle, stands under {284}.

2. > Tayome, **āvuso visākha**, saṅkhārā—

🚹⨂①(ti) *️⃣🤟🚹⨂①(ima) ⏏️(āvuso) 🚹⨀⓪(visākha) 🚹⨂①(saṅkhāra)  
three / these / friend / Visākha / mental constructions  
*Friend Visākha, there are three types of “saṅkhārā” (mental constructions)*

`āvuso visākha`: the word of address and the name called, ⓪ {285}, `visākha`
not `visākho`; `saṅkhārā` simply names, ① plural {284}.

3. > Rūpaṁ, **bhikkhave**, anattā.

🚻⨀①(rūpa) 🚹⨂⓪(bhikkhu) 🚹⨀①🆎(na+atta)  
form / Bhikkhave / not-self  
*`rūpa` (form), Bhikkhave, is `anattā` (not-self).*

`rūpaṁ` and `anattā` simply name, in ① {284}, with no verb between them;
`bhikkhave` calls the listeners, ⓪ {285}.

4. > Cakkhu, **bhikkhave**, ādittaṁ, rūpā ādittā, cakkhuviññāṇaṁ ādittaṁ, cakkhusamphasso āditto.

🚻⨀①(cakkhu) 🚹⨂⓪(bhikkhu) 🚻⨀①🔽(ta)(āditta) 🚻⨂①(rūpa) 🚻⨂①🔽(ta)(āditta) 🚻⨀①🆎(cakkhu+viññāṇa) 🚻⨀①🔽(ta)(āditta) 🚹⨀①🆎(cakkhu+samphassa) 🚹⨀①🔽(ta)(āditta)  
the eye / Bhikkhave / burning / forms / burning / eye-consciousness / burning / eye-contact / burning  
*The `cakkhu` (eye) is burning, `rūpā` (forms) are burning, `cakkhuviññāṇaṁ` (eye-consciousness) is burning, `cakkhusamphasso` (eye-contact) is burning.*

Every word but `bhikkhave` (⓪ {285}) is in ① {284}: `cakkhu`, `rūpā`,
`cakkhuviññāṇaṁ` and `cakkhusamphasso` name their things in four shapes of the
ending, and each `āditta` takes the gender and number of its noun.
