Most verbs and many nouns in the texts begin with one of twenty short words
that Kaccāyana calls `upasagga` (prefixes, 🔼): `viharati` ("dwells") is `vi`
before the root `√har`, `nirodho` ("cessation") `ni` before `√rudh`
("obstruct"), `abhisambuddho` ("fully awakened") `abhi` and `saṁ` before
`√budh` ("wake"). A prefix carries no ending, since {221} drops every ending
after a prefix or a particle, and keeps its place in every form of the
word. [Chapter 1](../1-basics/) sorts the prefixes into those that
follow the root's meaning, those that reverse it (`gacchati` "goes",
`āgacchati` "comes") and those that sharpen it (`jānāti` "knows", `pajānāti`
"knows clearly").

Ask of such a word: what is left when the prefix is taken off, and what did it
add? The Digital Pali Dictionary's `construction` field answers the first
(`vi + hara + ti`), the first table below the second. A prefix may change
shape at the join, and two or three may stand together (`abhisambuddho`); the
second table gives the shapes. The texts' analysis lines and the dictionary treat a prefixed word as
one lemma, `🟢⨀🤟▶️(viharati)`: the prefix is to be seen through, not looked
up.

### The twenty prefixes

In Kaccāyana's order from {221}, with the commonest meaning
([Appendix F](../f-upasagga/) has every sense), one example and the rule that
shapes it at the join, or {221} where nothing changes. The examples are the
texts', except `parābhavo` (Kaccāyana's own) and `dukkaraṁ`, `pidahati`,
`apagacchati` (Appendix F).

| prefix | meaning | example | rule |
| :-: | --- | --- | :-: |
| `pa` | forth; clearly | `pajānāti`, knows clearly | {221} |
| `parā` | away; the reverse | `parābhavo`, ruin | {221} |
| `ni` | down, into | `nirodho`, cessation | {221} |
| `nī` | out | `nissaraṇaṁ`, the way out | {28} |
| `u` | up | `udapādi`, arose | {35} |
| `du` | badly; hard to | `dukkaraṁ`, hard to do | {28} |
| `saṁ` | together; fully | `sambodhi`, full awakening | {31} |
| `vi` | apart; specially | `vimutti`, liberation | {221} |
| `ava` | down, off | `obhāso`, radiance | {50} |
| `anu` | along, after | `ananussutesu`, not heard before | {221} |
| `pari` | all round; fully | `pariññeyyaṁ`, to be fully understood | {28} |
| `adhi` | over; in | `ajjhattaṁ`, inwardly | {45} |
| `abhi` | towards; fully | `abhiññā`, direct knowledge | {221} |
| `pati` | back, against | `paṭipadā`, the way | {48} |
| `su` | well | `suvisuddhaṁ`, fully purified | {221} |
| `ā` | to; the reverse | `āmantesi`, addressed | {221} |
| `ati` | beyond | `atikkamma`, surpassing | {28} |
| `api` | over, on | `pidahati`, closes | {221} |
| `apa` | away | `apagacchati`, goes away | {221} |
| `upa` | near | `upasamāya`, to calm | {221} |

The dictionary writes `u` as `ud`, `du` as `dur` and sometimes `ni` as `nī`
(`nibbāna`, `nī + √vā`), their shapes before a vowel or a doubled consonant;
`api` loses its `a` in `pidahati`.

### What happens at the join

The Sandhikappa ([Appendix E](../e-sandhi/)) says what happens where prefix
and root meet; each rule number links to the full rule.

| what you see | what it was | example | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| `sam`, `saṅ`, `sañ`, `san` | `saṁ`, with the nasal of the next consonant's group; kept before `v`, `s`, `h` | `sambodhi`, `saṅkhārā`, `saññā`, `sandiṭṭhiko`; `saṁvattati` | {31}, {30} |
| `sam` before a vowel | `saṁ` | `samudayo` | {34} |
| a doubled consonant after `vi`, `u`, `pa`, `nī`, `ati` | the next consonant doubled | `vippayogo`, `uppajjati`, `nissaraṇaṁ`, `atikkamma` | {28} |
| `ud` before a vowel | `u` with a `d` inserted | `udapādi`, `udānesi` | {35} |
| a final vowel gone | a vowel dropped before a vowel | `upādāna` (`upa` + `ādāna`) | {12} |
| `abbh` before a vowel | `abhi` | `abbhuggacchi` | {44} |
| `ajjh` before a vowel | `adhi` | `ajjhattaṁ`, `ajjhagamā` | {45} |
| `pacc` before a vowel | `pati`, the `ti` become `cc` | `paccaññāsiṁ`, `paccayā` | {19} |
| `paṭi` | `pati`, in most words | `paṭipadā`, `paṭicca`, `paṭilabhi` | {48} |
| `o` before a consonant | `ava` | `obhāso`, `ohāretvā` | {50} |

:::note[`anu`, or `an` + `u`?]
Not every `anu-` is the prefix `anu`: the negative `an-` can stand before a
prefix beginning with `u`. `anupagamma` ("not approaching", chapter 4) is
`an` + `upa` + `√gam` and `anuttaraṁ` ("unsurpassed") `an` + `u` + `tara`,
while `ananussutesu` is `an` + `anu` + `√su`; the `construction` field
settles it.
:::

### Examples

> Tatra kho bhagavā pañcavaggiye bhikkhū **āmantesi**—

⏏️(tatra) ⏏️(kho) 🚹⨀①(bhagavant) 🚹⨂②🆎(pañca+vaggiya) 🚹⨂②(bhikkhu) 🟢⨀🤟⏮(āmanteti)  
there / indeed / the Bhagavā / of the group of five / bhikkhū / addressed  
*`ā` ("to") + `√mant` ("counsel"): counsel turned towards someone; unchanged before a consonant, and the `-esi` is the verb's ending, not the prefix's {221}.*

> appiyehi **sampayogo** dukkho, piyehi **vippayogo** dukkho

🚹⨂③🆎(na+piya) 🚹⨀①(sampayoga) 🚹⨀①(dukkha) 🚹⨂③(piya) 🚹⨀①(vippayoga) 🚹⨀①(dukkha)  
with the unloved / association / suffering / from the loved / separation / suffering  
*One word, `pa` + `√yuj` ("yoke"), under two prefixes: `saṁ` ("together") gives association, `vi` ("apart") separation. Before `p` the `ṁ` becomes `m` {31}; after `vi` the `p` doubles {28}.*

> **upasamāya** **abhiññāya** **sambodhāya** nibbānāya **saṁvattati**

🚹⨀④(upasama) 🚺⨀④(abhiññā) 🚹⨀④(sambodha) 🚻⨀④(nibbāna) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(saṁvattati)  
to calm / to direct knowledge / to enlightenment / to extinguishment / leads  
*`saṁ` twice: `sam` before `b` {31}, `saṁ` kept before `v` {30}. `upasamāya` is `upa` ("near") + `√sam` ("be calm"), `abhiññāya` `abhi` ("fully") + `√ñā` ("know"), `nibbānāya` `nī` + `√vā` ("blow").*

> pubbe **ananussutesu** dhammesu cakkhuṁ **udapādi**

⏏️(pubbe) 🚹⨂⑦🔽(ta)🆎(na+anussuta) 🚹⨂⑦(dhamma) 🚻⨀①(cakkhu) 🟢⨀🤟⏮(uppajjati)  
before / not heard / in dhammas / insight / arose  
*`udapādi` is `u` ("up") + `√pad` ("go"), "went up": a `d` is inserted after `u` before a vowel {35}, and the `a` after it is the `a` put before the root in the past {519}. `ananussutesu` is `an` + `anu` + `√su` ("hear"), "not heard along".*

### Exercises

Each word below is from the texts of chapters 3, 4 and 10. Name its prefix,
the shape it has at the join and the root or word after it, and say what it
adds to the meaning, and check the dictionary's `construction` field.

:::tip[Hint]
You may find the [Digital Pali Dictionary](https://digitalpalidictionary.github.io/)
useful for looking up words, as well as doing the sandhi and compound word
deconstruction.
:::

1. pajānāti
2. viharati
3. saṅkhārā
4. pariññeyyaṁ
5. paccaññāsiṁ
6. nissaraṇaṁ
7. ajjhattaṁ
8. uppajjati

### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > **pajānāti**

🟢⨀🤟▶️(pajānāti)  
knows  
*`pa` + `√ñā` ("know"): `pa` sharpens the root, "knows clearly"; nothing changes at the join. Chapter 3.*

2. > **viharati**

🟢⨀🤟▶️(viharati)  
dwells  
*`vi` + `√har` ("live" in the dictionary; chapter 1's `hāra` table reads `vihāro` as carrying oneself about, staying). Chapter 3.*

3. > **saṅkhārā**

🚹⨂①(saṅkhāra)  
mental constructions  
*`saṁ` ("together") + `√kar` ("make"): the `ṁ` becomes `ṅ` before `kh` {31}. Chapter 3.*

4. > **pariññeyyaṁ**

🚻⨀①🔽(ṇya)(pariññeyya)  
to be fully understood  
*`pari` ("all round") + `√ñā` ("know"): the `ñ` doubles after the short `i` {28}; `-eyya` says "to be … -ed" (chapter 8). Chapter 4.*

5. > **paccaññāsiṁ**

🟢⨀👆⏮(paṭijānāti)  
claimed  
*`pati` ("back") + `√ñā` ("know"): `pati` becomes `pacc` before a vowel {19}, and the `a` after it marks the past {519}: "knew back", claimed. Chapter 4.*

6. > **nissaraṇaṁ**

🚻①⨀(nissaraṇa)  
way out of  
*`nī` ("out") + `√sar` ("go"), "going out": the `s` doubles after the prefix {28} and the `ī` written short. Chapter 10.*

7. > **ajjhattaṁ**

🚻①⨀(ajjhatta)  
internally  
*`adhi` ("in") + `atta` ("self"): `adhi` becomes `ajjh` before a vowel {45}, "in oneself"; an `abyayībhāva` ([7.2](../7-2-abyayibhava/)). Chapter 10.*

8. > **uppajjati**

▶️🤟⨀(uppajjati)  
arise  
*`u` ("up") + `√pad` ("go"), "goes up": the `p` doubles after `u` {28}; its past `udapādi` has the `d` {35}. Chapter 10.*
