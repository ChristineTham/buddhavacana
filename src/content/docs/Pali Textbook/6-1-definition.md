Pāḷi runs nouns together into one word: `rañño putto` ("the king's son")
becomes `rājaputto`. Kaccāyana calls this a `samāsa` ("compound"): nouns
joined because their meanings belong together {316}. At the join each member
loses its ending {317}, and a member that had changed shape for it goes back
to its lemma, the dictionary form {318}: `rañño` is `rāja` again. The whole
then takes one ending, like any noun. Meeting a long word, ask which words it
holds and what ending it carries.

### How to recognise a compound

| what you see | what it means | example | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| nouns run together as one word | a `samāsa`: their meanings belong together | `rājaputto`, the king's son | {316} |
| no ending inside, one at the end | the members' endings dropped | the `-o` of `rājaputto` | {317} |
| a member in its dictionary form | back in its original form | `rāja` for `rañño` | {318} |
| a first member's `ṁ` kept as `ṅ` or `n` | a kept ending, in a few names | `pabhaṅkaro`, maker of light: `pabhaṁ` + `kara` | {317}, {31} |

### Examples

> rañño putto → **rājaputto**

🚹⨀⑥(rañño) 🚹⨀①(putto) → 🚹⨀①🆎(rāja+putta)  
of the king / son → the king's son  
*Endings of `rañño` and `putto` dropped {317}, `rañño` back to its lemma `rāja` {318}, one ending on the whole.*

### Exercises

Resolve each of these words from the Bodhikathā ([chapter 3](../3-awakening/))
and the Fire Sermon ([chapter 9](../9-fire/)) into its members and say which
ending the whole carries; each is linked to the sentence it stands in.

:::tip[Hint]
You may find the [Digital Pali Dictionary](https://digitalpalidictionary.github.io/)
useful for looking up words, as well as doing the sandhi and compound word
deconstruction.
:::

1. mārasenaṁ ([3: 12.3](../3-awakening/#u12-3))
2. jarāmaraṇaṁ ([3: 3.11](../3-awakening/#u3-11))
3. ekapallaṅkena ([3: 2.2](../3-awakening/#u2-2))
4. bodhirukkhamūle ([3: 2.1](../3-awakening/#u2-1))
5. saṅkhāranirodho ([3: 4.1](../3-awakening/#u4-1))
6. cakkhuviññāṇaṁ ([9: 2.3](../9-fire/#u2-3))

### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > mārassa senā → **mārasenaṁ**

🚹⨀⑥(mārassa) 🚺⨀①(senā) → 🚺⨀②🆎(māra+senā)  
of Māra / army → the army of Māra  
*`māra` (Māra) and `senā` (army): `mārassa` loses its ending; ② on the whole.*

2. > jarā ca maraṇaṁ ca → **jarāmaraṇaṁ**

🚺⨀①(jarā) ⏏️(ca) 🚻⨀①(maraṇaṁ) ⏏️(ca) → 🚻⨀①🆎(jarā+maraṇa)  
aging / and / death / and → aging and death  
*`jarā` (aging) and `maraṇa` (death), joined as "and"; ① on the whole.*

3. > eko pallaṅko → **ekapallaṅkena**

🚹⨀①(eko) 🚹⨀①(pallaṅko) → 🚹⨀③🆎(eka+pallaṅka)  
one / cross-legged posture → in one cross-legged posture  
*`eka` (one) and `pallaṅka` (cross-legged posture): endings dropped; ③ on the whole.*

4. > bodhirukkhassa mūle → **bodhirukkhamūle**

🚹⨀⑥🆎(bodhi+rukkha) 🚻⨀⑦(mūle) → 🚻⨀⑦🆎(bodhirukkha+mūla)  
of the Bodhi tree / at the root → at the root of the Bodhi tree  
*`bodhirukkha` (Bodhi tree, itself `bodhi` + `rukkha`) and `mūla` (root): ⑦ on the whole.*

5. > saṅkhārānaṁ nirodho → **saṅkhāranirodho**

🚹⨂⑥(saṅkhārānaṁ) 🚹⨀①(nirodho) → 🚹⨀①🆎(saṅkhāra+nirodha)  
of mental constructions / cessation → the cessation of mental constructions  
*`saṅkhāra` (mental construction) and `nirodha` (cessation): `saṅkhārānaṁ` loses its ending; ① on the whole.*

6. > cakkhusmiṁ viññāṇaṁ → **cakkhuviññāṇaṁ**

🚻⨀⑦(cakkhusmiṁ) 🚻⨀①(viññāṇaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(cakkhu+viññāṇa)  
in the eye / consciousness → eye-consciousness  
*`cakkhu` (eye) and `viññāṇa` (consciousness): `cakkhusmiṁ` loses its ending; ① on the whole, and the same shape for the other five senses.*
