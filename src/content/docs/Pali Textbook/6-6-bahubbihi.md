Some compounds name neither of their members. `chinnahattho` is `chinna` ("cut
off") and `hattha` ("hand"), yet it is not a hand and not a cutting: it is "a
man whose hand is cut off", `chinnahattho puriso`. Kaccāyana calls a compound
made "in the sense of another word" a `bahubbīhi` {328}; the name is itself
one, "much rice", said of a place that has it. The word named stands outside
the compound, which describes it as an adjective does.

So the question to ask when you meet a compound is: does it name one of its
own members, or something else? If something else, find the noun it agrees
with and unfold the compound with "whose", "by whom" or "in which". The
compound takes the gender, number and ending of that noun: `chinnahattho` is
🚹⨀① because `puriso` is. When the members join, their endings are dropped
{317} and each goes back to its lemma form {318}. The Buddha's epithets are
full of such compounds (`dasabalo`, "who has the ten powers"), and so is the
jhāna formula of [Bonus: 39, 11.1](../11-pbt/#s39-u11-1) (`avitakkaṁ avicāraṁ …
jhānaṁ`).

### How to recognise a `bahubbīhi`

The vutti of {328} first shows the outside word in each ending from ② to ⑦:

| the outside word is | example | the phrase it stands for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ② the thing reached | `āgatasamaṇo` | `āgatā samaṇā imaṁ saṅghārāmaṁ`: a monastery to which wanderers have come |
| ③ the doer | `jitindriyo` | `jitāni indriyāni anena samaṇena`: a wanderer by whom the senses are conquered |
| ④ the receiver | `dinnasuṅko` | `dinno suṅko yassa rañño`: a king to whom tax is paid |
| ⑤ the starting point | `niggatajano` | `niggatā janā asmā gāmā`: a village from which the people have gone out |
| ⑥ the owner | `chinnahattho` | `chinno hattho yassa purisassa`: a man whose hand is cut off |
| ⑦ the place | `sampannasasso` | `sampannāni sassāni yasmiṁ janapade`: a country in which the crops have ripened |

The ⑥ shape, "whose", is by far the commonest; the vutti's other examples sort
by the first member:

| the first member is | example | what it names |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a participle in `-ta` | `chinnahattho`, `jitindriyo` | see the table above |
| an adjective, often `a`- for `na` {333} | `anantañāṇo` | whose knowledge is endless |
| a numeral | `catuddiso`, `pañcacakkhu`, `dasabalo` | with the four directions, five eyes, ten powers |
| a comparison with `iva` or `viya` ("like") | `suvaṇṇavaṇṇo`, `brahmassaro` | whose colour is like gold's; whose voice is like Brahmā's |
| a compound nested inside | `amitaghanasarīro` | whose solid body is measureless (`ghanasarīra`, a `kammadhāraya`, inside) |

The name serves one later rule {167}: a `bahubbīhi` ending in a pronoun,
`piyapubbā` ("whose former [spouse] was dear"), keeps the ordinary noun
endings. Every rule number links to the full rule; the
[chapter overview](../6-0-samasa/) tables them all.

### Examples

> chinno hattho yassa purisassa so → **chinnahattho** puriso

🚹⨀①🔽(ta)(chinna) 🚹⨀①(hattha) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(ya) 🚹⨀⑥(purisa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(ta) → 🚹⨀①🆎(chinna+hattha) 🚹⨀①(purisa)  
cut off / hand / of whom / of the man / that [one] → whose hand is cut off / man  
*{328}: not a hand but the man whose hand it is; the compound takes the ending of `puriso`.*

> jitāni indriyāni anena samaṇena so → **jitindriyo** samaṇo

🚻⨂①🔽(ta)(jita) 🚻⨂①(indriya) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀③(ima) 🚹⨀③(samaṇa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(ta) → 🚹⨀①🆎(jita+indriya) 🚹⨀①(samaṇa)  
conquered / senses / by this / by the wanderer / that [one] → by whom the senses have been conquered / wanderer  
*{328}: the outside word is the doer, ③; `jita` + `indriya` gives `jitindriya`, `a` dropped before `i` {12}.*

> vipariṇāmo dhammo yassa taṁ → yaṁ … **vipariṇāmadhammaṁ**

🚹⨀①(vipariṇāma) 🚹⨀①(dhamma) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀⑥(ya) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(ta) → *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(ya) 🚻⨀①🆎(vipariṇāma+dhamma)  
change / nature / of which / that → what / subject to change  
*{328}: `-dhamma`, "whose nature is …", as `maraṇadhammo` below; neuter like the `yaṁ` ("what") it describes, the `rūpaṁ` asked about in [7: 6.5](../7-anatta/#u6-5).*

### Exercises

Each of the following is a `bahubbīhi` from chapters [5](../5-dhammacakka/) and
the [bonus chapter](../11-pbt/), with the word it agrees with where the sentence has one and a
link to its sentence. Look each up in a dictionary, name its members, unfold it
into a phrase with `yassa` ("whose") or `yasmiṁ` ("in which"), and say what it
names.

### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > nirodho dhammo yassa taṁ → **nirodhadhammaṁ**

🚹⨀①(nirodha) 🚹⨀①(dhamma) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀⑥(ya) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(ta) → 🚻⨀①🆎(nirodha+dhamma)  
cessation / nature / of which / that → subject to cessation  
*{328}: `-dhamma`, "whose nature is …", as `vipariṇāmadhammaṁ`; neuter like the `taṁ` it describes, "whatever is subject to origination" ([5: 11.1](../5-dhammacakka/#u11-1)).*

2. > maraṇaṁ dhammo yassa so → **maraṇadhammo**

🚻⨀①(maraṇa) 🚹⨀①(dhamma) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(ya) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(ta) → 🚹⨀①🆎(maraṇa+dhamma)  
death / nature / of whom / that [one] → whose nature is death, liable to death  
*{328}: `-dhammo`, "whose nature is": so too `jātidhammo`, `jarādhammo`, `byādhidhammo`, `sokadhammo`, `saṅkilesadhammo` in [Bonus: 47, 18.1](../11-pbt/#s47-u18-1).*

3. > natthi vitakko yasmiṁ taṁ → **avitakkaṁ** jhānaṁ

⏏️(na) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(atthi) 🚹⨀①(vitakka) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀⑦(ya) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(ta) → 🚻⨀①🆎(na+vitakka) 🚻⨀①(jhāna)  
not / there is / thinking / in which / that → free from thought / jhāna  
*{328}: `a`- for `na` {333}, the outside word in the ⑦; neuter like the `jhānaṁ` it describes ([Bonus: 39, 11.1](../11-pbt/#s39-u11-1)).*

4. > natthi vicāro yasmiṁ taṁ → **avicāraṁ** jhānaṁ

⏏️(na) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(atthi) 🚹⨀①(vicāra) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀⑦(ya) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(ta) → 🚻⨀①🆎(na+vicāra) 🚻⨀①(jhāna)  
not / there is / discursive thought / in which / that → free from discursive thought / jhāna  
*{328}: the twin of `avitakkaṁ` in the same formula: `vicāra` ("discursive thought") is absent from the second `jhānaṁ`.*
