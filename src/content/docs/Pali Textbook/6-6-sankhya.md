The usage examples provided here are not exhaustive, other usages are possible.

A derived word (`taddhitanāma`, "secondary derivative") is a noun made from
another noun by adding an affix (`paccaya`) in a stated sense {344}–{405}.
This section covers the numerals (`saṅkhyā`): the affixes that complete a
number (`saṅkhyāpūraṇa`, the ordinal), `ma` {373}, `ī` {375}, `tha` and `ṭha`
{384} and `tiya` {385}, with the replacements that go with them {374}, {377},
{386}–{387}; the rules that shape the cardinal numbers from eleven to ninety,
a hundred, a thousand and beyond {376}, {378}–{383}, {388}–{391},
{393}–{395}; the affix `ka` for a group {392}; and the indeclinable affixes
(`abyaya`), `dhā` and `so` in the sense of division {397} and `thā`, `thattā`
and `thaṁ` in the sense of manner {398}–{399}, which make words without
gender or `vibhatti` ending, marked ⏏️🔽 in this book. Appendix H tables
every `taddhita` rule with its examples.

### 6.6.1 `ma` for the ordinal {373}

* The affix `ma` is added to a numeral in the ⑥ ending in the sense of
  completing a number (`saṅkhyāpūraṇa`, the ordinal): the completer of five is
  `pañcamo` ("fifth"); so `sattamo` ("seventh"), `aṭṭhamo` ("eighth"),
  `navamo` ("ninth"), `dasamo` ("tenth").
* The ordinal is an adjective and takes the gender, number and `vibhatti`
  ending of its noun.

#### Example

> pañcannaṁ pūraṇo → **pañcamo**

⚧⨂⑥(pañcannaṁ) 🚹⨀①(pūraṇo) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ma)(pañcamo)  
of five / the completer → the fifth  
*`pañcannaṁ pūraṇo` ("the completer of five") becomes `pañcamo` ("the fifth") with the affix `ma` in the sense of completing a number {373}: the ⑥ ending of the base and the word `pūraṇo` are elided {317}, and the new word takes the ① ending `si`, which becomes `o` {104}.*

### 6.6.2 `sa` for `cha` {374}

* In the sense of the ordinal, `cha` ("six") is optionally replaced by `sa`:
  the completer of six is `saṭṭho` or `chaṭṭho` ("sixth"), with the affix
  `ṭha` of {384} and its `ṭh` doubled {29}.

#### Example

> channaṁ pūraṇo → **saṭṭho**

⚧⨂⑥(channaṁ) 🚹⨀①(pūraṇo) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ṭha)(saṭṭho)  
of six / the completer → the sixth  
*`channaṁ pūraṇo` ("the completer of six") becomes `saṭṭho` ("the sixth") with the affix `ṭha` {384}: `cha` is replaced by `sa` {374}, the `ṭh` is doubled {29}, and the ① ending `si` becomes `o` {104}; without the replacement, `chaṭṭho`.*

of six / the completer → the sixth  
*`channaṁ pūraṇo` ("the completer of six") becomes `chaṭṭho` ("the sixth") with the affix `ṭha` {384}, the `ṭh` doubled {29}; the replacement of `cha` by `sa` {374} is optional and here not made.*

### 6.6.3 `ī` after `dasa` with `eka` and the like, the feminine ordinal {375}

* After `eka` ("one") and the like, the affix `ī` is optionally added at the
  end of `dasa` ("ten") in the sense of completing a number, making the
  feminine ordinal: the completer of eleven is `ekādasī` ("the eleventh");
  so `pañcadasī` ("the fifteenth"), `cātuddasī` ("the fourteenth").
* By saying "completing a number" the rule excludes the cardinals `ekādasa`
  ("eleven") and `pañcadasa` ("fifteen"), which are `eko ca dasa ca`, "one
  and ten", made into one word.

#### Example

> ekādasannaṁ pūraṇī → **ekādasī**

⚧⨂⑥🆎(eka+dasa) 🚺⨀①(pūraṇī) → 🚺⨀①🔽(ī)(ekādasī)  
of eleven / the completer → the eleventh  
*`ekādasannaṁ pūraṇī` ("the completer of eleven", feminine) becomes `ekādasī` ("the eleventh") with the affix `ī` {375}: the ⑥ ending of the base and the word `pūraṇī` are elided {317}, the final `a` of `dasa` is elided before the affix {83}, and the feminine ① ending `si` is elided {220}.*

of fifteen / the completer → the fifteenth  
*`pañcadasannaṁ pūraṇī` ("the completer of fifteen") becomes `pañcadasī` ("the fifteenth") with the affix `ī` {375}, the final `a` of `dasa` elided before it {83}.*

*`catuddasannaṁ pūraṇī` ("the completer of fourteen") becomes `cātuddasī` ("the fourteenth") with the affix `ī` {375}: the `d` of `dasa` is doubled after `catu` {28} and the first vowel is lengthened {403}.*

### 6.6.4 `so` for `cha` before `dasa` {376}

* When `dasa` ("ten") follows, `cha` ("six") always (`niccaṁ`) becomes `so`:
  `soḷasa` ("sixteen"), the `d` of `dasa` becoming `ḷ` {379}.

#### Example

> **soḷasa**

⚧⨂①🆎(cha+dasa)  
sixteen  
*`cha` ("six") with `dasa` ("ten") makes `soḷasa` ("sixteen"): `cha` always becomes `so` before `dasa` {376}, and the `d` of `dasa` becomes `ḷ` {379}; the numeral is one form for all three genders.*

### 6.6.5 A niggahīta at the end {377}

* At the end of these numerals a niggahīta `ṁ` is inserted: `pañcadasiṁ` ("on
  the fifteenth [day]"), `cātuddasiṁ` ("on the fourteenth"), the feminine
  ordinals of 6.6.3 with their `ī` shortened {403}.

#### Example

> **pañcadasiṁ**

🚺⨀②🔽(ī)(pañcadasiṁ)  
on the fifteenth  
*`pañcadasī` ("the fifteenth", 6.6.3) takes a niggahīta at its end {377}, the `ī` shortened before it {403}: `pañcadasiṁ` ("on the fifteenth [day]"); likewise `cātuddasiṁ` ("on the fourteenth").*

### 6.6.6 `ti` at the end {378}

* At the end of the tens, `ti` is added: `vīsati` ("twenty"), `tiṁsati`
  ("thirty"), beside `vīsaṁ` and `tiṁsaṁ` (6.6.17); the niggahīta is elided
  before it {39}.

#### Example

> **vīsati**

⚧⨀①🔽(ti)(vīsati)  
twenty  
*`vīsaṁ` ("twenty", 6.6.17) takes `ti` at the end {378}, its niggahīta elided before it {39}: `vīsati`; likewise `tiṁsati` ("thirty").*

### 6.6.7 `l` for `d` and `r` {379}

* In the numerals, the letters `d` and `r` are replaced by `l`: `soḷasa`
  ("sixteen", `d` to `ḷ`), `cattālīsaṁ` ("forty", `r` to `l`).

#### Example

> **cattālīsaṁ**

⚧⨀①(cattālīsaṁ)  
forty  
*Four `dasa` ("ten") reduced to one {388} become `cattāra` before the ending `yo`, and the `yo` becomes `īsaṁ` {389}; the final `a` of `cattāra` is elided before `ī` {12} and its `r` becomes `l` {379}: `cattālīsaṁ` ("forty"), while in `soḷasa` ("sixteen", 6.6.4) it is the `d` of `dasa` that becomes `ḷ`.*

### 6.6.8 `bā` for `dvi` before `vīsati`, `dasa`; `du`, `di`, `do` {380}

* Before `vīsati` ("twenty") and `dasa` ("ten"), `dvi` ("two") is replaced by
  `bā`: `bāvīsati` ("twenty-two"), `bārasa` ("twelve", with `r` for the `d`
  of `dasa` {381}).
* By the mention of `tu` ("and further"), `dvi` is also replaced by `du`, `di`
  and `do`: `durattaṁ` and `dirattaṁ` ("two nights"), `diguṇaṁ` ("double"),
  `dohaḷinī` ("a woman with the longing of pregnancy", `do` for `dvi` in
  `dohaḷa`). The first three are taken up again at {386}, 6.6.14.

#### Example

> **bāvīsatindriyāni**

🚻⨂①🆎(dvi+vīsati+indriya)  
the twenty-two faculties  
*`dvi` ("two") before `vīsati` ("twenty") becomes `bā` {380}: `bāvīsati` ("twenty-two"), joined to `indriyāni` ("faculties") by sandhi, its final `i` elided before the `i` of `indriya` {12}: `bāvīsatindriyāni` ("the twenty-two faculties").*

### 6.6.9 `r` for the `d` of `dasa` {381}

* In a numeral, the `d` of `dasa` ("ten") after `eka` ("one") and the like is
  optionally replaced by `r`: `ekārasa` or `ekādasa` ("eleven"), `bārasa` or
  `dvādasa` ("twelve").
* By saying "in a numeral" the rule excludes a compound with a noun:
  `dvādasāyatanāni` ("the twelve sense bases") keeps its `d`.

#### Example

> **ekārasa**

⚧⨂①🆎(eka+dasa)  
eleven  
*`eka` ("one") with `dasa` ("ten") makes `ekārasa` ("eleven"): the final `a` of `eka` becomes `ā` {383} and the `d` of `dasa` becomes `r` {381}; without the change of `d`, `ekādasa`.*

### 6.6.10 After `aṭṭha` and the like {382}

* After `aṭṭha` ("eight") and the like too, the `d` of `dasa` is optionally
  replaced by `r` in a numeral: `aṭṭhārasa` or `aṭṭhadasa` ("eighteen").
* Not after `pañca` or `cha` (`pañcadasa`, `soḷasa`), and not in a derivative
  that is no numeral (`aṭṭhadasiko`, "connected with eighteen").

#### Example

> **aṭṭhārasa**

⚧⨂①🆎(aṭṭha+dasa)  
eighteen  
*`aṭṭha` ("eight") with `dasa` ("ten") makes `aṭṭhārasa` ("eighteen"): the final `a` of `aṭṭha` becomes `ā` {383} and the `d` of `dasa` becomes `r` {382}; without the change, `aṭṭhadasa`.*

### 6.6.11 `ā` for the final of `dvi`, `eka`, `aṭṭha` {383}

* In a numeral, the final vowel of `dvi` ("two"), `eka` ("one") and `aṭṭha`
  ("eight") optionally becomes `ā`: `dvādasa` ("twelve"), `ekādasa`
  ("eleven"), `aṭṭhārasa` ("eighteen").
* Not in a compound that is no numeral: `dvidanto` ("two-tusked"), `ekadanto`
  ("one-tusked"), `ekacchanno` ("covered with a single [roof]"),
  `aṭṭhatthambho` ("having eight pillars") keep their short vowel.

#### Example

> **dvādasa**

⚧⨂①🆎(dvi+dasa)  
twelve  
*`dvi` ("two") with `dasa` ("ten") makes `dvādasa` ("twelve"): the final `i` of `dvi` becomes `ā` {383}; with `bā` for `dvi` {380} and `r` for the `d` {381} the word is `bārasa` (6.6.8).*

### 6.6.12 `tha`, `ṭha` after `catu`, `cha` {384}

* After `catu` ("four") and `cha` ("six"), the affixes `tha` and `ṭha` are
  used in the sense of completing a number: `catuttho` ("fourth"), `chaṭṭho`
  ("sixth", 6.6.2), the consonant doubled {29}.

#### Example

> catunnaṁ pūraṇo → **catuttho**

⚧⨂⑥(catunnaṁ) 🚹⨀①(pūraṇo) → 🚹⨀①🔽(tha)(catuttho)  
of four / the completer → the fourth  
*`catunnaṁ pūraṇo` ("the completer of four") becomes `catuttho` ("the fourth") with the affix `tha` {384}: the `th` is doubled {29}, and the ① ending `si` becomes `o` {104}.*

### 6.6.13 `tiya` after `dvi`, `ti` {385}

* After `dvi` ("two") and `ti` ("three"), the affix `tiya` is used in the
  sense of completing a number: `dutiyo` ("second"), `tatiyo` ("third"), with
  `du` and `ta` for `dvi` and `ti` {386}.

#### Example

> dvinnaṁ pūraṇo → **dutiyo**

⚧⨂⑥(dvinnaṁ) 🚹⨀①(pūraṇo) → 🚹⨀①🔽(tiya)(dutiyo)  
of two / the completer → the second  
*`dvinnaṁ pūraṇo` ("the completer of two") becomes `dutiyo` ("the second") with the affix `tiya` {385}: `dvi` becomes `du` before it {386}, and the ① ending `si` becomes `o` {104}.*

### 6.6.14 `du`, `ta` before `tiya` {386}

* Before the affix `tiya`, `dvi` ("two") is replaced by `du` and `ti`
  ("three") by `ta`: `dutiyo` ("second"), `tatiyo` ("third"), the ordinals of
  6.6.13.
* By the mention of `api` ("also"), `dvi` becomes `du` elsewhere too,
  `durattaṁ` ("two nights"); and by the mention of `ca` ("and") it becomes
  `di`: `dirattaṁ` ("two nights"), `diguṇaṁ saṅghāṭiṁ pārupetvā` ("having put
  on the double outer robe").

#### Example

> **durattaṁ**

🚻⨀①🆎(dvi+ratti)  
two nights  
*`dvi` ("two") with `ratti` ("night") makes `durattaṁ` ("two nights"), a `digu` ("numeral compound"): `dvi` becomes `du` {386}, the final `i` of `ratti` becomes `a` at the end of the compound {337}, and the compound is neuter and singular {321}, its ① ending `si` becoming `aṁ` {219}.*

### 6.6.15 `aḍḍha` with the ordinals {387}

* With `aḍḍha` ("half") as their accompanying word (`upapada`), `catuttha`
  ("fourth"), `dutiya` ("second") and `tatiya` ("third") are replaced by
  `aḍḍhuḍḍha` ("three and a half"), `divaḍḍha` or `diyaḍḍha` ("one and a
  half") and `aḍḍhatiya` ("two and a half"), each formed together with the
  `aḍḍha`: "the fourth with a half" counts three whole and a half.

#### Example

> aḍḍhena catuttho → **aḍḍhuḍḍho**

🚻⨀③(aḍḍhena) 🚹⨀①🔽(tha)(catuttho) → 🚹⨀①🆎(aḍḍha+catuttha)  
with a half / the fourth → three and a half  
*`aḍḍhena catuttho` ("the fourth with a half", three and a half) becomes `aḍḍhuḍḍho`: with `aḍḍha` ("half") as the accompanying word, `catuttha` is replaced by `aḍḍhuḍḍha`, formed together with the `aḍḍha` {387}, and the ① ending `si` becomes `o` {104}.*

### 6.6.16 Of like forms one alone remains {388}

* When words or sounds of the same form occur more than once (`asakiṁ`), one
  alone remains (`ekasesa`): `puriso ca puriso ca` ("a man and a man") is
  `purisā` ("men"), the one form left taking the plural ending. The rule
  serves the tens of 6.6.17, where the repeated `dasa` is reduced to one
  before it is replaced.
* Words of different form are not reduced: `hatthī ca asso ca ratho ca
  pattiko ca` ("an elephant, a horse, a chariot and a foot-soldier") makes
  the compound `hatthiassarathapattikā`; nor is a word that occurs once,
  `puriso` ("a man").

#### Example

> puriso ca puriso ca → **purisā**

🚹⨀①(puriso) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(puriso) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨂①(purisā)  
a man / and / a man / and → men  
*`puriso ca puriso ca` ("a man and a man") becomes `purisā` ("men"): of the two like forms one alone remains {388}, and it takes the plural ending `yo`, which becomes `ā` {107}, the final `a` of `purisa` being elided before it {83}.*

### 6.6.17 The forms of twenty to ninety {389}

* In counting, `dasa` ("ten") taken two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight
  or nine times is reduced to one {388} and replaced before the ending `yo`
  by `vī`, `ti`, `cattāra`, `paññā`, `cha`, `satta`, `asa` and `nava` in that
  order, and the `yo` by `īsaṁ`, `āsaṁ`, `ṭhi`, `ri` or `ti`, `īti` and
  `uti`: `vīsaṁ` ("twenty"), `tiṁsaṁ` ("thirty"), `cattālīsaṁ` ("forty"),
  `paññāsaṁ` ("fifty"), `saṭṭhi` ("sixty"), `sattari` or `sattati`
  ("seventy"), `asīti` ("eighty"), `navuti` ("ninety").
* A single `dasa` ("ten") is not changed, nor `dasa` repeated outside
  counting: `dasadasakā purisā` ("men by tens").

*Two `dasa` ("ten") reduced to one {388} become `vī` before the ending `yo`, and the `yo` becomes `īsaṁ` {389}; the two `ī` join by sandhi {12}: `vīsaṁ` ("twenty").*

### 6.6.18 `catu` before `dasa` and the rest: `cuddasa`, `tālīsaṁ` {390}

* In some places (`navā`), when `catu` ("four") stands as the accompanying
  word of a numeral, its `tu` is elided and the `ca` left at the head of the
  word becomes `cu` or `co`: `cuddasa`, `coddasa` or `catuddasa`
  ("fourteen").
* By the mention of `api` ("too"), the `ca` at the head of a word is elided,
  or becomes `cu` or `co`, even where `catu` is not an accompanying word:
  `tālīsaṁ`, `cuttālīsaṁ`, `cottālīsaṁ` beside `cattālīsaṁ` ("forty").

#### Example

> **cuddasa**

⚧⨂①🆎(catu+dasa)  
fourteen  
*`catu` ("four") with `dasa` ("ten") makes `cuddasa` ("fourteen"): the `tu` of `catu` is elided and its `ca` becomes `cu` {390}, and the `d` of `dasa` is doubled {28}; with `co`, `coddasa`; without the rule, `catuddasa`.*

### 6.6.19 Forms established by direct statement {391}

* Words for which no rule has been stated (`aniddiṭṭhalakkhaṇa`), whether in
  their letters, words and consonants, their gender, their class as noun,
  prefix or particle, as `abyayībhāva` compound, `taddhita` derivative or
  verb, in counting and numerals, tense, `kāraka`, usage and technical terms,
  in `sandhi`, the natural state, `vuddhi`, elision, insertion, modification
  and metathesis, or in the assignment of `vibhatti` endings, are established
  by direct statement (`nipātana`): the grammar states the form itself.
* The rule gives no example of its own; the next rule, {392}, invokes it for
  `dvisataṁ` and the rest.

### 6.6.20 `ka` after `dvi` and the like for a group {392}

* After `dvi` ("two") and the rest, the affix `ka` is used in the sense of
  more than one (`anekattha`), a group of that many: `satassa dvikaṁ` ("a
  pair of hundreds") is `dvisataṁ` ("two hundred"), and so `tisataṁ`,
  `catusataṁ`, `pañcasataṁ`, `chasataṁ`, `sattasataṁ`, `aṭṭhasataṁ`,
  `navasataṁ`, `dasasataṁ` ("ten hundred"), which is `sahassaṁ` ("a
  thousand"); the forms are established by direct statement (`nipātana`).

#### Example

> satassa dvikaṁ → **dvisataṁ**

🚻⨀⑥(satassa) 🚻⨀①🔽(ka)(dvikaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(dvi+sata)  
of a hundred / a pair → two hundred  
*`satassa dvikaṁ` ("a pair of hundreds") is `dvisataṁ` ("two hundred"): `dvi` ("two") takes the affix `ka` in the sense of a group {392}, the endings are elided {317}, and the form with `sata` ("hundred") as its last member is established by direct statement {392}, {393}, neuter and singular with the ending `aṁ` {219}.*

### 6.6.21 A hundred and a thousand {393}

### 6.6.22 The higher numbers by tens {394}

* Beyond these numerals, each next number is made by multiplying by ten: ten
  multiplied by ten is `sataṁ` ("a hundred"), a hundred by ten `sahassaṁ` ("a
  thousand"), then `dasasahassaṁ` ("ten thousand"), `satasahassaṁ` ("a
  hundred thousand"), `dasasatasahassaṁ` ("a million") and `koṭi` ("ten
  million"); a hundred thousand `koṭi` multiplied by a hundred is `pakoṭi`,
  and the rest are to be applied in the same way.

> sahassassa dasaguṇitaṁ katvā → **dasasahassaṁ**

🚻⨀⑥(sahassassa) 🚻⨀②🆎(dasa+guṇita) 🔽(tvā)(katvā) → 🚻⨀①🆎(dasa+sahassa)  
of a thousand / multiplied by ten / having made → ten thousand  
*`sahassassa dasaguṇitaṁ katvā` ("having multiplied a thousand by ten") gives `dasasahassaṁ` ("ten thousand") {394}: `dasa` ("ten") with `sahassa` ("thousand"), a `digu`, neuter and singular {321}.*

### 6.6.24 `dhā` in the sense of division; and `so` {397}

  by rule"), `byañjanaso` ("letter by letter"), `padaso` ("word by word").

*`ekena vibhāgena` ("by one division") becomes `ekadhā` ("in one way") with the affix `dhā` in the sense of division {397}: the ③ ending of the base and the word `vibhāgena` are elided {317}, and the derived word is an indeclinable, its own ① ending `si` elided {221}.*
