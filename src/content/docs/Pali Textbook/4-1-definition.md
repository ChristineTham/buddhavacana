The usage examples provided here are not exhaustive, other usages are possible.

A `samāsa` ("compound") is a joining of nouns whose meanings are connected into
one word: `rañño putto` ("the son of the king") becomes `rājaputto`. This
section covers the three rules that say what a compound is and what happens to
its members: nouns whose meanings are connected are named `samāsa` {316}, the
`vibhatti` endings of the members are elided {317}, and a member whose lemma
ends in a vowel returns to its original form {318}. The kinds of compound that
the answers name, `tappurisa`, `kammadhāraya` and `dvanda`, are defined by
their own rules in sections [4.5](../4-5-tappurisa/),
[4.3](../4-3-kammadharaya/) and [4.7](../4-7-dvanda/).

### 4.1.1 A compound is a joining of nouns whose meanings are connected {316}

* Of nouns (`nāma`) whose word-meanings are being employed together, whatever
  has a connected meaning (`yuttattha`) is named `samāsa` ("compound"):
  `kathinassa dussaṁ` ("cloth for the `kathina` [frame]") is made into the one
  word `kathinadussaṁ`.
* Only nouns are compounded, and only when their meanings are connected to
  each other: a noun with a verb is a sentence, not a compound, and nouns that
  merely stand side by side without a connected meaning stay separate (the
  counter-examples below).

The name `samāsa` is used again by {337}, the rule on the final of a compound
(section [4.8](../4-8-samasanta/)). The derivation below runs through the next
two rules, {317} and {318}, and the compound then takes a `vibhatti` ending of
its own: for a neuter noun in `a` the `si` ending becomes `aṁ` {219}.

#### Example

> kathinassa dussaṁ → **kathinadussaṁ**

🚻⨀⑥(kathinassa) 🚻⨀①(dussaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(kathina+dussa)  
of the kathina / cloth → kathina cloth  
*`kathinassa dussaṁ` ("the cloth of the `kathina`", cloth for the `kathina` [frame]) becomes `kathinadussaṁ`, a `tappurisa` of the ⑥ ending.*

| | kathinassa + dussaṁ (kathina + sa ⑥⨀, dussa + si ①⨀) | rule |
| --- | --- | :-: |
| → | kathina + ~~sa~~ + dussa + ~~si~~ | {317} |
| → | kathina + dussa | {318} |
| → | kathinadussa + si (①⨀) | |
| → | kathinadussa + ~~si~~(aṁ) | {219} |
| → | **kathinadussaṁ** | |

The compound in the Tipiṭaka ([Kathinānujānana](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/3V/7/7.1#1391)):

> idaṁ saṁghassa **kathinadussaṁ** uppannaṁ

*️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(idaṁ) 🚹⨀⑥(saṁghassa) 🚻⨀①🆎(kathina+dussa) 🚻⨀①(🔽(ta)(uppannaṁ))  
this / of the Saṅgha / kathina cloth / arisen  
*This kathina cloth of the Saṅgha [has] arisen.*

By saying `nāmānaṁ` ("of nouns") we exclude a noun with a verb:

> devadatto pacati

🚹⨀①(devadatto) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(pacati)  
Devadatta / cooks  
*Devadatta cooks: a noun with a verb is a sentence, not a compound.*

> yaññadatto pacati

🚹⨀①(yaññadatto) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(pacati)  
Yaññadatta / cooks  
*Yaññadatta cooks.*

By saying `yuttattho` ("whose meanings are connected") we exclude nouns that
merely stand side by side without a connected meaning:

> bhato rañño putto devadattassa

🚹⨀①(bhato) 🚹⨀⑥(rañño) 🚹⨀①(putto) 🚹⨀⑥(devadattassa)  
the servant / of the king / the son / of Devadatta  
*The king's servant, Devadatta's son: `rañño` goes with `bhato` and `devadattassa` with `putto`, so `rañño` and `putto` do not make `rājaputto`.*

#### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > āgantukassa bhattaṁ → **āgantukabhattaṁ**

🚹⨀⑥(āgantukassa) 🚻⨀①(bhattaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(āgantuka+bhatta)  
of a newcomer / food → food for newcomers  
*`āgantukassa bhattaṁ` ("the food of a newcomer", food for newcomers) becomes `āgantukabhattaṁ`, a `tappurisa` of the ⑥ ending.*

2. > jīvitaṁ eva indriyaṁ → **jīvitindriyaṁ**

🚻⨀①(jīvitaṁ) ⏏️(eva) 🚻⨀①(indriyaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(jīvita+indriya)  
life / itself / the faculty → the life faculty  
*`jīvitaṁ eva indriyaṁ` ("the faculty that is life itself") becomes `jīvitindriyaṁ`, a `kammadhāraya`; the final `a` of `jīvita` is elided before the `i` of `indriya` {12}.*

3. > samaṇo ca brāhmaṇo ca → **samaṇabrāhmaṇā**

🚹⨀①(samaṇo) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(brāhmaṇo) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨂①🆎(samaṇa+brāhmaṇa)  
an ascetic / and / a brahmin / and → ascetics and brahmins  
*`samaṇo ca brāhmaṇo ca` ("an ascetic and a brahmin") becomes `samaṇabrāhmaṇā`, a `dvanda`, which stands in the plural.*

4. > **sāriputtamoggallānā**

🚹⨂①🆎(sāriputta+moggallāna)  
Sāriputta and Moggallāna  
*`sāriputtamoggallānā` joins the names `sāriputta` and `moggallāna`: "Sāriputta and Moggallāna", a `dvanda`.*

5. > **brāhmaṇagahapatikā**

🚹⨂①🆎(brāhmaṇa+gahapatika)  
brahmins and householders  
*`brāhmaṇagahapatikā` joins `brāhmaṇa` ("brahmin") and `gahapatika` ("householder"): "brahmins and householders", a `dvanda`.*

6. > **jīvitindriyaṁ** upacchindati

🚻⨀②🆎(jīvita+indriya) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(upacchindati)  
the life faculty / cuts off  
*[He] cuts off the life faculty.*

7. > santi, bhante, **samaṇabrāhmaṇā** lajjino kukkuccakā sikkhākāmā

🟢⨂🤟▶️(santi) 🚹⨀⓪(bhante) 🚹⨂①🆎(samaṇa+brāhmaṇa) 🚹⨂①(lajjino) 🚹⨂①(kukkuccakā) 🚹⨂①🆎(sikkhā+kāma)  
there are / venerable sir / ascetics and brahmins / scrupulous / conscientious / desirous of training  
*There are, venerable sir, ascetics and brahmins [who are] scrupulous, conscientious and desirous of training.*

### 4.1.2 The inflections of the members are elided {317}

* When nouns whose meanings are connected are compounded, the `vibhatti`
  endings of the members are elided: in `kathinadussaṁ` the `sa` of
  `kathinassa` and the `si` of `dussaṁ`, in `āgantukabhattaṁ` ("food for
  newcomers") the `sa` of `āgantukassa` and the `si` of `bhattaṁ`.
* By the mention of `tesaṁ` ("of those"), the inflections, suffixes, words,
  letters and insertions (`āgama`) of compounds, secondary derivatives
  (`taddhita`), verbs (`ākhyāta`) and primary derivatives (`kitaka`) are
  elided as well: in `vāsiṭṭho` ("a descendant of Vasiṭṭha") the `ṇ` of the
  suffix `ṇa` is elided, in `venateyyo` ("Vinatā's son") the `ṇ` of `ṇeyya`.
* The word `ca` ("and") in the rule is for restriction (`avadhāraṇa`): in some
  compounds the ending of the first member is kept. `pabhaṅkaro` ("the maker
  of light"), `amatandado` ("the giver of the deathless"), `medhaṅkaro` ("the
  maker of wisdom", a former Buddha) and `dīpaṅkaro` ("the maker of light",
  the Buddha Dīpaṅkara) keep the `aṁ` of `pabhaṁ`, `amataṁ`, `medhaṁ` and
  `dīpaṁ`, whose `ṁ` becomes the nasal of the consonant that follows {31}.

Once the endings are elided, the members stand in their lemma form {318} and
the compound takes a `vibhatti` ending of its own, as the derivation shows.

#### Example

> āgantukassa bhattaṁ → **āgantukabhattaṁ**

🚹⨀⑥(āgantukassa) 🚻⨀①(bhattaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🆎(āgantuka+bhatta)  
of a newcomer / food → food for newcomers  
*`āgantukassa bhattaṁ` ("the food of a newcomer", food for newcomers) becomes `āgantukabhattaṁ`: the `sa` of `āgantukassa` and the `si` of `bhattaṁ` are elided, and the compound takes its own `si`, which becomes `aṁ` in the neuter {219}.*

| | āgantukassa + bhattaṁ (āgantuka + sa ⑥⨀, bhatta + si ①⨀) | rule |
| --- | --- | :-: |
| → | āgantuka + ~~sa~~ + bhatta + ~~si~~ | {317} |
| → | āgantuka + bhatta | {318} |
| → | āgantukabhatta + si (①⨀) | |
| → | āgantukabhatta + ~~si~~(aṁ) | {219} |
| → | **āgantukabhattaṁ** | |

The compound in the Tipiṭaka ([Visākhāvatthu](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/3V/8/8.18#1655)):

> **āgantukabhattaṁ** dātuṁ, gamikabhattaṁ dātuṁ

🚻⨀②🆎(āgantuka+bhatta) 🔽(tuṁ)(dātuṁ) 🚻⨀②🆎(gamika+bhatta) 🔽(tuṁ)(dātuṁ)  
food for newcomers / to give / food for travellers / to give  
*To give food for newcomers, to give food for travellers (`dātuṁ`, "to give", is the infinitive in `tuṁ`; `gamikabhattaṁ`, "food for travellers", is a compound formed in the same way).*

Resolve the following compounds into their members and say which `vibhatti`
ending is kept; translate the sentences.

1. pabhaṅkaro
2. amatandado
3. medhaṅkaro
4. dīpaṅkaro
5. esa devamanussānaṁ, sammūḷhānaṁ pabhaṅkaro
6. taṇhaṅkaro medhaṅkaro, athopi saraṇaṅkaro; dīpaṅkaro ca sambuddho
7. vāsiṭṭho mayā diṭṭho

#### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > pabhaṁ karo → **pabhaṅkaro**

🚺⨀②(pabhaṁ) 🚹⨀①(karo) → 🚹⨀①🆎(pabhaṁ+kara)  
light / the maker → the maker of light  
*`pabhaṁ karo` ("the maker of light") becomes `pabhaṅkaro`, a `tappurisa` of the ② ending in which the `aṁ` of `pabhaṁ` is kept, its `ṁ` becoming `ṅ` before `k` {31}.*

2. > **amatandado**

🚹⨀①🆎(amataṁ+dada)  
the giver of the deathless  
*`amatandado` ("the giver of the deathless") keeps the `aṁ` of `amataṁ` ("the deathless", ②) before `dada` ("giving"), the `ṁ` becoming `n` before `d` {31}.*

3. > **medhaṅkaro**

🚹⨀①🆎(medhaṁ+kara)  
the maker of wisdom  
*`medhaṅkaro` ("the maker of wisdom", a former Buddha) keeps the `aṁ` of `medhaṁ` ("wisdom", ②) before `kara` ("maker").*

4. > **dīpaṅkaro**

🚹⨀①🆎(dīpaṁ+kara)  
the maker of light  
*`dīpaṅkaro` ("the maker of light", the Buddha Dīpaṅkara) keeps the `aṁ` of `dīpaṁ` ("light", ②) before `kara` ("maker").*

5. > esa devamanussānaṁ, sammūḷhānaṁ **pabhaṅkaro**

*️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(esa) 🚹⨂⑥🆎(deva+manussa) 🚹⨂⑥(🔽(ta)(sammūḷhānaṁ)) 🚹⨀①🆎(pabhaṁ+kara)  
this one / of gods and men / bewildered / the maker of light  
*This one [is] the maker of light for bewildered gods and men.*

6. > **taṇhaṅkaro medhaṅkaro**, athopi **saraṇaṅkaro**; **dīpaṅkaro** ca sambuddho

🚹⨀①🆎(taṇhaṁ+kara) 🚹⨀①🆎(medhaṁ+kara) ⏏️(athopi) 🚹⨀①🆎(saraṇaṁ+kara) 🚹⨀①🆎(dīpaṁ+kara) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(sambuddho)  
Taṇhaṅkara / Medhaṅkara / and also / Saraṇaṅkara / Dīpaṅkara / and / the Fully Awakened  
*Taṇhaṅkara, Medhaṅkara, and also Saraṇaṅkara, and Dīpaṅkara the Fully Awakened [One]: four former Buddhas, each name keeping the `aṁ` of its first member (`taṇhā`, "craving"; `saraṇa`, "refuge").*

7. > vāsiṭṭho mayā diṭṭho

🚹⨀①(🔽(ṇa)(vāsiṭṭho)) *️⃣👆⚧⨀③(mayā) 🚹⨀①(🔽(ta)(diṭṭho))  
Vāsiṭṭha / by me / seen  
*Vāsiṭṭha [was] seen by me: `vāsiṭṭho` is not a compound but a secondary derivative (`taddhita`), `vasiṭṭha` with the suffix `ṇa`, whose `ṇ` is elided by the same rule.*

### 4.1.3 A member ending in a vowel returns to its original form {318}

* When the `vibhatti` endings have been elided, a member whose lemma (`liṅga`)
  ends in a vowel has its original form (`pakatirūpa`): `rañño` gives back
  `rāja` in `rājaputto` ("the king's son") and `rājapuriso` ("the king's
  man"), `sotaṁ` gives back `sota` in `cakkhusotaṁ` ("eye and ear"), and
  `mukhaṁ` gives back `mukha` in `mukhanāsikaṁ` ("mouth and nose").

The member is then the bare lemma, the word as a dictionary lists it, which is
why this book writes the members of a compound in lemma form, 🚹⨀①🆎(rāja+putta).
The compound takes its own ending: for a masculine noun in `a` the `si` ending
becomes `o` {104}.

#### Example

> rañño putto → **rājaputto**

🚹⨀⑥(rañño) 🚹⨀①(putto) → 🚹⨀①🆎(rāja+putta)  
of the king / son → the king's son  
*`rañño putto` ("the son of the king") becomes `rājaputto`, a `tappurisa` of the ⑥ ending: `rañño` gives back the lemma `rāja`.*

| | rañño + putto (rāja + sa ⑥⨀, putta + si ①⨀) | rule |
| --- | --- | :-: |
| → | rāja + ~~sa~~ + putta + ~~si~~ | {317} |
| → | rāja + putta | {318} |
| → | rājaputta + si (①⨀) | |
| → | rājaputta + ~~si~~(o) | {104} |
| → | **rājaputto** | |

The compound in the Tipiṭaka ([Aggasāvakayuga](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/7D/1/1.13#157)):

> ayaṁ kho khaṇḍo ca **rājaputto** tisso ca purohitaputto

*️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(ayaṁ) ⏏️(kho) 🚹⨀①(khaṇḍo) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①🆎(rāja+putta) 🚹⨀①(tisso) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①🆎(purohita+putta)  
this / indeed / Khaṇḍa / and / the king's son / Tissa / and / the chaplain's son  
*This Khaṇḍa, the king's son, and Tissa, the chaplain's son, … (`purohitaputto`, "the chaplain's son", is a compound of the same form).*

Resolve the following compounds into their members, name the type of

1. cakkhusotaṁ

#### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > cakkhu ca sotañca → **cakkhusotaṁ**

🚻⨀①(cakkhu) ⏏️(ca) 🚻⨀①(sotaṁ) ⏏️(ca) → 🚻⨀①🆎(cakkhu+sota)  
the eye / and / the ear / and → eye and ear  
*`cakkhu ca sotañca` ("the eye and the ear"; `sotañca` is `sotaṁ ca` in sandhi) becomes `cakkhusotaṁ`, a `dvanda`: `sotaṁ` gives back `sota`.*

2. > mukhañca nāsikā ca → **mukhanāsikaṁ**

🚻⨀①(mukhaṁ) ⏏️(ca) 🚺⨀①(nāsikā) ⏏️(ca) → 🚻⨀①🆎(mukha+nāsikā)  
the mouth / and / the nose / and → mouth and nose  
*`mukhañca nāsikā ca` ("the mouth and the nose"; `mukhañca` is `mukhaṁ ca` in sandhi) becomes `mukhanāsikaṁ`, a `dvanda`: `mukhaṁ` gives back `mukha`.*

3. > rañño puriso → **rājapuriso**

🚹⨀⑥(rañño) 🚹⨀①(puriso) → 🚹⨀①🆎(rāja+purisa)  
of the king / man → the king's man  
*`rañño puriso` ("the man of the king") becomes `rājapuriso`, a `tappurisa` of the ⑥ ending: `rañño` gives back `rāja`.*

4. > te, ānanda, **rājapurisā** ghaṭikāraṁ kumbhakāraṁ upasaṅkamitvā

*️⃣🤟🚹⨂①(te) 🚹⨀⓪(ānanda) 🚹⨂①🆎(rāja+purisa) 🚹⨀②(ghaṭikāraṁ) 🚹⨀②🆎(kumbha+kāra) 🔽(tvā)(upasaṅkamitvā)  
those / Ānanda / the king's men / Ghaṭikāra / the potter / having approached  
*Those king's men, Ānanda, having approached Ghaṭikāra the potter, … (`upasaṅkamitvā` is the absolutive in `tvā`; `kumbhakāraṁ`, "the potter", is `kumbha`, "pot", with `kāra`, "maker").*
