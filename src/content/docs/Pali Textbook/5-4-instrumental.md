Many sentences name the thing an action is done with: the sickle the rice is
reaped with, the eye a form is seen with. Kaccāyana calls that thing the `karaṇa` ("the instrument") {279}, and the noun
that names it takes the ③ ending {286}: `dattena` ("with a sickle") from
`datta`, `cakkhunā` ("with the eye") from `cakkhu`, `agginā` ("with fire") from
`aggi`. English says "with" or "by".

The ending does more work than this. When the verb is passive, "the man
killed by the king", the one who did the killing stands in ③ {288}; a ③ noun
goes with `saha` and `saddhiṁ` ("together with") {287}; and ③ can also give
the reason for an action, the time at which it happens, the limb a defect
sits in or the clan a person is known by {289}–{292}. The question to ask of
a ③ noun is "with what, or by whom?", and if neither fits, "why, or when?".

The ③ ending looks like `-ena` (`kāyena`, "with the body"), `-āya`
(`vijjāya`, "by knowledge"), `-inā` and `-unā` (`agginā`, `cakkhunā`) or a
bare `-ā` (`raññā`, "by the king"; `manasā`, "with the mind"), and in the
plural `-ehi`, `-ebhi` or `-hi` (`viññūhi`, "by the wise");
[Appendix G](../g-vibhatti/) tables them all.

### The uses of the ③ ending

| what the noun is doing | what you see | example | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| the instrument: the thing the action is done, seen or heard with | a ③ noun beside the verb, "with", "by" | `dattena vīhiṁ lunāti` ("[he] reaps the rice with a sickle") | {279}, {286} |
| accompanied by: the companion of `saha` or `saddhiṁ` ("together with"), `vinā` ("without") or `samaṁ` ("equal to") | one of those words next to a ③ noun | `mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ` ("together with a great community of monks") | {287} |
| the doer (`kattā`) of a passive verb: the one by whom the thing was done | a ③ noun with a passive verb, often a word in `-ta` such as `hato` ("killed") | `raññā hato poso` ("the man [was] killed by the king") | {288} |
| the cause (`hetu`): the reason for the action | a ③ noun that answers "why?", "because of" | `annena vasati` ("[he] stays because of food") | {289} |
| the time, in the sense of the ⑦ ending | `tena samayena`, the opening of countless passages | `tena kālena` ("at that time") | {290} |
| the limb a defect sits in | a part of the body in ③ beside the word for the defect | `akkhinā kāṇo` ("one-eyed in the eye") | {291} |
| the distinguishing attribute (`visesana`): the clan or quality someone is known by | a ③ noun beside a name or a word of praise | `gottena gotamo nātho` ("the Protector [is] a Gotama by clan") | {292} |

Three rules elsewhere touch the same ground. The ② ending is sometimes found
where ③ is expected {307} ([5.5](../5-5-accusative/)); with `maṇḍita`
("gladdened") and `ussukka` ("zealous") either ③ or ⑦ is used {315}
([5.3](../5-3-locative/)); and the doer of a passive verb sometimes takes the
⑥ ending instead, `kato me kalyāṇo` ("good has been done by me") {308}
([5.7](../5-7-genitive/)). With an active verb the doer takes the ① ending
{284} ([5.6](../5-6-agent/)).

### Examples

The instrument {279}, {286}:

> **dattena** vīhiṁ lunāti

🚻⨀③(dattena) 🚹⨀②(vīhiṁ) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(lunāti)  
with a sickle / the rice / [he] reaps  
*[He] reaps the rice with a sickle.*

Accompanied by {287}:

> sahā’pi **gaggena** saṅgho uposathaṁ kareyya

⏏️(saha) ⏏️(api) 🚹⨀③(gaggena) ⨀(🚹①(saṅgho) 🚹②(uposathaṁ) 🟢🤟⏯(kareyya))  
together with / even / Gagga / the Saṅgha / the uposatha (observance day ceremony) / may perform  
*The Saṅgha may perform the uposatha even together with Gagga.*

The doer of a passive verb {288}:

> **raññā** hato poso

🚹⨀③(raññā) ⨀🚹①(🔽(ta)(hato) poso)  
by the king / killed / the man  
*The man [was] killed by the king.*

### Exercises

Translate the following from Pali to English. Each has a word with the ③
ending: find it and say which row of the table it belongs to. Look up the
words in a dictionary, perform the necessary sandhi and compound word
deconstruction, and take note of the `vibhatti` endings to deduce the meaning
of the sentence. All but the second are sentences from the texts of chapters
[3](../3-awakening/) and [5](../10-pbt/).

You may find the [Digital Pali Dictionary](https://digitalpalidictionary.github.io/)
useful for looking up words, as well as doing the sandhi and compound word
deconstruction.
:::

1. Idhāvuso, bhikkhu mettāsahagatena cetasā ekaṁ disaṁ pharitvā viharati.
2. mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ
3. Vuttaṁ kho panetaṁ bhagavatā kakacūpamovāde.
4. Sabbo rāgo pahīno me.
5. Viññāṇassa nirodhena, natthi dukkhassa sambhavo.
6. Tena samayena buddho bhagavā uruvelāyaṁ viharati najjā nerañjarāya tīre bodhirukkhamūle paṭhamābhisambuddho.

### Answers

*Note: please do the exercises first before checking the answers.*

1. > Idhāvuso, bhikkhu **mettāsahagatena cetasā** ekaṁ disaṁ pharitvā viharati.

🔼(idhāvuso) 🚹①⨀(bhikkhu) 🚹③⨀(mettāsahagata) 🚹③⨀(cetas) 🚺②⨀(eka) 🚺②⨀(disā) 🔼(pharitvā) ▶️🤟⨀(viharati)  
here friend / bhikkhu / filled with goodwill/friendliness / with intent / one / direction / having filled / stays aware  
*Here, friend, a bhikkhu, with intent, stays aware having filled an entire compass direction with goodwill/friendliness.*

The instrument {286}: the mind, `cetasā`, is what the direction is filled with.

2. > **mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena** saddhiṁ

🚹⨀③(mahatā) 🚹⨀③🆎(bhikkhu+saṅgha) ⏏️(saddhiṁ)  
with a great / community of monks / together  
*Together with a great community of monks.*

Accompanied by {287}: `saddhiṁ` ("together with") goes with a ③ noun.

3. > Vuttaṁ kho panetaṁ **bhagavatā** kakacūpamovāde.

🚻①⨀(vutta) 🔼(kho) 🔼(panetaṁ) 🚹③⨀(bhagavant) 🚹⑦⨀(kakacūpamovāda)  
said / indeed / but this / by Bhagavant / exhortation with the simile of the saw  
*But this is indeed said by the Bhagavant in the exhortation with the simile of the saw.*

The doer of a passive verb {288}: `vuttaṁ` ("said") is passive; the one who said it stands in ③.

4. > Sabbo rāgo pahīno **me**.

🚹①⨀(sabba) 🚹①⨀(rāga) 🚹①⨀(pahīna) 👆③⨀(ahaṁ)  
all / passion / abandoned / by me  
*All passions abandoned by me.*

The doer of a passive verb {288}: `pahīno` ("abandoned") is passive; `me` is the ③ form of `ahaṁ` ("I").

5. > Viññāṇassa **nirodhena**, natthi dukkhassa sambhavo.

🚻⑥⨀(viññāṇa) 🚹③⨀(nirodha) ▶️🤟⨀(natthi) 🚹⑥⨀(dukkha) 🚹①⨀(sambhava)  
of consciousness / with the cessation / there is not / of suffering / birth  
*With the cessation of consciousness, there is no birth of suffering.*

The cause {289}: the cessation is the reason there is no birth of suffering.

6. > **Tena samayena** buddho bhagavā uruvelāyaṁ viharati najjā nerañjarāya tīre bodhirukkhamūle paṭhamābhisambuddho.

*️⃣🤟🚹⨀③(ta) 🚹⨀③(samaya) 🚹⨀①(buddha) 🚹⨀①(bhagavant) 🚺⨀⑦(uruvelā) 🟢⨀🤟▶️(viharati) 🚺⨀⑥(nadī) 🚺⨀⑥(nerañjarā) 🚻⨀⑦(tīra) 🚻⨀⑦🆎(bodhirukkha+mūla) 🚹⨀①🆎(paṭhama+abhisambuddha)  
at that / time / the Buddha / the Bhagavā / at Uruvelā / dwells / of the river / Nerañjarā / on the bank / at the root of the bodhi tree / newly fully awakened  
*At that time the Buddha, the Bhagavā, was dwelling at Uruvelā on the bank of the Nerañjarā river at the root of the Bodhi tree, having just attained full understanding (`abhisambuddha`).*

The time, in the sense of ⑦ {290}: `tena samayena` ("at that time") opens the text.
