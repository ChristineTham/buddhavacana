The usage examples provided here are not exhaustive, other usages are possible.

A derived word (`taddhitanāma`, "secondary derivative") is a noun made from
another noun by an affix (`paccaya`) added in a stated sense: the `vibhatti`
ending of the base and the word that states the sense (`samūho`, `ṭhānaṁ`)
are elided {317}, and the new word, which counts as a noun {601}, takes a
`vibhatti` ending of its own. This section covers six rules, each naming a
sense and the affix that carries it: a collection (`samūha`), with `kaṇ` and
`ṇa` {354} and, after `gāma`, `jana`, `bandhu`, `sahāya` and the like, with
`tā` {355}; "that is the occasion of it" (`tadassa ṭhāna`), with `iya` {356};
a likeness, the sense of comparison (`upamā`), with `āyitatta` {357}; a
dependence, "resting on that" (`tannissita`), with `la` {358}; and an
abundance, "abounding in that" (`tabbahula`), with `ālu` {359}. Appendix
[H](../h-taddhita/) tables every `taddhita` rule.

### 6.3.1 `kaṇ`, `ṇa` in the sense of a collection {354}

* `kaṇ` and `ṇa` are added to a noun in the ⑥ ending, plural, in the sense of
  a collection (`samūha`) of what it names: a collection of princes,
  `rājaputtānaṁ samūho`, is `rājaputtako` with `kaṇ` and `rājaputto` with
  `ṇa`. The `ṇ` of either affix is elided {396} and marks it as one that
  strengthens the first vowel of the base {400}: `manussa` ("human being")
  gives `mānussako`, `mānusso`.
* The vutti gives both words for each of its four bases, `rājaputtako` and
  `rājaputto`, `mānussako` and `mānusso`, `māyūrako` and `māyūro` ("a flock of
  peacocks"), `māhiṁsako` and `māhiṁso` ("a herd of buffaloes"); all are
  masculine.

#### Example

> rājaputtānaṁ samūho → **rājaputtako**

🚹⨂⑥🆎(rāja+putta) 🚹⨀①(samūho) → 🚹⨀①🔽(kaṇ)(rājaputtako)  
of princes / a collection → a body of princes  
*`rājaputtānaṁ samūho` ("a collection of princes") becomes `rājaputtako` with the affix `kaṇ` {354} in the sense of a collection: the ending of the base and the word `samūho` are elided {317}, and the `ṇ` of the affix {396}; with `ṇa` the same phrase gives `rājaputto`.*

### 6.3.2 `tā` after `gāma`, `jana`, `bandhu`, `sahāya` and the like {355}

* After `gāma` ("village"), `jana` ("person"), `bandhu` ("relative"), `sahāya`
  ("companion") and the like, the affix `tā` is added in the sense of a
  collection (`samūha`), an exception to {354}: a collection of villages,
  `gāmānaṁ samūho`, is `gāmatā`.
* A word in `tā` is feminine, and its ① singular `si` is elided {220}; the
  vutti's words are `gāmatā`, `janatā`, `bandhutā`, `sahāyatā` and `nagaratā`
  ("a group of towns").

#### Example

> gāmānaṁ samūho → **gāmatā**

🚹⨂⑥(gāmānaṁ) 🚹⨀①(samūho) → 🚺⨀①🔽(tā)(gāmatā)  
of villages / a collection → a group of villages  
*`gāmānaṁ samūho` ("a collection of villages") becomes `gāmatā` with the affix `tā` {355} in the sense of a collection: the ending of the base and the word `samūho` are elided {317}, the derived word is feminine, and its `si` is elided {220}.*

### 6.3.3 `iya` in the sense "that is the occasion of it" {356}

* `iya` is added to a noun in the ⑥ ending in the sense "that is the occasion
  of it" (`tadassa ṭhāna`): what is an occasion of intoxication, `madanassa
  ṭhānaṁ`, is `madaniyaṁ` ("intoxicating"). The final vowel of the base is
  elided before the affix {83}; the vutti's words are neuter, the `si`
  becoming `aṁ` {219}.
* The vutti's eight words are `madaniyaṁ`, `bandhaniyaṁ`, `mucchaniyaṁ`,
  `rajaniyaṁ`, `kamaniyaṁ` ("desirable, lovely"), `gamaniyaṁ` ("leading to"),
  `dussaniyaṁ` ("hateful") and `dassaniyaṁ`. The dictionaries and the Tipiṭaka
  spell them with a long vowel, `madanīyaṁ`.

#### Example

> madanassa ṭhānaṁ → **madaniyaṁ**

🚻⨀⑥(madanassa) 🚻⨀①(ṭhānaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🔽(iya)(madaniyaṁ)  
of intoxication / the occasion → intoxicating  
*`madanassa ṭhānaṁ` ("the occasion of intoxication") becomes `madaniyaṁ` with the affix `iya` {356} in the sense "that is the occasion of it", intoxicating: the ending of the base and the word `ṭhānaṁ` are elided {317}, the final `a` of `madana` is elided before the affix {83}, and the neuter `si` becomes `aṁ` {219}.*

### 6.3.4 `āyitatta` in the sense of comparison {357}

* `āyitatta` is added to the word a thing is likened to, in the sense of
  comparison (`upamā`): a forest that appears like smoke, `dhūmo viya dissati
  aduṁ vanaṁ`, shows `dhūmāyitattaṁ`, a smoke-like state, and one that
  appears like darkness `timirāyitattaṁ`. The final vowel of the base is
  elided before the affix {83}; the word is neuter, its `si` becoming `aṁ`
  {219}.

#### Example

> dhūmo viya dissati aduṁ vanaṁ → **dhūmāyitattaṁ**

🚹⨀①(dhūmo) ⏏️(viya) 🔴⨀🤟▶️(dissati) *️⃣🤟🚻⨀①(aduṁ) 🚻⨀①(vanaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🔽(āyitatta)(dhūmāyitattaṁ)  
smoke / like / appears / that / forest → smokiness, a smoke-like state  
*`dhūmo viya dissati aduṁ vanaṁ` ("that forest appears like smoke") gives `dhūmāyitattaṁ` with the affix `āyitatta` {357} in the sense of comparison, the state of looking like smoke, which the vutti names with `tadidaṁ` ("this is") `dhūmāyitattaṁ`; the final `a` of `dhūma` is elided before the affix {83} and the neuter `si` becomes `aṁ` {219}.*

### 6.3.5 `la` in the sense "resting on that" {358}

* `la` is added in the sense "resting on that" (`tannissita`): what rests on
  what is bad, `duṭṭhu nissitaṁ`, is `duṭṭhullaṁ` ("gross, lewd"), and what
  rests on knowledge, `vedaṁ nissitaṁ`, is `vedallaṁ` (a `vedalla`, a
  discourse of questions and answers). The `l` of the affix is doubled {404};
  the words are neuter, the `si` becoming `aṁ` {219}.
* The vutti adds, with `ca`, the sense of {356}, "that is the occasion of it":
  `duṭṭhu ṭhānaṁ duṭṭhullaṁ`, `vedassa ṭhānaṁ vedallaṁ`.

#### Example

> duṭṭhu nissitaṁ → **duṭṭhullaṁ**

⏏️(duṭṭhu) 🚻⨀①🔽(ta)(nissitaṁ) → 🚻⨀①🔽(la)(duṭṭhullaṁ)  
on what is bad / resting → gross, lewd  
*`duṭṭhu nissitaṁ` ("resting on what is bad") becomes `duṭṭhullaṁ` with the affix `la` {358} in the sense "resting on that", gross, lewd: the `l` of the affix is doubled {404} and the neuter `si` becomes `aṁ` {219}.*

### 6.3.6 `ālu` in the sense "abounding in that" {359}

* `ālu` is added in the sense "abounding in that" (`tabbahula`), of one whose
  nature the thing is or in whom it abounds: `abhijjhā assa pakati`
  ("covetousness is his nature") or `abhijjhā assa bahulā` ("covetousness
  abounds in him") gives `abhijjhālu` ("covetous"). The final vowel of the
  base is elided before the affix {83}, and the `si` of the word in `u` is
  elided {220}.
* The vutti's words are `abhijjhālu`, `sītālu` ("sensitive to cold"), `dhajālu`
  ("full of banners") and `dayālu` ("full of compassion").

#### Example

> abhijjhā assa pakati → **abhijjhālu**

🚺⨀①(abhijjhā) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(assa) 🚺⨀①(pakati) → 🚹⨀①🔽(ālu)(abhijjhālu)  
covetousness / his / nature → covetous  
*`abhijjhā assa pakati` ("covetousness is his nature"), or `abhijjhā assa bahulā` ("covetousness abounds in him"), becomes `abhijjhālu` with the affix `ālu` {359} in the sense "abounding in that", covetous: the final `ā` of `abhijjhā` is elided before the affix {83} and the `si` is elided {220}.*
