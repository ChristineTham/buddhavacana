When words join into a compound their endings are dropped {317} and the whole
takes one ending; the last member usually keeps its shape. Four rules let it
change at the `samāsanta` ("the end of a compound"), whatever the kind of
compound. The first is the one you will meet everywhere: a word with endings of
its own, such as `rāja` ("king": `rājā`, `rañño` {135}), closes a compound in
plain `a` with the endings of `purisa`, so `devānaṁ rājā` ("the king of the
gods") becomes `devarājo`. The rule's own examples include `sattāhaṁ` ("for
seven days"), met in the Bodhikathā. When a compound ends in an unexpected
shape, ask whether it is one of these four changes.

### The four changes

| what you see | what it means | example | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | :-: |
| a last member in plain `a`, whether its word has endings of its own (`rāja`) or another final (`akkhi` "eye", `go` "cow"); sometimes `ā` or `i` | it takes the endings of `purisa`; the change is optional: `devarājā` stands beside `devarājo` | `devarājo` ("the king of the gods") | {337} |
| `-ka` after a feminine word in `ī` or `ū` (Kaccāyana's `nadī`), the `ī` now short | the affix `ka` closes the compound, here a `bahubbīhi`, "having many …" | `bahunadiko` ("with many rivers") | {338} |
| `tudaṁ-` or `jāni-` before `-pati` ("husband") | both stand for `jāyā` ("wife"): "wife and husband" | `tudaṁpatī` ("wife and husband") | {339} |
| `-dhanvā` | `dhanu` ("bow") with the affix `ā`: "whose bow is …" | `gāṇḍīvadhanvā` ("he whose bow is Gāṇḍīva") | {340} |

The first change also shapes `pañcāhaṁ` ("five days"),
`visālakkho` ("large-eyed", from `akkhi`) and, with the final `i`, `sugandhi`
("sweet-smelling"); its vutti also adds `-ka` after `kattu` ("doer"):
`bahukattuko` ("with many workers"). The other three are rare. Every rule
number links to the full rule in the Kaccāyana translation; [the chapter
overview](../6-0-samasa/) tables them all.

### Examples

> devānaṁ rājā → **devarājo**

🚹⨂⑥(devānaṁ) 🚹⨀①(rājā) → 🚹⨀①🆎(deva+rāja)  
of the gods / king → the king of the gods  
*{337}: `rājā` on its own, `-rājo` at the end of a compound, with the ending of `puriso`.*

> bahū nadiyo yasmiṁ so → **bahunadiko**

🚺⨂①(bahū nadiyo) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑦(yasmiṁ) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(bahu+nadī+ka)  
many / rivers / in which / that → with many rivers  
*{338}: `nadī` ("river"), feminine in `ī`, takes `-ka` at the end, its `ī` short.*

> jāyā ca pati ca → **tudaṁpatī**

🚺⨀①(jāyā) ⏏️(ca) 🚹⨀①(pati) ⏏️(ca) → 🚹⨂①🆎(jāyā+pati)  
wife / and / husband / and → wife and husband  
*{339}: `tudaṁ-` before `-pati` stands for `jāyā` ("wife"); `jānipatī` says the same.*

> gāṇḍīvo dhanu yassa so → **gāṇḍīvadhanvā**

🚹⨀①(gāṇḍīvo) 🚻⨀①(dhanu) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀⑥(yassa) *️⃣🤟🚹⨀①(so) → 🚹⨀①🆎(gāṇḍīva+dhanu+ā)  
Gāṇḍīva / bow / whose / he → he whose bow is Gāṇḍīva  
*{340}: `dhanu` ("bow") with the affix `ā` ends the compound as `-dhanvā`; it names Arjuna.*
